## Safety Flags
- [CRITICAL/llm] Warfarin + Aspirin + Clopidogrel - critical bleeding risk
  Triple antithrombotic therapy (warfarin + aspirin + clopidogrel) in an elderly patient (67 years old) significantly increases the risk of major bleeding, especially post-PCI, with a documented major-bleed rate >10%/year.
- [CRITICAL/llm] Warfarin + Amiodarone + Fluconazole - critical bleeding risk
  Concurrent use of warfarin with both amiodarone and fluconazole, both strong CYP2C9 inhibitors, significantly increases warfarin's anticoagulant effect and the risk of major bleeding, especially with the addition of antiplatelets. INR can climb to >5 in days.
- [CRITICAL/llm] Amiodarone + Fluconazole - critical QT prolongation risk
  The combination of amiodarone and fluconazole, both potent QT prolongers, poses a critical risk of Torsades de Pointes, especially in a patient with structural heart disease (Atrial Fibrillation, NSTEMI).
- [MAJOR/llm] Statin + Amiodarone/Fluconazole - drug interaction caution
  Initiating statin therapy requires careful agent selection due to potential interactions with amiodarone and fluconazole, which can increase statin levels and risk of myopathy/rhabdomyolysis, particularly with CYP3A4-metabolized statins like simvastatin or lovastatin.
- [MODERATE/graph] Warfarin - interacts with caution
  Warfarin interacts with Aspirin. Evidence: Lifelong Warfarin (target INR 3.0; range 2.5-3.5) plus Aspirin 75-150mg daily
