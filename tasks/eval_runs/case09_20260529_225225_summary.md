## Safety Flags
- [CRITICAL/llm] Amiodarone + Fluconazole - QT prolongation risk
  The patient is currently on two QT-prolonging drugs (amiodarone and fluconazole) and has structural heart disease (AF, NSTEMI), significantly increasing the risk of Torsades de Pointes.
- [CRITICAL/llm] Warfarin + Aspirin + Clopidogrel - critical bleeding risk (triple therapy)
  Combining warfarin with dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin and clopidogrel) in an elderly patient (67 years) post-PCI significantly increases the risk of major bleeding.
- [CRITICAL/llm] Warfarin + Amiodarone + Fluconazole - critical bleeding risk (CYP2C9 inhibition)
  Co-administration of warfarin with two strong CYP2C9 inhibitors (amiodarone and fluconazole) significantly increases warfarin's anticoagulant effect, posing a critical risk of supratherapeutic INR and major hemorrhage, especially with the current INR at 2.4.
- [MAJOR/llm] Statin + Amiodarone + Fluconazole - myopathy risk
  Amiodarone and fluconazole are inhibitors of CYP3A4 and/or CYP2C9, which can significantly increase the plasma concentrations of many statins (e.g., simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin, rosuvastatin), raising the risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis.
- [MAJOR/llm] Clopidogrel + Amiodarone + Fluconazole - reduced effectiveness
  Amiodarone and fluconazole are moderate inhibitors of CYP2C19, which is required for clopidogrel activation, potentially reducing its antiplatelet effect and increasing the risk of stent thrombosis.
- [MODERATE/graph] Warfarin - interacts with caution
  Warfarin interacts with Aspirin. Evidence: Lifelong Warfarin (target INR 3.0; range 2.5-3.5) plus Aspirin 75-150mg daily
