## Safety Flags
- [MAJOR/llm] ACE-I + Spironolactone - hyperkalemia interaction caution
  Combining an ACE inhibitor with spironolactone in a patient with eGFR 58 mL/min/1.73m² significantly increases the risk of severe hyperkalemia, requiring close monitoring of serum potassium and renal function.
- [MAJOR/llm] Dapagliflozin + RAS blockers + Furosemide - acute kidney injury and hypotension risk
  The combination of dapagliflozin with an ACE inhibitor, spironolactone, and furosemide significantly increases the risk of acute kidney injury, hypotension, and volume depletion, especially in a patient with heart failure and eGFR 58 mL/min/1.73m².
- [MODERATE/llm] Metformin - increased lactic acidosis risk with new HF medications
  The addition of ACE-I, spironolactone, dapagliflozin, and furosemide increases the risk of acute kidney injury, which can lead to metformin accumulation and an elevated risk of lactic acidosis.
- [MODERATE/llm] Gliclazide MR - less preferred in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction
  Sulfonylureas like gliclazide are generally less preferred for glycemic control in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction due to potential for weight gain and hypoglycemia, which can negatively impact cardiovascular outcomes.
- [MODERATE/llm] Gliclazide MR + Dapagliflozin + Bisoprolol - increased hypoglycemia risk
  Combining gliclazide with dapagliflozin and bisoprolol increases the risk of hypoglycemia, with bisoprolol potentially masking hypoglycemic symptoms.
- [MODERATE/llm] GLP-1 RA + Gliclazide MR - increased hypoglycemia risk
  Adding a GLP-1 receptor agonist to a sulfonylurea like gliclazide significantly increases the risk of hypoglycemia, necessitating careful blood glucose monitoring.
