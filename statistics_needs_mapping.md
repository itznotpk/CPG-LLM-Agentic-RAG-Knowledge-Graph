# Statistics to Solution Mapping

Key pain point: Doctors in rural/mobile settings have limited time, limited records, and limited connectivity.


Main project focus:

> Doctors cannot memorise every Clinical Practice Guideline (CPG). Our AI assistant retrieves and answers according to CPGs at the point of care.

## 1. CPG Knowledge Burden

**Statistic**

- Clinical guidelines can be **100+ pages** long.
- Doctors may only have around **10.5 to 14.3 minutes** per primary care consultation.

**Sources**

- Guideline length: JAMA notes that guideline documents are often hundreds of pages long, making them difficult and time-consuming to read and digest. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/1906589
- Consultation time: Malaysian primary care studies reported mean consultation times of **10.54 minutes** and **14.3 minutes**. https://journal.unisza.edu.my/myjas/index.php/myjas/article/view/268 and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31866195/

**Problem**

Doctors need to make decisions quickly, but CPG knowledge is spread across long documents that are hard to search during consultation.

**What Our Solution Delivers**

- CPG-based question answering
- Fast retrieval from long guideline PDFs
- Answers grounded in official CPG content
- Summaries of relevant recommendations

**Why It Matters**

We reduce the need for doctors to manually memorise or search long CPGs during short consultations.

**Poster Message**

> Doctors have minutes; CPGs have pages.

## 2. Sabah Doctor Access Gap

**Statistic**

- Worst case in Sabah: **1 doctor for 14,675 people in Kalabakan**
- Sabah average: around **1 doctor for 795 people**
- In Malaysian public primary care, only **53.5% of rural clinics** were staffed with doctors, compared with **93.8% of urban clinics**

**Sources**

- Sabah doctor ratios: CodeBlue reported Sabah's statewide doctor-to-population ratio as **1:795**, with disparity from **1:268 in Kota Kinabalu** to **1:14,675 in Kalabakan**. https://codeblue.galencentre.org/2025/12/moh-projects-sabah-needs-over-4600-extra-doctors/
- Rural clinic doctor staffing: PLOS One national facility survey reported **53.5%** of rural public clinics were staffed with medical doctors versus **93.8%** of urban public clinics. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5308860/

**Problem**

When doctors are unevenly distributed, rural patients may have fewer chances to consult a doctor directly. Some rural clinics may operate without a doctor on-site and rely on other healthcare workers, referrals, rotating doctors, or mobile clinic visits.

**What Our Solution Delivers**

- Faster access to CPG recommendations during consultation
- Offline support for rural/mobile clinic settings
- More consistent guidance even when doctors rotate between locations
- Structured notes for continuity

**Why It Matters**

We help doctors deliver more consistent CPG-based care when consultation opportunities are limited, doctors rotate between sites, or rural care depends on mobile/visiting services.

**Poster Message**

> When doctor access is limited, every consultation must count.

## 3. Evidence-Based Practice and CPG Access Gap

**Statistic**

- **76% of Malaysian doctors** reported poor facilities to access evidence as a barrier to evidence-based practice.

**Source**

- Lai, Teng, and Lee found **76% of doctors** considered poor facilities to access evidence a barrier to evidence-based practice in Malaysia. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2989979/

**Problem**

Doctors are expected to practise according to evidence and CPGs, but they may not have enough time, access, or support to search and apply guideline evidence during consultation.

**What "Poor Facilities to Access Evidence" Means**

This does not only mean "no internet." Even with internet, evidence may still be difficult to use during consultation because:

- search results may not be official, local, or up to date
- CPGs are scattered across websites, portals, and long PDFs
- some evidence requires institutional access or login
- online PDFs are not consultation-ready and still require manual searching
- doctors need Malaysian/local CPG recommendations, not random internet results

**Rural Context**

In rural or mobile clinic settings, this access gap becomes more serious because doctors may face:

- unstable connectivity
- limited devices or clinic IT support
- fewer nearby specialists or senior doctors to consult
- broader case coverage with less immediate backup
- limited consultation opportunities with the same patient

**What Our Solution Delivers**

- CPG-based answers at the point of care
- Fast retrieval from official guideline PDFs
- Summaries of relevant CPG recommendations
- Offline access to guideline knowledge in rural/mobile settings

**Why It Matters**

We reduce the practical barriers that stop doctors from using CPG evidence during real consultations.

**Poster Message**

> The barrier is not the absence of guidelines. It is accessing the right guideline fast enough during care.

**Why Medication Harm Is Not Used as the Main Statistic**

Medication harm is important, but it is broader and less localised to our main project claim. If we use medication harm as a main statistic, the project may look like a medication safety system only.

Our main problem is CPG access: doctors have limited time to search, understand, and apply long guidelines during consultation. Medication safety can still be mentioned as one example where CPG-based guidance helps, but it should support the CPG access problem rather than replace it.

## Overall Positioning

Our solution does not replace doctor judgement.

It helps doctors by delivering:

- **CPG retrieval:** find the right guideline content quickly
- **CPG-grounded answers:** reduce unsupported or memory-based decisions
- **Rural readiness:** offline access for mobile and low-connectivity care
- **Continuity:** structured records and notes for follow-up

**Main Poster Claim**

> An AI assistant that helps doctors apply CPGs faster and more consistently during consultation.
