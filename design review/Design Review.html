MHNexus · Design Review · 14 May 2026

The bones are right. The consultation flow is where to invest.

MHNexus reads as a serious clinical product — Geist + slate + teal land the "clinical luxury" brief, the design tokens are well-defined, and the Home dashboard already does its job. The next wave of polish is concentrated in the consultation flow, the sidebar, and a handful of system-level rules: tightening glass layers, calming the badge stack on Diagnosis, and choosing a single visual language for charts.

Verdict at a glance

Four quick read-outs against the goals in DESIGN.md: clinical luxury, trust, density, and calm.

What's working

  • Tokens are honest and complete. Slate ramp + teal accent + tinted shadows + Geist/Geist Mono read exactly like the "clinical luxury" target. Numeric/`ds-numeric` is correctly tabular.
  • Today's Pulse strip is the best idea in the app. Three coloured cues (critical / overdue / next-up) with one CTA is the right pattern for a doctor who has 4 seconds before the next patient.
  • Pipeline visibility. Surfacing AI reasoning + CPGs consulted + citation count is rare and trust-building.
  • Realistic data. Mei Ling, Wong Kin Meng, Siti Nurhaliza, MMC license format, Hospital Kuala Lumpur — feels regionally true.

The five things I'd fix first

Ordered by user impact, not by effort. Home is intentionally out of scope — these all target the consultation flow, the sidebar, and the system-wide primitives that ripple into both.

01
High

Glass-on-glass-on-glass.

GlassPanel wraps GlassCard wraps a coloured-tint card. By step 3 of consult, you're four translucent layers deep with three sets of borders. Pick one: frosted panels are the page background, real cards are opaque white with a 1px line. The current stack also hurts text contrast on the schedule patient-name rows.

Status pills, risk badges, ICD badges, AI-recommended badges — all on one row.

A differential card can carry 4 badges plus a numbered circle plus a checkmark. Collapse to: rank number · diagnosis · ICD (mono, no pill) · one risk dot · one trailing "AI top pick" label. Badges should be the exception, not the texture.

Medium

The sidebar uses two visual systems for "active".

In light mode, active = gradient fill + bordered + shadow + right-side accent bar. In dark mode, active = soft tint + border, no bar. Pick one — I'd suggest the dark-mode treatment everywhere (tinted bg, 1px accent border, accent text, no bar, no shadow). Quieter, more clinical.

Polish

Vitals colours are emoji-coded.

Red systolic + orange diastolic + violet HR + blue SpO₂ + green weight + amber temp = six unrelated hues on one chart. Use a single accent series with line-style or weight to differentiate (e.g. teal solid + slate dashed for BP pair), reserving red strictly for out-of-range values. This is the kind of detail that signals "real medical software" vs "demo".

A novel feature — measuring vitals from the camera feed. It deserves a more confident interface; right now the empty-state dwarfs the action.

The background is the wrong colour.

Body uses #f0f4ff (cool lavender) — every other surface in the product sits on slate-50 / slate-100. This screen reads as if it belongs to a different app. Drop to var(--bg-primary) so the scanner feels like it lives inside MHNexus, not next to it.

03

System-level fixes

Cheap to land, ripple everywhere. Worth doing before another screen ships.

01 · COLOUR

Teal is the only category colour.

Reserve violet/sky/amber for state (in-progress, info, warning). Risk uses the documented risk-low/moderate/high/critical scale only. Aim for 2 hues on a normal screen, 3 max when a banner is active.

02 · ELEVATION

Pick one glass layer.

Frosted = the page chrome. Cards = opaque white + 1px slate-200 line + the existing --shadow-sm. Drop backdrop-blur from cards — it bleeds the patient-name rows underneath and costs real GPU on long lists.

03 · TYPOGRAPHY

One eyebrow per section, max.

If a section already has a heading + a divider + a card border, the eyebrow is the fourth signal of "this is a section". Keep eyebrows for cross-cutting strips (Today's Pulse) and kill them from individual metric tiles.

04 · DENSITY

p-6 is a default, not a rule.

GlassCard.p-6 is everywhere. List rows should be p-3 / p-4. The schedule list currently spends ~33% of its height on padding and borders; tighten to a 56–64px row.

05 · BADGES

Build a "two-signal-max" rule.

Each row may carry one status pill and one ID chip (ICD, NSN). Risk is a 6px dot in the metadata line. AI-recommended is text, not a pill. This alone reclaims 30–40% of visual weight on Diagnosis and MyPatients.

06 · MOTION

Spring on confirm, not on hover.

Spring physics on hover-lift makes the whole UI feel jelly-ish on long lists. Keep springs for: state changes (step transitions), positive confirmation (toast), and the AI "thinking → answered" reveal. Hover = linear 120ms.

07 · DARK MODE

Audit dark mode for contrast, not just colour-flips.

"text-slate-400 on bg-slate-800" appears in many places and sits below WCAG AA. Cards inside cards inside dark mode lose all border definition (white/5 on white/5 is invisible). Use slate-300 for body text in dark mode and white/10 minimum for inner borders.

08 · DATA VIZ

Charts speak the brand.

Vitals lines: teal primary, slate secondary (dashed), rose only for out-of-range. Reference ranges as a soft slate band, not gridlines. Match the typographic discipline of the rest of the UI — no per-metric colour theme.

09 · COPY

Talk like a clinician, not a product.

"AI Risk Assessment & Diagnosis" → "Diagnosis". "Assistant impact today" → "Today's AI assist". "Today's Pulse" → keep, it's good. Strong verbs over labels: "Confirm & continue" beats "Approve plan".
