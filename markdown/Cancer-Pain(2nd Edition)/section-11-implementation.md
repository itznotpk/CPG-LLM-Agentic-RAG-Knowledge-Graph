## 11.1 Facilitating and Limiting Factors

| Category | Factor |
|---|---|
| **Facilitating** | Availability of the CPG to healthcare providers (hardcopies and softcopies/online) |
| **Facilitating** | Regular seminars/conferences/courses for healthcare providers on management of cancer pain including those involving professional bodies |
| **Facilitating** | Presence of 'Pain as 5th Vital Sign' strategy and pain-free hospital concept involving multidisciplinary team |
| **Facilitating** | Public awareness activities involving governmental/non-governmental organisations (e.g. World Hospice and Palliative Care Day) |
| **Limiting** | Limited awareness and understanding/knowledge in managing cancer pain among healthcare providers, especially on the use of opioids |
| **Limiting** | Variation in clinical management and preferences |
| **Limiting** | Insufficient resources in terms of expertise and medications |
| **Limiting** | Misconception about the disease and its management by the public |

---

## 11.2 Potential Resource Implications

Appropriate assessment and treatment of cancer pain is crucial for successful management. This includes proper use of opioids for moderate to severe cancer pain. However, barriers to its use dampen the aims of controlling cancer pain at various levels of care.

**Key actions needed:**
*   Educate both healthcare providers and patients/carers on judicious use of opioids in cancer pain
*   Collaborate with NGOs (e.g. hospice organisations) to facilitate guideline implementation
*   Ensure IR morphine is available in **all primary care centres**

## Clinical Audit Indicator

In line with the key recommendations of the CPG and the National Key Performance Index of Palliative Medicine:

| Indicator | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Percentage of patients with cancer pain score 7-10 prescribed with strong opioids** | (Total patients with cancer pain score 7-10 prescribed strong opioids in a period) ÷ (Total patients with cancer pain score 7-10 in same period) × 100% |
