## Overview

Pain is a highly complex and subjective phenomenon. Its components are not only physiological, but also include behavioural, cognitive, emotional, spiritual and social aspects. The interpretation of pain and how the sufferer responds to it is unique and individualised.

**Assessment of pain is:**
*   A vital step in cancer pain management
*   The responsibility of all healthcare providers
*   Must be performed prior to treatment to plan appropriate interventions and assess effectiveness

**Pain assessment aims to determine the:**
*   Nature and pathophysiology of pain
*   Severity of pain
*   Impact of pain on functions and quality of life
*   Response to interventions

Like other clinical assessments, a complete pain assessment requires a detailed history, physical examination and relevant investigations.

---

## 3.1 Clinical Presentation

Cancer pain can be classified by various methods according to aetiology, pathophysiology, anatomical location, temporal pattern and severity. In the clinical context, cancer pain is often described using a combination of these classifications.

### Table 1. Classification of Cancer Pain Based on Pathophysiology

| Pathophysiology | Clinical Characteristics |
|---|---|
| **Nociceptive Pain** | Pain due to tissue damage associated with an identifiable somatic or visceral lesion. Subdivided into somatic and visceral types. |
| — *Somatic Pain* | Damage of somatic tissue (bones, soft tissue). Character: aching, stabbing or throbbing. Usually well localised. Often worse with movement. |
| — *Visceral Pain* | Damage to viscera (liver, intestines, pancreas, bladder, etc). Character: cramping/gnawing (obstruction) or aching/sharp/throbbing (organ capsule). Usually diffuse and difficult to localise. May be referred to somatic structures. |
| **Neuropathic Pain** | Pain due to abnormal somatosensory processing in peripheral or central nervous system. Character: burning, pricking, electric-like, shooting or stabbing; sometimes deep aching. Located in area innervated by compressed/damaged nerve. Often associated with loss of sensation. Allodynia or dysaesthesia may be present. |

*Source: Ministry of Health Malaysia. CPG Management of Cancer Pain. Putrajaya: MoH; 2010.*

> **Key Insight:** Not all pain is from the cancer itself — it could be due to pre-existing conditions (e.g. osteoarthritis, migraine, osteoporosis) which may be managed differently.

---

### Common Pain Syndromes

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Nociceptive (direct tumour)** | Base of skull metastasis, Vertebral syndrome, Diffuse or multifocal bone pain, Neoplastic involvement of viscera (e.g. liver capsular pain) |
| **Neuropathic (direct tumour)** | Peripheral nerve syndromes, Brachial and lumbosacral plexopathy, Leptomeningeal metastasis, Epidural spinal cord/nerve root/cauda equina compression |
| **Therapy-related** | Post-operative pain (e.g. post-thoracotomy), Post-radiation syndromes, Post-chemotherapy syndromes (e.g. peripheral neuropathy) |

## 3.2 Clinical Assessment

### 3.2.1 History Taking

Taking a good pain history is important for accurate clinical assessment as most pain diagnoses can be made based on history alone.

### Table 2. Points for History Taking

| Component | Key Points |
|---|---|
| **Characteristics of pain** | Site(s) - single/multiple, Quality - sharp/dull/throbbing/colicky, Intensity - pain score, Timing - persistent/episodic/on movement/spontaneous, Radiation, Aggravating and relieving factors, Associated symptoms (numbness, abnormal sensation, hyperalgesia, allodynia) |
| **Cancer history** | Site(s) - primary/metastatic, Treatment(s) - surgery/chemotherapy/radiotherapy/targeted therapy |
| **Medication** | Analgesics and adjuvants, Side effects, Concurrent medications (including traditional/alternative), Treatment response/adherence |
| **Co-morbidities** | Renal/liver disease, Cardiac/respiratory disease, Cognitive impairment, Other pain conditions (acute/chronic), Previous alcohol or drug abuse |
| **Psychosocial-spiritual** | Emotional/psychological status (depression, anxiety, stress), Meaning of pain to the patient, Effects on ADL/appetite/sleep, Effects on socio-economic functioning, Perception of pain and pain medications |

*Source: Ministry of Health Malaysia. CPG Management of Cancer Pain. Putrajaya: MoH; 2010.*

---

### 3.2.2 Physical Examination

After taking a full history, physical examination serves to confirm the clinical diagnosis and provide a comprehensive understanding of the patient's condition and extent of problems.

### 3.2.3 Investigations

Investigations may be necessary to support diagnosis and/or assist clinical decision-making.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Radiological** | Plain X-rays, bone scans, CT scans, MRI |
| **Blood** | Liver and renal function tests |

> **Clinical Note:** Investigations should be ordered only if the results could potentially influence clinical management.

## 3.3 Pain Assessment Tools

Effective pain management requires careful assessment and documentation of pain. Pain assessment tools incorporate unidimensional and multidimensional measures.

### Validated Unidimensional Tools

| Tool | Key Feature |
|---|---|
| **Visual Analogue Scale (VAS)** | Linear scale |
| **Numerical Rating Scale (NRS)** | 0-10 number scale |
| **Verbal Rating Scale (VRS)** | Categorical adjectives (no pain → very severe) |
| **Faces Pain Scale (FPS)** | Picture-based faces |

> **MoH Implementation:** In the implementation of 'Pain as the 5th Vital Sign', MoH has advocated the pain assessment tools in Appendix 4a (MoH Pain Scale) & 4c (VRS).

### Reliability Comparison (Correlational Study, Cancer Patients ≥65 Years)

| Metric | Best Performing Tool |
|---|---|
| Test-retest reliability for **current** pain | NRS-11 (highest) |
| Reliability for **least** pain | VDS (highest) |
| Reliability for **average** pain | FPS (highest) |
| Reliability for **worst** pain | Mixed scale (highest) |

> **Note:** All four scales were reliable and valid for assessing cancer pain.

### Highly Recommended Tools (Large SR, Psychometric + Clinical Utility)

| Rating | Tools |
|---|---|
| **Highly recommended** | McGill Pain Questionnaire (MPQ)-Short Form, NRS, VAS |
| **Recommended** | Brief Pain Inventory (BPI), BPI-Short Form, MPQ, Pain Disability Index |

> **Note:** No mention of quality of primary papers used in the review.

> **Cross-reference from Appendix 4:**
> **Summary of Available Assessment Tools**
> * **a. Ministry of Health (MoH) Pain Scale:** Combines NRS, VAS and faces scale (0-10).
> * **b. FLACC Scale:** Observational score for children >1 month to 3 years or adults unable to communicate. Scores Face, Legs, Activity, Cry, Consolability.
> * **c. Verbal Rating Scale (VRS):** Categorizes pain from 'no pain' (0) to 'very severe pain' (4).
> * **d. Short-form McGill Pain Questionnaire (SF-MPQ-2):** Evaluates 24 descriptors of pain on a 0-10 scale.
> * **e. Leeds Assessment of Neuropathic Symptoms and Signs (LANSS) Scale:** Differentiates neuropathic vs non-neuropathic pain (>12 score suggests neuropathic).
> * **f. PainDETECT Questionnaire:** Self-report questionnaire predicting likelihood of neuropathic component.
> * **g. Doeleur Neuropathique en 4 (DN4) Scale:** Diagnosis of neuropathic pain based on 7 symptoms and 3 clinical exam items (Score ≥ 4 suggests neuropathic pain).
> * **h & i. NCCN Distress Thermometer (English/Malay):** Single-item tool to measure psychological distress.
> * **j. Integrated Palliative Care Outcome Scale (IPOS):** Comprehensive assessment covering pain, shortness of breath, nausea, etc.
> * **k. Edmonton Symptom Assessment System (ESAS) Tool:** Profile of symptom severity over time.
> * **l. Pain Assessment in Advanced Dementia (PAINAD) Scale:** Similar parameters to FLACC, specifically for dementia patients.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 1**
> * Accurate and comprehensive assessment should be performed prior to treatment in all patients with cancer pain.
> * Appropriate pain assessment tools should be used regularly on patients with cancer pain and documented accordingly.
>   * The preferred unidimensional tools are Visual Analogue Scale, Numerical Rating Scale, Verbal Rating Scale and Faces Pain Scale.

### 3.3.1 Assessment Tools for Neuropathic Pain

Neuropathic pain is defined by IASP as *'pain caused by a lesion or disease of the somatosensory nervous system'*. It is a frequent consequence of cancer pain and poses considerable suffering.

#### Predictors and Symptoms (Cross-Sectional Study)

| Category | Findings |
|---|---|
| **Predictors** | Age <65 years, disease duration >6 months, stage IV cancer, history of chemotherapy, moderate-to-severe cancer pain |
| **Common symptoms** | Tingling, electric shock, 'pins and needles' |

#### Diagnostic Accuracy of Neuropathic Pain Tools

| Tool | AUC | Specificity | Cut-off | Reference Standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LANSS** | 0.96 | 100% | 12 points | Clinician assessment |
| **PainDETECT** | 0.870 (95% CI 0.813 to 0.926) | 100% | ≥19 | Pain specialist diagnosis |
| **DN4** | 0.857 (95% CI 0.799 to 0.914) | 88.7% | ≥4 | Pain specialist diagnosis |

> **Note:** The diagnosis of neuropathic pain needs to be confirmed with clinical assessment.

|---|---|

### 3.3.3 Pain Assessment Tools in Cognitive Impairment / Learning Disability

Pain assessment in patients with cognitive impairment is challenging as self-reported tools are inaccurate. Observational tools are recommended instead.

| Tool | Population | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| **PAINAD** (Pain Assessment in Advanced Dementia) | Patients with advanced dementia | Inter-rater: ICC 0.92 (rest), 0.98 (movement). Internal consistency: Cronbach's α 0.76 (rest), 0.80 (movement). |
| **FLACC** (Face Legs Activity Cry Consolability) | Cognitively impaired adults (per MoH guidelines) | — |

> **WHO Guidance:** PAINAD is one of the tools mentioned in WHO guidelines to assess pain in patients with advanced dementia.

*   Observational tools e.g. FLACC scale or PAINAD may be useful to assess pain in cognitively impaired adults.
