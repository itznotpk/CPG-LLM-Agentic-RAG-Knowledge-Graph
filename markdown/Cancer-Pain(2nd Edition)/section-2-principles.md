## Guiding Principles

The principles of cancer pain management have remained similar over the years and their importance has been strengthened by research. A systematic review has reaffirmed the core principles outlined in the first edition of the Malaysian CPG on Management of Cancer Pain.

| # | Principle | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Comprehensive pain assessment** | Detailed history, physical examination, psychological assessment, suitable pain measurement tools and appropriate diagnostic procedures. Regular reassessment is vital. |
| 2 | **Concept of Total Pain** | A holistic review of physical, psychological, social and spiritual aspects to understand the multidimensionality of pain (see Figure 1). |
| 3 | **Multidisciplinary team** | A team of different expertise providing effective and comprehensive pain relief through various treatment methods. |
| 4 | **Patient and family-centred care** | Involving the patient and their family/caregiver to understand values and preferences when planning treatment. |
| 5 | **Individualisation** | Recognising that pain experience and response is unique to each individual. |

---

## Figure 1. Concept of Total Pain

The concept of **Total Pain** is a holistic framework that categorizes a patient's overall pain experience into four interconnected dimensions.

| Dimension | Contributing Factors |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | Caused by cancer, Caused by treatment, Co-morbidities |
| **Psychological** | Anxiety, Depression, Fear of suffering, Past experience of illness |
| **Social** | Loss of role & social status, Loss of job, Financial concerns, Worries about future of family, Dependency |
| **Spiritual** | Anger at fate / anger with God, Loss of faith, Finding meaning, Fear of the unknown |

*Source: Franklin AE, Lovell MR. Pain and Pain Management. In: Roderick Duncan MacLeod, Lieve Van den Block, editors. Textbook of Palliative Care. Cham: Springer International Publishing; 2018. p. 1-29.*

## Multidisciplinary Team

A multidisciplinary team is often required to address the many needs of a patient.

### Core Team Composition

| Role |
|---|
| Physician |
| Nurse |
| Pharmacist |
| Clinical Psychologist |
| Social Worker |
| Physiotherapist |
| Occupational Therapist |
| Spiritual Care Provider |

> **Note:** Other healthcare providers may be included based on the patient's needs.

### Evidence for MDT Approach

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| High-intensity interprofessional collaboration studies | Improvement in mean patient satisfaction, Less uncertainty and concerns among patients, Adequacy in pain management |
| Cohort study (MDT palliative care team) | Significantly reduced pain intensity and other symptoms in cancer patients |
| Study (clinical pharmacist-led team) | Improved standardisation of opioid administration, pain scores and QoL; reduced GI AEs vs usual care |

## Patient and Family-Centred Care

Good communication and collaboration with the patient and their family/caregiver during decision-making will ensure optimal care in the patient's best interest.

> **Evidence:** The involvement of patients and their family/caregiver in managing cancer pain reduces barriers to analgesic use and decreases the worst pain score.

---

## Individualisation

Healthcare providers should be aware that the patient's experience and response to pain is highly individualised.

**Influencing factors:**
*   Age
*   Cognitive abilities
*   Cultural background
*   Previous experience of pain

> **Clinical Note:** Recognising individual variability may help in providing individualised care and alleviating pain more effectively.
