## Overview

World Health Organization (WHO) reported that there were 48,639 new cancer cases in Malaysia in 2020. Cancer accounted for over 10% of all medically-certified deaths in the country in 2021. For those living with cancer, pain is a common and distressing symptom that affects their quality of life.

Pain is defined as *'an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with, or resembling that associated with, actual or potential tissue damage'*. Cancer pain or cancer-related pain is pain experienced by patients with cancer due to cancer itself or its treatment.

---

## Prevalence of Cancer Pain

A large meta-analysis showed the following prevalence rates:

| Setting | Prevalence |
|---|---|
| After curative treatment | 39.3% |
| During anticancer treatment | 55.0% |
| Advanced, metastatic or terminal disease | 66.4% |
| Moderate to severe pain (NRS ≥5) across all patients | 38.0% |

> **Key Insight:** Cancer pain remains prevalent despite treatment, and about a third of patients suffer from more than just mild pain. There is no local data on the prevalence of cancer pain. However, a local study found that recognition of cancer symptoms (including pain) was relatively low across Malaysia.

## Opioid Consumption in Malaysia

The consumption of strong opioid analgesics (morphine, oxycodone, fentanyl etc.), which are essential for managing moderate to severe cancer pain, is relatively low in Malaysia compared with other countries.

| Metric | Malaysia | Global Average |
|---|---|---|
| Total strong opioid consumption (excl. methadone), 2005 | 0.086 DDD/1000 inhabitants/day | — |
| Total strong opioid consumption (excl. methadone), 2014 | 0.126 DDD/1000 inhabitants/day | 32.8 DDD/1000 inhabitants/day |

> **Clinical Note:** Malaysia's opioid consumption remains significantly below the global average, suggesting undertreatment of moderate to severe cancer pain.

## Barriers to Effective Pain Control

The main barriers relate to physicians' and patients' attitudes towards the use of opioids.

### Physician Barriers

| Barrier | Percentage |
|---|---|
| Felt they lacked knowledge to manage severe pain | 46% |
| Concerned about opioid addiction | 40% |
| Worried about legal issues | 38% |

### Patient Barriers

| Barrier | Percentage |
|---|---|
| Believed opioids were only for terminal cases | 62% |
| Feared adverse effects (e.g. constipation, nausea) | 54% |
| Feared becoming addicted | 48% |

> **Clinical Note:** These misconceptions and fears may prevent patients from reporting their pain or requesting opioids, and physicians from prescribing adequate doses or using appropriate routes of administration.

## Purpose of This CPG

The first edition of CPG in cancer pain management in Malaysia has helped to spearhead improvement in this field. Since then, services that provide cancer pain management increased at hospital, primary care and community hospice levels. This updated CPG aims to expand the information and incorporate new and current evidence of pharmacological/non-pharmacological management in cancer pain.
