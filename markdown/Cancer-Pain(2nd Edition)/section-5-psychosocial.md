Psychoeducation, psychological and spiritual interventions are important in the management of cancer pain. Patients with cancer pain may perceive the pain as the most feared physical consequence — related to losing hope for cure or as a punishment for previous wrongdoings. It can also affect mood and cause anxiety and other psychological symptoms.

---

## 5.1 Psychoeducation Intervention

Educational interventions given by healthcare providers (e.g. provision of educational information, behavioural instructions and advice).

| Source | Finding | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| SR (4 RCTs) | Mixed results in improvement of pain intensity and interference | Jadad Score 2-4 |
| Meta-analysis (12 RCTs, pain education via interviews ± phone calls) | Small reduction in pain intensity vs control (SMD= -0.11, 95% CI -0.20 to -0.02) | Mixed (Jadad Score) |

---

## 5.2 Psychological Intervention

Types of psychological strategies available include imagery, relaxation and cognitive restructuring.

### Evidence Summary

| Source | Intervention | Finding | Quality/Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large meta-analysis | Psychosocial interventions (psychotherapy, hypnosis, desensitisation, meditation) | Moderate positive effects on pain severity (Hedge's g=0.34, 95% CI 0.23 to 0.46) and pain interference (Hedge's g=0.40, 95% CI 0.21 to 0.60) vs control | Heterogeneous primary papers |
| RCT (advanced cancer) | Brief cognitive behavioural strategies | Lower symptom cluster distress (pain, fatigue, sleep disturbance) only at 6 weeks (p=0.04) | — |
| RCT (alexithymia + cancer pain) | Psychoeducation, problem-solving, cognitive restructuring, stress management, progressive relaxation | Improved alexithymia leading to significantly bigger change in pain intensity score vs treatment as usual | — |

> **Clinical Note:** Although the evidence for psychoeducation and psychological interventions is of moderate level, their important role in the management of cancer pain should be acknowledged. These interventions usually need sufficient time before benefits are seen.

---

## 5.3 Spiritual Intervention

Spiritual intervention is a part of holistic care in alleviating cancer pain which complements physical, psychological and social strategies.

| Source | Finding | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| SR (11 studies) on Dignity Therapy, focused narrative intervention, mindfulness-based stress reduction | Paucity and heterogeneity of evidence on cancer pain. Some evidence suggested spiritual care may aid in coping with pain rather than altering pain intensity. | Mixed |

**Key points:**
*   Spiritual interventions were well received by patients
*   They do not appear to cause harm

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 15**
> * Psychoeducation, psychological and spiritual interventions should be considered in the management of cancer pain.
