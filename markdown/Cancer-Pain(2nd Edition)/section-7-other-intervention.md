Physical and complementary therapies have gained widespread recognition as valuable interventions for relieving cancer-related symptoms. These approaches offer a diverse range of options to address the unique needs of patients.

> **Caution:** A SR on complementary and alternative medicine (CAM) interventions on breast cancer-related pain suggested that CAM should be used cautiously along with other medical treatments.

---

## Evidence Summary Table

| Therapy | Source | Finding | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TENS** | Cochrane SR | Insufficient evidence on effectiveness vs control (sham/placebo). However, TENS was well tolerated. | — |
| **Exercise** | SR | Reduced cancer pain vs non-exercise/usual care (SMD= -0.45, 95% CI -0.62 to -0.28) | High risk or some concern of bias |
| **Massage** | SR (advanced cancer) | Mixed results on effectiveness in reducing cancer pain | Mixed |
| **Massage** | Meta-analysis | Reduced pain vs no-massage (SMD= -1.25, 95% CI -1.63 to -0.87) | Mixed |
| **Massage (subgroup)** | Same meta-analysis | Body massage, aroma massage and foot reflexology all effective in pain reduction | Mixed |
| **Massage ± Aromatherapy** | Cochrane SR | Significant medium- and long-term pain relief vs no massage | Mixed |
| **Aromatherapy massage vs control** | Meta-analysis | No difference in reduction of cancer pain | — |
| **Aromatherapy alone/combined (breast cancer)** | SR on CAM | Did not reduce breast cancer-related pain | — |
| **Acupuncture vs sham/analgesics** | Cochrane SR | Insufficient evidence overall | — |
| **Acupuncture (by cancer type)** | Same Cochrane SR | Effective: pancreatic, late-stage, chronic neuropathic cancer pain. Not effective: ovarian cancer, stomach carcinoma. | — |

## Detailed Notes by Therapy

### Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation (TENS)

Insufficient evidence to support or refute its use, but it is well tolerated and may be considered as an adjunct.

---

### Exercise

Exercise has potential to alleviate pain, improve physical function and enhance overall well-being. It is a promising non-pharmacological intervention.

---

### Massage and Aromatherapy

*   **Massage alone** has growing evidence for pain reduction, particularly body massage, aroma massage and foot reflexology
*   **Aromatherapy alone** does not appear to reduce cancer pain independently
*   Massage therapy was found to reduce breast cancer-related pain, but aromatherapy alone or in combination with massage did not

---

### Acupuncture

> **Clinical Conclusion (SR of 14 SRs):** Clinicians may consider acupuncture as an adjunctive therapy for cancer-related pain management, in particular when pain control is unsatisfactory using analgesics alone.
