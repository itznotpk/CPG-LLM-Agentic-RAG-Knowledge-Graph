## Overview

Surgical management of breast cancer depends on the stage and clinical presentation. This section covers the multidisciplinary team approach, surgical margins in breast-conserving surgery (BCS), sentinel lymph node management, surgery for locally advanced and metastatic disease, breast reconstruction, and loco-regional recurrence.

---

## 6.1 Multi-Disciplinary Team (MDT)

Multidisciplinary team (MDT) meetings provide an opportunity for multiple specialties to collaboratively integrate diagnoses and treatment decisions for breast cancer patients.

### Core MDT Members

| Discipline |
|---|
| Breast surgeon |
| Radiologist |
| Pathologist |
| Oncologist |
| Breast care nurse |

**Other relevant healthcare providers** (e.g. anaesthetist, plastic surgeon, radiotherapist, geneticist, gynaecologist, social worker) may be involved when required.

**MDT impact on survival:** HR=**0.82** (95% CI 0.74 to 0.91) — an **18% reduction** in mortality.³⁷˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵ⁻²

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 10**
> *   Multidisciplinary team approach should be considered in the management of breast cancer to improve clinical outcomes.

## 6.2 Surgery for Early Breast Cancer

### a. Adequate Tumour-Free Margin in Breast Conserving Surgery

#### i. Invasive Carcinoma

BCS with negative margin followed by adjuvant RT is an effective local treatment in treating early breast cancer.

| Finding | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Positive margins vs negative margins (IBTR) | **OR=2.44** (95% CI 1.97 to 3.03) — 2-fold increase in IBTR | 38, level III |
| Margins wider than "no ink on tumour" | **NOT** associated with lower IBTR | 38, level III |

> **Key Insight:** "No tumour at ink margin" on histopathological examination is adequate for BCS in invasive breast carcinoma. Wider margins do NOT further reduce recurrence risk.

#### ii. Ductal Carcinoma In Situ (DCIS)

There is variation in the definition of adequate margin for DCIS following BCS in the range of 0–2 mm.⁴˒²⁵˒⁴⁰˒⁴¹˒⁴²

| Study | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Retrospective cohort (no adjuvant RT) | Larger negative margins → significantly lower LR rate | 43, level II-2 |
| — Margins ≤2 mm | HR=0.75 | — |
| — Margins >2–10 mm | HR=0.58 | — |
| — Margins >10 mm | HR=0.31 | — |
| With <2 mm margin + RT | LR reduced: HR=0.29 (p<0.0001) | 43, level II-2 |
| <2 mm margin + RT vs ≥2 mm without RT | **No difference** in LRR: HR=0.77 (95% CI 0.19 to 3.23) | 44, level II-2 |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 11**
> *   In women treated with breast conserving surgery for DCIS of <2 mm margin, the benefits and risks of further treatment (surgery or radiotherapy) should be discussed to reduce the risk of local recurrence.

### b. Treatment After Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy

Axillary lymph nodes dissection (ALND) in early breast cancer with clinically node-negative patients has largely been abandoned due to upper limb lymphoedema, paraesthesia, seroma and motor nerve injury risks.

#### Key Trials

| Trial | Population | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACOSOG Z0011** | cT1-T2N0, BCS, ≤2 positive SLNs | SLND alone non-inferior to SLND + ALND | 45, level I |
| — OS | — | HR=0.79 (90% CI 0.56 to 1.10) | — |
| — DFS | — | HR=0.82 (95% CI 0.58 to 1.17) | — |
| — LR | — | 1.6% (SLND) vs 3.1% (ALND), p=0.11 | — |
| **AMAROS** | cT1-T2N0, BCS or mastectomy, positive SLNs | Axillary RT comparable to ALND | 46, level I |
| — OS | — | HR=1.17 (95% CI 0.85 to 1.63) | — |
| — DFS | — | HR=1.18 (95% CI 0.93 to 1.51) | — |
| **Meta-analysis** | Similar group (includes AMAROS) | Lymphoedema: 23.2% (ALND) vs 10.8% (RT) | 47, level I |

*Clinically lymph node negative refers to: no evidence of axillary lymph node involvement on physical examination and imaging studies, AND negative on cyto/histopathology in case of a suspicious axillary lymph node.*

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 12**
> *   In early breast cancer patients:
>     *   With clinically lymph nodes negative who have **BCS** and SLN biopsy — **no further axillary surgery** is needed in ≤2 positive SLNs, but axillary radiotherapy may be considered
>     *   With clinically lymph nodes negative who have **mastectomy** and SLN biopsy with ≤2 positive SLNs — axillary treatment (either RT or surgery) should be offered

## 6.3 Surgery for Locally Advanced Breast Cancer

Neoadjuvant systemic therapy is indicated for women with inoperable breast cancer. It can render inoperable cancer to resectable cancer.²⁶

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 13**
> *   Inoperable breast cancer should be referred for neoadjuvant systemic therapy prior to surgical intervention.

### BCS Following Neoadjuvant Systemic Therapy

A meta-analysis showed **no difference in LR and regional recurrence** between BCS and mastectomy in LABC with good response to NACT. However, DFS (OR=2.35, 95% CI 1.84 to 3.01) and OS (OR=2.12, 95% CI 1.51 to 2.98) were higher in BCS.⁴⁸˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵ⁻²

> **Clinical Note:** BCS following neoadjuvant systemic therapy can be considered in suitable patients if expertise and facilities are available.

## 6.4 Timing for Breast Reconstruction

Post-mastectomy radiation therapy (PMRT) has detrimental effect on aesthetic outcome and is associated with higher complication rates following breast reconstruction.

### Two-Stage Implant-Based Reconstruction (when PMRT anticipated)

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | Placement of tissue expander (TE), followed by expansion within 1–6 months |
| **Stage 2** | TE exchanged with permanent implant — either before or after radiation therapy |

### Comparative Outcomes (Systematic Review, n=1,565)

| Metric | TE Radiation | Implant Radiation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implant failure rate | No significant difference | No significant difference | 50, level II-2 |
| Infection rate | **21.03%** | 9.69% | 50, level II-2 |
| Best aesthetic results | **75.0%** | 67.6% (p<0.01) | 50, level II-2 |
| Grade IV capsular contracture | **1.22%** | 6.3% (p<0.01) | 50, level II-2 |

### Delayed vs Immediate Reconstruction

| Metric | Delayed | Immediate | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall complications | **Lower** OR=0.38 (0.24 to 0.62) | Higher | 51, level II-2 |
| Major complications | **Lower** OR=0.52 (0.31 to 0.89) | Higher | 51, level II-2 |

> **Clinical Note:** In delayed reconstruction in a previously irradiated patient, **autologous tissue** reconstruction is the preferred method.²⁶

## 6.5 Surgery for Metastatic Breast Cancer

Patients with stage IV breast cancer have poor prognosis with **5-year survival rate of 27%**.⁵²˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

### a. Surgery on Primary Tumour

| Study | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| RCT (surgical vs non-surgical) | No benefit in OS: HR=0.691 (0.358 to 1.333) | 53, level I |
| Cochrane SR | No OS difference: HR=0.83 (0.53 to 1.31) | 54, level I |
| — Local PFS | **Improved:** HR=0.22 (0.08 to 0.57) | 54, level I |
| — Distant PFS | **Worse:** HR=1.42 (1.08 to 1.86) | 54, level I |

> **Clinical Note:** Surgery after initial systemic therapy may be considered in patients requiring **palliation** of symptoms or with impending complications (fungating tumour, pain, bleeding, ulceration).²⁶

### b. Local Treatment of Metastatic Sites

#### i. Bone Metastases

*   RT provides adequate pain control in **75–85%** of patients⁵⁵˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ
*   Surgery for symptomatic thoraco-lumbar spinal metastases: 30-day mortality **4.9%**, 3-month survival **81.5%**, improvement of neurological function⁵⁶˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵ⁻²

#### ii. Lung Metastases

Resection of lung metastases may offer survival benefit for patients with:⁵⁷˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵ⁻²
*   Disease-free interval >36 months (p=0.0007)
*   Unilateral pulmonary metastases (p=0.0267)
*   Complete metastasectomy (p=0.0153)

#### iii. Liver Metastases

*   Liver resection offered survival advantage in carefully selected patients with isolated liver metastases⁵⁸⁻⁵⁹˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ
*   Liver resection carried low post-operative mortality if performed in large-volume hepatobiliary centres

#### iv. Brain Metastases

*   Gross total resection with SRS: reduced LR (HR=0.32, 0.17 to 0.6), longer OS (HR=0.6, 0.39 to 0.91) vs SRS alone for large brain metastases⁶²˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵ⁻²
*   WBRT alone: reserved for patients with poor performance status and multiple unresectable metastases

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 14**
> *   An individualised treatment may be offered in selected patients with metastatic breast cancer.

## 6.6 Loco-Regional Recurrence

All patients with loco-regional recurrence (LRR) should be managed by an MDT to discuss all suitable treatment options.

The decision for further systemic therapy should be individualised based on:⁶³
*   Tumour biology
*   Previous treatment
*   Length of disease-free interval
*   Patient-related factors (co-morbidities, preferences, etc.)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 15**
> *   An individualised approach should be offered in patients with loco-regional recurrent breast cancer.
