## Overview

Supportive treatment is essential for the complete management of breast cancer and covers psychosocial assessment, breast care nursing, lifestyle modifications, palliative care, patient navigation and support groups.

---

## a. Psychosocial Assessment and Intervention

Most breast cancer patients experience at least some psychosocial distress during the course of diagnosis and treatment.

### Prevalence of Psychological Distress (Malaysian Data)

| Condition | Prevalence |
|---|---|
| Overall psychological distress | **47.1%** |
| Depression | 25.3% |
| Anxiety | 18.8% |
| Post-traumatic stress disorder | 19.6% |

*Sources: Chan CM, et al. 2015¹³⁹; Chan CMH, et al. 2018¹⁴⁰*

### Common Psychosocial Concerns¹⁴¹

| Concern |
|---|
| Fear of recurrence |
| Physical symptoms (fatigue, sleep disturbance, pain) |
| Body image disruption |
| Sexual dysfunction |
| Treatment-related anxieties |
| Intrusive thoughts about illness/persistent anxiety |
| Marital/partner communication |
| Feelings of vulnerability |
| Existential concerns regarding mortality |

> **Key Insight:** Almost half of Malaysian families with cancer survivors experienced financial constraints.¹⁴² Other issues include lack of constant psychosocial support and tendency to delay or default treatment.¹⁴³⁻¹⁵⁰

### Screening for Distress

*   Women with breast cancer should be screened for emotional distress using **validated self-assessment tools** by trained healthcare providers.⁴
*   Assessment should occur at **key time points**: diagnosis, during treatment, after treatment, and throughout survivorship.

#### Recommended Screening Tools

| Tool | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ)** | Recommended |
| **Emotion Thermometer (ET)** | Recommended |
| Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) | Previously widely used; now copyrighted and requires payment |

### Psychosocial Interventions

The following should be offered by trained healthcare providers:⁴

| Intervention |
|---|
| Cognitive behaviour therapy (CBT) |
| Psychosocial support |
| Psycho-education programmes (printed materials, audiovisual materials, telephone support and counselling) |
| Managing Cancer and Living Meaningfully (CALM)¹⁵¹˒¹⁵² |
| Mindfulness Therapy¹⁵³ |
| Acceptance and Commitment Therapy¹⁵⁴ |

## b. Breast Care Nurse (BCN)

A breast care nurse improves continuity of care and provides important information, support and referral for a wide range of needs.¹⁵⁵

### Improving BCN Accessibility

| Strategy |
|---|
| Clarification of role — focusing on psychosocial assessment and support |
| Appropriate training, accreditation and ongoing professional development |
| Inclusion in MDT |
| Availability in rural and remote areas through telephone and video conferencing |
| Sustainable, ongoing funding for positions |

> **Clinical Note:** A breast care nurse should be assigned to **all patients** to provide information on management and psychosocial support throughout diagnosis, treatment and follow-up.⁴

## c. Lifestyle Modifications

Lifestyle changes can be recommended to patients as an adjunct to standard breast cancer treatment.¹⁵⁶

### Key Findings

*   Malaysian breast cancer cohort study: high proportion of survivors being overweight/obese¹⁴⁸˒¹⁵⁷
*   No particular diet style has been found to be more beneficial for reducing recurrence risk¹⁵⁶

### Recommended Healthy Lifestyle

| Recommendation | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Regular physical activity (**≥150 min/week**) | Most robust effect of all lifestyle factors on reducing recurrence¹⁵⁶ |
| Achieve and maintain healthy weight | Through regular physical activity and dietary modification |
| Limit alcohol | Below 5 units/week (3–4 drinks/week or 6 g alcohol/day)²⁵ |
| Smoking cessation | —²⁵ |

## d. Palliative Care

Palliative care aims to improve quality of life of patients and families in facing life-threatening illness by effective pain management and addressing other distressing symptoms. It also incorporates psychosocial and spiritual care.¹⁵⁸

### Key Principles

*   Palliative care begins at **diagnosis** of advanced breast cancer
*   Should be delivered **concurrently** with disease-directed therapies
*   Facilitates patient's autonomy on further management based on medical information
*   Should be initiated by the **primary care team** and then augmented by a palliative care physician

### Essential Components of Palliative Care²⁶

| Component |
|---|
| Establishment of rapport and relationships with patients and family/caregivers |
| Management of symptoms, psychosocial/spiritual distress and functional status |
| Exploration of patient's understanding and education about illness and prognosis |
| Clarification of treatment goals |
| Assessment and support of coping needs |
| Assistance with medical decision making |
| Coordination with other care providers |
| Provision of referrals to other care providers as indicated |

## e. Patient Navigation Programme (PNP)

Patient navigation programme is a community-based service delivery intervention designed to promote access to timely diagnosis and treatment of cancer by eliminating barriers to care.¹⁵⁹˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

### Principles of PNP

| # | Principle |
|---|---|
| 1 | Navigation is a **patient-centric** healthcare delivery model |
| 2 | Core function: elimination of barriers to timely care through one-on-one relationship |
| 3 | Navigator's role should be clearly defined and integrated into the healthcare team |
| 4 | Delivery should be **cost-effective** and commensurate with required skills |
| 5 | Who navigates should be determined by skill level required (trained lay persons to professionals) |
| 6 | Navigation begins and does not end until the cancer is resolved |
| 7 | Navigate patients across disconnected levels of care (primary to tertiary) |
| 8 | Larger systems require a navigation coordinator or champion |

### Evidence

*   PNP is effective in improving screening rates, adherence to follow-up, and timeliness of diagnosis¹⁵⁹˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ
*   Minimal research on effectiveness for post-treatment surveillance¹⁶⁰˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵ

> **Key Insight:** Malaysia has only **66.8%** 5-year survival rate, mainly due to late presentation and poor adherence to treatment.² Cancer Research Malaysia has identified PNP as a potential community-based solution.¹⁶¹

## f. Breast Cancer Patient Support Groups

Breast cancer support groups are invaluable resources that provide:

| Function |
|---|
| Emotional, social and material support |
| Empowering patients to self-care during and after treatment |
| Public education on breast cancer awareness |

*   A case-control study showed patients supported by a peer group enjoyed **higher quality of life** compared with others.¹⁶²˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵ⁻²
