## Overview

Triple assessment — consisting of clinical assessment, imaging (ultrasound and/or mammography) and pathology (histology and/or cytology) — is an established method for the diagnosis of breast cancer.⁴

---

## 5.1 Triple Assessment

### 5.1.1 Clinical Assessment

Adequate history taking to assess risk and thorough clinical breast examination (CBE) are mandatory in breast cancer diagnosis. The degree of suspicion based on CBE is variable.

---

### 5.1.2 Imaging

#### a. Mammography + Ultrasonography

Combined mammography and ultrasound (US) assessment improve breast cancer detection in symptomatic women above 35 years old. In women **younger than 35**, US should be used as the initial imaging modality.⁴

#### b. Digital Breast Tomosynthesis (DBT)

DBT (3D mammography) is a screening and diagnostic imaging tool. Strong evidence supports its value:

##### DBT + FFDM vs FFDM Alone (Meta-analysis)¹⁷˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

| Metric | DBT + FFDM | FFDM Alone |
|---|---|---|
| Sensitivity | **90.77%** (80.70 to 96.51) | 60.00% (47.10 to 71.96) |
| Specificity | **96.49%** (96.04 to 96.90) | 95.55% (95.04 to 96.01) |
| Reduction in recall rate | -27.6 per 1000 screens (95% CI -30.8 to -24.5) | — |
| Reduction in false positive rate | -29.5 per 1000 screens (95% CI -32.9 to -26.4) | — |

*   Incremental detection rate of **3.9 cancers per 1000** (95% CI 2.7 to 5.1) for DBT vs FFDM.¹⁹˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ
*   Pooled DOR of DBT: **26.04** (95% CI 8.70 to 77.95) vs DOR of FFDM: 16.24 (95% CI 5.61 to 47.04).²⁰˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ
*   Sensitivity of DBT ranges from 69–100%, specificity 54–100%.²¹˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵ⁻²

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 3**
> *   Digital breast tomosynthesis may be considered in screening and diagnosis of breast cancer based on its availability.

---

#### c. BI-RADS® Reporting System

BI-RADS® (Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System) was established by ACR in 1993 to standardise breast imaging reporting and reduce variations in interpretation.²²˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

##### BI-RADS® Assessment Categories

| Category | Assessment | Management | Likelihood of Cancer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Need additional imaging or prior examinations | Recall for additional imaging | Not applicable |
| **1** | Negative | Routine screening | Essentially 0% |
| **2** | Benign | Routine screening | Essentially 0% |
| **3** | Probably benign | Short interval follow-up (6 months) | >0% but ≤2% |
| **4a** | Low suspicion of malignancy | Tissue diagnosis | >2% to ≤10% |
| **4b** | Moderate suspicion of malignancy | Tissue diagnosis | >10% to ≤50% |
| **4c** | High suspicion of malignancy | Tissue diagnosis | >50% to <95% |
| **5** | Highly suggestive of malignancy | Tissue diagnosis | ≥95% |
| **6** | Known biopsy-proven malignancy | Surgical excision when appropriate | Not applicable |

##### BI-RADS® Breast Composition Categories (2013 Edition)

| Category | Description | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | Almost entirely fatty | Mammography is highly sensitive |
| **b** | Scattered areas of fibroglandular density | — |
| **c** | Heterogeneously dense | May obscure small masses |
| **d** | Extremely dense | Lowers sensitivity of mammography |

> **Clinical Note:** In BI-RADS 2013, the use of percentages is discouraged. Assignment is based on the chance that a mass can be obscured by fibroglandular tissue, rather than the percentage of breast density.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 4**
> *   Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS®) is the preferred reporting method in the management of breast cancer.

---

#### d. Image-Guided Biopsy

MIBT is the standard of care for diagnosing most breast lesions. Image-guided MIBT is recommended for both palpable and non-palpable lesions to increase accuracy of sampling.²³˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

*   **Core needle biopsy** or vacuum-assisted technique is preferable to FNAC for better tumour characterisation, marker analysis and immunohistochemistry.²³˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ
*   Pathology and imaging concordance by MIBT shows a **success rate of ≥90%**.²³˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

#### e. Management of Discordant Lesions

Repeat image-guided percutaneous core or vacuum-assisted needle biopsy is an alternative when initial core biopsy results are non-diagnostic or discordant. Another alternative is surgical excision.²⁴˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 5**
> *   MIBT with core needle is the preferred diagnostic technique for both palpable and non-palpable breast lesions.
> *   Repeat image-guided MIBT or consider surgical excision when the initial core biopsy results are non-diagnostic or discordant with the imaging findings.

---

#### f. Role of MRI

MRI may be considered in the following clinical situations:⁴˒¹⁸˒²⁵˒²⁶

| Clinical Situation |
|---|
| Invasive lobular cancer |
| LCIS |
| Suspicion of multicentricity |
| Genetic high risk |
| Occult disease (T0 N+/M+ disease) |
| Paget's disease without radiological evidence of underlying tumour |
| Breast implants/foreign bodies |
| Diagnosis of recurrence in previous breast reconstruction |
| Follow-up after neo-adjuvant therapy |
| Dense breasts |
| Pre-operative planning in BCS |

> **Clinical Note:** Surgical decisions should **NOT** be based solely on MRI findings. Additional tissue sampling of areas of concern identified by breast MRI is recommended.

## 5.1.3 Laboratory Investigation

### a. Histopathological Examination

Core needle biopsy is a well-established tool for diagnosing both palpable and non-palpable breast lesions, as part of the triple assessment.

*   If core needle biopsy is not available, **FNAC** may be considered for palpable breast lumps. In equivocal FNAC, core biopsy should be performed.

#### Minimum Dataset for Surgical Pathology Report

| Element |
|---|
| Maximum diameter of invasive tumour |
| Location (side and quadrant), multifocality/multicentricity |
| Tumour type (WHO classification) |
| Histological grade |
| Lymph node involvement and total number examined |
| Resection margins |
| Lymphovascular invasion |
| Non-neoplastic breast changes |
| Hormone receptor status (ER/PR) |
| HER2/c-erbB2 assessment |

#### ER/PR Receptor Status

*   **Positive interpretation:** ≥1% of tumour cells showing positive nuclear staining of any intensity
*   **Negative:** <1% of tumour cells show staining of any intensity²⁷˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 6**
> *   Estrogen and progesterone receptors status should be assessed in **all cases** of breast cancer.
> *   HER2 test using immunohistochemistry should be performed on **all invasive** breast cancer specimens.
> *   In-situ hybridisation test should be done **only in equivocal HER2 (IHC 2+)** on invasive breast cancer specimens.

---

### b. HER2 Testing

HER2 is a trans-membrane epidermal growth factor receptor important in tumour growth signalling. Assessment is essential for prognosis and eligibility for HER2-targeted therapy.

| IHC Result | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **3+** | HER2 positive | No further testing needed |
| **2+** | Equivocal | Reflex ISH test on same or new specimen |
| **0 to 1+** | HER2 negative | No further testing needed |

*   HER2 can be accurately assessed in core needle biopsy: **sensitivity 93%** (80.94–98.5%), **specificity 99.9%** (98.05–100%).²⁸˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

#### Criteria for Re-testing HER2

**A new HER2 test should be ordered on the excision specimen** if initial core biopsy is negative AND any of the following:
*   Tumour is grade 3
*   Amount of invasive tumour in the core biopsy specimen is small
*   Resection specimen contains high-grade carcinoma morphologically distinct from core
*   Core biopsy result is equivocal after both ISH and IHC
*   Doubt about handling of the core biopsy specimen

*Source: Wolff AC, et al. J Clin Oncol. 2018.*²⁹

### c. Ki67 as Predictor and Prognostic Marker

Ki67 (anti-MIB-1) detects the proliferative index of tumours.

#### Evidence on Ki67 Predictive Value

| Study | Finding | HR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Cohort (early BC) — IBTR | High Ki67 predicted IBTR | 3.126 (1.390 to 7.029) |
| Cohort (early BC) — LRR | High Ki67 predicted LRR | 3.759 (1.923 to 7.340) |
| Cohort (early BC) — DMFS | High Ki67 predicted DMFS | 3.436 (1.926 to 6.130) |
| Cohort (early BC) — BCSS | High Ki67 predicted BCSS | 4.948 (2.530 to 9.674) |
| Luminal B reclassification — LRR | LB predicted LRR | 3.612 (1.555 to 8.340) |
| Luminal B reclassification — DMFS | LB predicted DMFS | 3.023 (1.501 to 6.087) |
| Luminal B reclassification — BCSS | LB predicted BCSS | 3.617 (1.629 to 8.031) |

*Sources: Millar EK, et al.³⁰˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵ⁻²; Pérez-López ME, et al.³²˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵ⁻²*

> **Clinical Note:** Lack of consistency in Ki67 method and interpretation across laboratories limits its value. Contradicting evidence warrants better studies. The use of Ki67 is based on **clinician's discretion**.

## 5.2 Staging

Staging is based on the current AJCC Cancer Staging Manual (8th Edition).³³˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

> **Cross-reference:** See Appendix: TNM Classification for detailed staging tables.

### 5.2.1 Early Breast Cancer

*   In **asymptomatic** early breast cancer (DCIS, stage I, IIA, IIB): routine imaging screening for metastasis should **NOT** be performed.⁴
*   In patients with **signs/symptoms** of lung, liver and bone diseases or abnormal labs: imaging should be performed.
*   **Bone scan** is indicated for localised bone pain or elevated ALP if not visualised by plain radiograph or CT.⁴

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 7**
> *   Patients with early breast cancer and:
>     *   Asymptomatic — routine imaging screening for metastasis should **NOT** be performed
>     *   With signs/symptoms of lung, liver, bone metastases or abnormal labs — routine imaging **should** be performed
>     *   With localised bone pain, elevated ALP or suggestive symptoms — bone scintigraphy if plain radiography or CT staging is negative

### 5.2.2 Locally Advanced Breast Cancer (Stage III)

More imaging is needed for staging: CT scan, bone scintigraphy, MRI and PET/CT.²⁵˒²⁶

LABC includes:³⁴˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ
*   Large breast tumours (>5 cm)
*   Cancers involving skin of the breast or underlying chest wall muscles
*   Cancers involving multiple ipsilateral axillary, internal mammary or infra/supra-clavicular lymph nodes
*   Inflammatory breast cancer

### 5.2.3 Advanced (Metastatic) Breast Cancer (Stage IV)

Similar imaging as LABC for assessment of bony and visceral metastases.²⁵˒²⁶

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 8**
> *   Patients with locally advanced and advanced breast cancer: CT, bone scintigraphy, MRI or PET/CT should be done to assess the extent of disease depending on indications.

---

### Role of ¹⁸FDG PET/CT

PET/CT scanning is **NOT indicated** for staging of clinical stage I, II or operable III (T3N1) breast cancer due to:²⁶
*   High false-negative rate for small (<1 cm) and/or low-grade lesions
*   Low sensitivity for axillary nodal metastases
*   Low prior probability of detectable metastatic disease
*   High rate of false-positive scans

#### Diagnostic Performance Comparison

| Modality | Sensitivity | Specificity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| ¹⁸FDG PET/CT (distant metastases) | **0.97** (0.84 to 0.99) | **0.95** (0.93 to 0.97) | 35, level III |
| Conventional imaging (distant metastases) | 0.56 (0.38 to 0.74) | 0.91 (0.78 to 0.97) | 35, level III |
| ¹⁸FDG PET/CT (bone metastases) | **0.93** (0.82 to 0.98) | **0.99** (0.95 to 1.00) | 36, level III |
| Bone scintigraphy (bone metastases) | 0.81 (0.58 to 0.93) | 0.96 (0.76 to 1.00) | 36, level III |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 9**
> *   ¹⁸FDG PET/CT may be considered when standard imaging studies are **equivocal or suspicious** in locally advanced or metastatic disease.
