## Overview

Systemic therapy for breast cancer includes neoadjuvant therapy, adjuvant therapy, and therapy for metastatic disease. It encompasses chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, anti-HER2 therapy, and bone-modifying agents.

---

## 6.3.1 Neoadjuvant Therapy

Neoadjuvant therapy refers to the administration of treatment with the intent of downstaging the tumour and improving operability and surgical outcomes.

### a. Chemotherapy

| Finding | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| NACT vs adjuvant chemo — BCS rate | **Higher** with NACT: 64.8% vs 49.0% (RR=1.28, 1.22 to 1.34) | 64, level I |
| NACT vs adjuvant chemo — LR rate | **Higher** with NACT: 21.4% vs 15.9% (RR=1.37, 1.17 to 1.61) | 64, level I |
| NACT vs adjuvant chemo — Breast cancer mortality | **No difference:** 34.4% vs 33.7% (RR=1.06, 0.95 to 1.18) | 64, level I |
| Highest pCR rate | **Triple negative** and **HER2-positive** tumours | 65, level I |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 16**
> *   Neoadjuvant chemotherapy may be offered to patients with **triple negative or HER2-positive** early breast cancer to enable breast conserving surgery, but its benefits and risks need to be discussed with the patients.

---

### b. Endocrine Therapy

The role of neoadjuvant endocrine therapy (NET) is generally reserved for candidates unsuitable for chemotherapy or surgery.

| Comparison | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| NET vs NACT — Clinical response rate | **No significant difference** | 66, level I |
| NET vs NACT — BCS rate | **No significant difference** | 66, level I |
| NET vs NACT — Toxicity profile | **Significantly lower** in NET arm | 66, level I |
| AIs vs tamoxifen (subgroup) — Clinical response | **AIs superior:** OR=1.69 (1.36 to 2.10) | 66, level I |
| AIs vs tamoxifen — BCS rate | **AIs superior:** OR=1.62 (1.24 to 2.12) | 66, level I |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 17**
> *   Neoadjuvant endocrine therapy, preferably aromatase inhibitors, may be considered in **post-menopausal women** with hormone receptor-positive breast cancer who are not suitable for chemotherapy.

---

### c. Anti-HER2 Therapy (Neoadjuvant)

HER2 is overexpressed in **15–20%** of breast cancers and is associated with an aggressive clinical course.⁶⁷

#### Key Evidence

| Trial | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Pooled RCTs** — Trastuzumab + chemo vs chemo alone | pCR higher: RR=2.07 (1.39 to 2.46) | 68, level I |
| **NeoSphere** (Phase II) — Chemo + dual HER2 blockade | pCR **45.8%** vs 29% (trastuzumab + docetaxel alone) | 69, level I |
| **TRYPHAENA** (Phase II) — Pertuzumab + trastuzumab + chemo | pCR 57–66% across all arms; similar cardiac safety | 70, level I |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 18**
> *   Chemotherapy and **trastuzumab-based treatment** should be offered to patients with HER2-positive breast cancer who require neoadjuvant therapy.
> *   Addition of **pertuzumab** as dual HER2 blockade may be considered in high risk patients (i.e. ≥T2 or ≥N1 early stage breast cancer).

#### Subcutaneous Trastuzumab

In a large multicentre RCT, SC trastuzumab was non-inferior to IV formulation in both neoadjuvant and adjuvant setting:⁷¹˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵ
*   pCR: **42.2%** (SC) vs **37.4%** (IV) — difference 4.7% (95% CI -4.0 to 13.4)
*   Adverse events of any grade: comparable between groups

## 6.3.2 Adjuvant Therapy

Adjuvant therapy is used after surgery to reduce the rate of cancer recurrence.

### Risk Categories for Adjuvant Systemic Therapy (St. Gallen)

| Low Risk | Intermediate Risk | High Risk |
|---|---|---|
| pN0 AND all of: tumour ≤2 cm, Grade 1, no vessel invasion, ER/PR+, HER2-, age ≥35 | pN0 AND at least one of: tumour >2 cm, Grade 2/3, vessel invasion, HER2 overexpression, age <35; OR pN+ (N1-3) and HER2- | pN+ (N1-3) and HER2 overexpression; OR pN+ (N≥4) |

---

### a. Adjuvant Chemotherapy

#### Taxane-Based Chemotherapy Evidence

| Finding | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Taxane vs non-taxane — DFS | **Improved:** OR=0.82 (0.76 to 0.88) | 73, level I |
| Taxane vs non-taxane — OS | **Improved:** OR=0.83 (0.75 to 0.91) | 73, level I |
| Node positive: docetaxel vs paclitaxel — OS | **Docetaxel superior:** OR=0.79 (0.63 to 0.98) | 73, level I |
| Sequential taxane-anthracycline vs concurrent — DFS | **Sequential superior:** RR=0.90 (0.84 to 0.98) | 74, level I |
| Taxane-anthracycline vs anthracycline — Leukaemia | **Reduced:** RR=0.40 (0.18 to 0.90) | 75, level I |
| Taxane-anthracycline vs anthracycline — Neurotoxicity | **Increased:** RR=5.97 (1.72 to 20.65) | 75, level I |

**Optimal administration:** Paclitaxel — weekly; Docetaxel — tri-weekly.⁷³˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵ

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 19**
> *   Taxane-based adjuvant chemotherapy should be offered in patients requiring adjuvant chemotherapy, especially in **node positive** breast cancer.

---

### b. Adjuvant Endocrine Therapy

Five years of adjuvant endocrine therapy is the standard of care for hormone receptor-positive breast cancer.

#### Extended Endocrine Therapy Evidence

| Regimen | Key Outcome | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 10 years tamoxifen vs 5 years | Recurrence reduced: RR=0.84 (0.76 to 0.94) | 76, level I |
| — BC mortality (after 10 years) | Reduced: RR=0.71 (0.58 to 0.88) | 76, level I |
| — Endometrial cancer risk | Increased: RR=1.74 (1.30 to 2.34) | 76, level I |
| 5yr tamoxifen + 5yr letrozole vs 5yr tamoxifen | DFS improved: HR=0.57 (0.43 to 0.75); no OS benefit | 77, level I |
| 10 years AI vs 5 years AI | No significant DFS or OS benefit | 78, level I |

#### Current Options for Adjuvant Endocrine Therapy

| Option |
|---|
| Tamoxifen alone for 5 years |
| AIs alone for 5 years |
| Sequential tamoxifen + AIs for 5 years |
| Tamoxifen up to 10 years |
| Tamoxifen followed by extended AIs for 10 years |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 20**
> *   Adjuvant extended endocrine therapy may be offered to hormone receptor-positive breast cancer based on individual risk of recurrence and potential side effects.

### Monitoring Bone Health on Aromatase Inhibitors

AIs are associated with risk of fracture due to bone loss.⁷⁹

#### When to Start Bisphosphonates

Start if DEXA scan shows **osteoporosis** or **moderate-severe osteopenia (T score < -2.0)** or accelerated bone loss (≥4%/year). Also consider if **≥2** of these risk factors are present:⁸⁰˒ ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

| Risk Factor |
|---|
| Age >65 years |
| T score < -1.5 on DEXA |
| Smoking (current or previous) |
| Family history of hip fracture |
| Personal history of fragility fracture >50 years |
| Oral corticosteroids >6 months |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 21**
> *   All breast cancer patients on aromatase inhibitors should have **bone densitometry** done at baseline and periodically thereafter.
> *   Bisphosphonates or denosumab should be started if T score < -2.0 on DEXA or patient has ≥2 risk factors of osteoporosis.

### c. Ovarian Suppression/Ablation

| Finding | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| SOFT/TEXT (OAS + tamoxifen vs tamoxifen alone) | Improved DFS and OS in high-risk pre-menopausal | 81, level I |
| Meta-analysis (29 RCTs) — Women ≤40 years | DFS improved: HR=0.84 (0.73 to 0.97) | 82, level I |
| — OS | Improved: HR=0.78 (0.66 to 0.94) | 82, level I |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 22**
> *   Ovarian ablation or suppression may be offered in **pre-menopausal women** with high risk early or advanced stage breast cancer.

---

### d. Adjuvant Anti-HER2 Therapy

#### Duration of Trastuzumab

| Study | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cochrane SR — Trastuzumab vs no trastuzumab | OS improved: HR=0.66 (0.57 to 0.77) | 83, level I |
| — | DFS improved: HR=0.60 (0.50 to 0.71) | 83, level I |
| **PERSEPHONE** — 6 vs 12 months | Non-inferior 4yr DFS: HR=1.07 (90% CI 0.93 to 1.24) | 85, level I |
| **Meta-analysis** (5 RCTs) — 1yr vs shorter | 1 year more effective: HR for DFS=1.31 (1.08 to 1.59) | 86, level I |
| **Aphinity** — Pertuzumab + trastuzumab | Small iDFS improvement: HR=0.81 (0.66 to 1.00); no OS benefit | 87, level I |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 23**
> *   **Trastuzumab** should be given to women with HER2-positive breast cancer having adjuvant chemotherapy.
> *   Trastuzumab for **6 months** in adjuvant setting may be considered based on the discretion of the treating clinicians.

## 6.3.3 Systemic Therapy for Metastatic Disease

Treatment choice in MBC is influenced by: patient factors (age, co-morbidities, performance status, preferences, menopausal status), tumour biology (HR and HER2 status), previous therapies, disease-free interval, tumour load, visceral crisis, and resource availability.⁶³

### a. Chemotherapy for MBC

| Study | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cochrane SR — Combination vs single agent | OS: HR=0.82 (0.75 to 0.89); better TTP and response rate | 88, level I |
| Meta-analysis — After anthracycline/taxane | OS: HR=0.90 (0.84 to 0.96) | 89, level I |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 24**
> *   Combination chemotherapy may be considered in **fit MBC patients** with impending visceral crisis or when rapid resolution of symptoms is required.

---

### b. Endocrine Therapy for MBC

Endocrine therapy is the preferred option for HR-positive MBC, unless visceral crisis or endocrine resistance.

#### Definitions of Endocrine Resistance

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Primary** resistance | Relapse while on first 2 years of adjuvant ET; or PD within first 6 months of first-line ET for advanced BC |
| **Secondary** resistance | Relapse after first 2 years on ET; or relapse within 12 months of completing adjuvant ET; or PD >6 months after initiating ET for advanced BC |

#### CDK 4/6 Inhibitors — First-Line Evidence

| Trial | Combination | Median PFS vs Control | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PALOMA-2** | Palbociclib + letrozole | **24.8 vs 14.5 months** HR=0.58 | 91, level I |
| **MONALEESA-2** | Ribociclib + letrozole | **25.3 vs 16.0 months** HR=0.568 | 92, level I |
| **MONALEESA-7** (pre-menopausal) | Ribociclib + endocrine + goserelin | HR=0.55 (0.44 to 0.69) | 93, level I |
| — OS update | — | HR for death=**0.71** (0.54 to 0.95) | 94, level I |
| **MONARCH 3** | Abemaciclib + AI | Effective and safe | 95, level I |

#### Other Combinations

| Agent | Key Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Fulvestrant** vs anastrozole (FALCON) | PFS improved: HR=0.797 (0.637 to 0.999) | 90, level I |
| **Everolimus** + exemestane (BOLERO-2) | PFS improved: HR=0.43 (0.35 to 0.54); higher stomatitis/pneumonitis | 96, level I |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 25**
> *   Endocrine therapy should be considered as **first-line treatment** in HR-positive, HER2-negative MBC unless there is evidence of visceral crisis or endocrine resistance.

---

### c. Anti-HER2 Therapy for MBC

| Trial/Study | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cochrane SR — Trastuzumab in HER2+ MBC | OS: HR=0.82 (0.71 to 0.94); PFS: HR=0.61 (0.54 to 0.70) | 97, level I |
| — Cardiac risk | CHF: RR=3.49 (1.88 to 6.47); LVEF decline: RR=2.65 (1.48 to 4.74) | 97, level I |
| **CLEOPATRA** — Pertuzumab + trastuzumab + docetaxel | PFS prolonged 6.3 months (HR=0.68); OS prolonged 15.7 months | 98, level I |
| **EMILIA** — T-DM1 vs lapatinib + capecitabine | PFS: HR=0.65 (0.55 to 0.77); OS: 30.9 vs 25.1 months | 99, level I |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 26**
> *   Anti-HER2 blockade may be considered in HER2-positive MBC.

## 6.3.4 Supportive Therapy — Bone-Modifying Agents

| Study | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Meta-analysis — Bisphosphonates (adjuvant) | No OS benefit (HR=0.89, 0.79 to 1.01); lower bone fractures (RR=0.59) | 100, level I |
| Cochrane SR — Bisphosphonates (MBC) | Reduced SRE risk: RR=0.86 (0.78 to 0.95); ONJ <0.5% | 101, level I |
| Local evaluation | **12-weekly IV zoledronic acid** most cost-effective | 102, level I |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Recommendation 27**
> *   Bisphosphonates may be offered in breast cancer patients with bone metastases to reduce skeletal-related events.
> *   The preferred regimen is **12-weekly intravenous zoledronic acid**.

## Appendix: Medications and Common Side Effects

The table below summarises key medications used in systemic therapy of breast cancer and their common side effects.

### Chemotherapy Agents

| Medication | Common Side Effects | Key Monitoring |
|---|---|---|
| **Capecitabine** | Hand-foot syndrome, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, mucositis, hyperbilirubinaemia | Cardiotoxicity; dose reduction in moderate renal dysfunction |
| **Cyclophosphamide** | Myelosuppression, nausea, vomiting, alopecia, rashes | Urotoxicity risk; monitor CBC |
| **Docetaxel** | Neutropenia, anaemia, febrile neutropenia, hypersensitivity, neuropathy, alopecia, fluid retention | Prophylactic anti-emetics and corticosteroids; not given if neutrophils <1500 |
| **Doxorubicin** | Cardiomyopathy/CHF (seldom but can be fatal), extravasation/skin necrosis, myelosuppression, mucositis, alopecia | Cardiac function monitoring; cumulative dose limit |
| **Epirubicin** | Cardiac toxicity, myelosuppression, amenorrhoea, conjunctivitis, mucositis, alopecia | Cardiac function monitoring; caution in hepatic impairment |
| **Eribulin** | Neutropenia, anaemia, fatigue, nausea, constipation, peripheral neuropathy | Monitor CBC prior to each dose |
| **5-Fluorouracil** | Diarrhoea, stomatitis, angina, hand-foot syndrome, alopecia | Cardiotoxicity monitoring; caution in heart disease |
| **Gemcitabine** | Neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, increased liver enzymes, proteinuria, dyspnoea, rash | Monitor renal and hepatic function |
| **Paclitaxel** | Hypersensitivity, neurotoxicity, neutropenia, alopecia, nausea | Pre-treat with corticosteroids, antihistamines and H2 antagonists |
| **Vinorelbine** | Neutropenia, anaemia, liver enzyme elevation, nausea, constipation, peripheral neuropathy, asthenia | Monitor CBC; liver function during treatment; consider stool softener |

### Targeted Therapy Agents

| Medication | Common Side Effects | Key Monitoring |
|---|---|---|
| **Abemaciclib** | Diarrhoea, nausea, fatigue, neutropenia, infections | Monitor CBC; monitor every 2 weeks first 2 months, then monthly |
| **Ado-trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1)** | Infusion reactions, hepatotoxicity, thrombocytopenia, fatigue, nausea | Hepatic and cardiac toxicity monitoring; can be fatal |
| **Everolimus** | Stomatitis, infections, rash, diarrhoea, fatigue, decreased appetite | Monitor CBC every 6 months in first year |
| **Lapatinib** | Diarrhoea, nausea, hand-foot syndrome, rash, fatigue | Monitor hand-foot syndrome |
| **Olaparib** | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, constipation, stomatitis | Monitor haematological toxicity at baseline and monthly |
| **Palbociclib** | Neutropenia, leucopenia, anaemia, fatigue, nausea, diarrhoea, stomatitis | CBC at beginning of each cycle and day 15 of first 2 cycles |
| **Pertuzumab** | Alopecia, rash, diarrhoea, neutropenia, fatigue; LVEF dysfunction (seldom but can be fatal) | Cardiac function monitoring; prophylactic antihistamines |
| **Ribociclib** | Nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhoea, alopecia, rash | Cardiac function monitoring; caution in hepatic impairment |
| **Trastuzumab** | Cardiotoxicity (can be fatal), infusion reactions, nausea, cough, headache, fatigue | Cardiac monitoring; CBC every 2 weeks for first 2 cycles |

### Hormonal Therapy Agents

| Medication | Common Side Effects | Key Monitoring |
|---|---|---|
| **Anastrozole** | Hot flushes, arthritis/arthralgia, bone pain, osteoporosis, fractures, hypertension | Bone mineral density throughout treatment |
| **Exemestane** | Hot flushes, sweating, nausea, musculoskeletal pain, arthralgia, fatigue | Bone mineral density throughout treatment |
| **Fulvestrant** | Injection site pain, hot flushes, nausea, bone pain, arthralgia, fatigue, increased hepatic enzymes | Caution in hepatic impairment |
| **Letrozole** | Hot flushes, flushing, headache, dizziness, arthralgia, asthenia | Bone mineral density throughout treatment |
| **Tamoxifen** | Hot flushes, bone/musculoskeletal pain, nausea, gastrointestinal disorders, oedema, hypercholesterolaemia | Caution in patients with history of thromboembolic events |
