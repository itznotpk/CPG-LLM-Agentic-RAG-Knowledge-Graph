## Overview

Breast cancer is the most important cancer among women worldwide including in Malaysia. Regardless of gender, breast cancer contributed to 19.0% of all new cancer cases diagnosed in 2012–2016 compared with 17.7% in 2007–2011. The new cases of breast cancer had increased from 32.1% to 34.1% of overall cancer among women in the same period, a 2% increment. The Age-Standardised Incidence Rate (ASR) increased to 34.1 per 100,000 population (2012–2016) from 31.1 per 100,000 population (2007–2011). The incidence starts to increase at age 25 and peaks at age 60–64 years.ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

## Staging at Diagnosis

At diagnosis, **52.1%** of cases were diagnosed at early stage (stage I and II) with mainly stage II (34.5%). However, the percentage of **late diagnosis** (stage III and IV) had **increased** from 43.2% (2007–2011) to **47.9%** (2012–2016).ˡᵉᵛᵉˡ ᴵᴵᴵ

## Principles for Early Diagnosis

Early diagnosis improves cancer outcomes by provision of effective treatment, at lower cost and with less complex interventions. The principles to achieve early diagnosis are relevant at all healthcare levels:³

1.  Awareness of cancer symptoms and accessing care
2.  Clinical evaluation, diagnosis and staging
3.  Access to treatment

## Rationale for This CPG Update

Over the years, many advancements in management strategy are observed including screening methods, early diagnosis and treatment modalities. Different strategies of breast cancer classification and staging have evolved. Key developments include:

*   Intrinsic (molecular) subtyping — essential in clinical trials
*   Advanced imaging technologies for detecting distant metastasis and recurrent disease
*   Advances in molecular biology and pharmacology — enabling targeted therapy design

In view of these developments, it was timely to update the CPG on Management of Breast Cancer (Second Edition, 2010) to guide healthcare providers based on the latest available evidence.
