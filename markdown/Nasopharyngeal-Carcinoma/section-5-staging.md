> **Context:** TNM staging criteria and radiological imaging recommendations for NPC.

Cancer staging plays an important role in determining the best treatment approach and prognosis of the disease. In this CPG, the latest edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC Cancer Staging Manual 2010, 7th Edition) is used to stage NPC. 

TNM staging consists of a clinical examination alongside pathological and radiological investigations. 
- Clinical examination of the nasopharynx, regional lymph nodes, and distant metastatic sites (lung, liver, bone) is crucial.
- Cranial nerves examination is vital as nerve involvement may be the first and only presentation.

*(For visual diagrams, refer to Appendix 4: TNM Staging Diagram below)*

## 5.1 TNM Staging Definitions

### Primary Tumour (T)
| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| **TX** | Primary tumour cannot be assessed |
| **T0** | No evidence of primary tumour |
| **Tis** | Carcinoma in situ |
| **T1** | Tumour confined to the nasopharynx, or tumor extends to oropharynx and/or nasal cavity without parapharyngeal extension* |
| **T2** | Tumour with parapharyngeal extension* |
| **T3** | Tumour involves bony structures of skull base and/or paranasal sinuses |
| **T4** | Tumour with intracranial extension and/or involvement of cranial nerves, hypopharynx, orbit, or extension to the infratemporal fossa/masticator space |

*\*Note: Parapharyngeal extension denotes posterolateral infiltration of tumour.*

### Regional Lymph Nodes (N)
| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| **NX** | Regional lymph nodes cannot be assessed |
| **N0** | No regional lymph node metastasis |
| **N1** | Unilateral metastasis in cervical lymph node(s), ≤6 cm in greatest dimension, above the supraclavicular fossa, and/or unilateral/bilateral retropharyngeal lymph nodes, ≤6 cm. |
| **N2** | Bilateral metastasis in cervical lymph node(s), ≤6 cm in greatest dimension, above the supraclavicular fossa* |
| **N3a** | Metastasis in a lymph node(s)* >6 cm in dimension |
| **N3b** | Extension to the supraclavicular fossa** |

*\*Note: Midline nodes are considered ipsilateral nodes.*
*\*\*Note: Supraclavicular zone or fossa is defined by: (1) superior margin of sternal end of clavicle, (2) superior margin of lateral end of clavicle, (3) point where neck meets shoulder.*

### Distant Metastasis (M)
| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| **M0** | No distant metastasis |
| **M1** | Distant metastasis |

## 5.2 Anatomic Stage/Prognostic Groups

| Stage | T Class | N Class | M Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 0** | Tis | N0 | M0 |
| **Stage I** | T1 | N0 | M0 |
| **Stage II** | T1, T2 | N0, N1 | M0 |
| **Stage III** | T1, T2 | N2 | M0 |
| **Stage III** | T3 | N0, N1, N2 | M0 |
| **Stage IVA** | T4 | N0, N1, N2 | M0 |
| **Stage IVB** | Any T | N3 | M0 |
| **Stage IVC** | Any T | Any N | M1 |

## 5.3 Radiological Staging Modalities

Imaging studies are essential for clinical staging to identify deep tumour infiltration and locoregional cervical lymph node involvement. 

| Modality | Indication & Advantages | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **MRI** | **Superior for soft tissue involvement.** <br>• More sensitive than CT scan for skull base and intracranial tumour infiltration and identification of retropharyngeal lymph node metastasis (69.0% vs 52.1%, p<0.001). <br>• *Note: No significant difference in detection of the rest of the neck lymph node metastasis compared to CT.* <br>• Capable of depicting obvious focal masses as well as early cancers with only mild mucosal thickening. <br>• Highly accurate for submucosal involvement not detected by endoscopy (Sensitivity 100%, Specificity 93%). | Level III |
| **FDG PET-CT** | **Superior for N and M classification.** <br>• Better sensitivity and specificity compared to other modalities (MRI/CT, radiography, ultrasound, scintigraphy) for N (pooled sensitivity 84%, specificity 90%) and M classification (pooled sensitivity 87%, specificity 98%) in *newly diagnosed* NPC (but not T classification). <br>• High accuracy (96.2) in detecting distant metastasis. <br>• Best modality for diagnosing local residual or recurrent NPC (pooled sensitivity 95%, specificity 90%). | Level III |
| **MRI + FDG PET-CT** | **Best for tumour restaging.** <br>• Combined use is more accurate (overall accuracy of 92.1) for tumour restaging than when either modality is used independently. | Level II-2 |
| **CT Scan** | **Alternative option.** <br>• Widely used in local settings due to the limited availability of MRI and FDG PET-CT. <br>• Specifically indicated when there is a contraindication for MRI, such as the presence of a pacemaker. | Consensus |

*(For visual imaging details, refer to Appendix 5: Radiological Staging Figures below)*

### Recommendation 3

- All nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) patients should be staged using the tumour node metastasis (TNM) system.
- The preferred imaging modality is:
  - **Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)** for local and locoregional staging of NPC.
  - **18 F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography CT (FDG PET-CT)** for distant metastasis in NPC.
- For restaging of residual and recurrent NPC, a combination of MRI and FDG PET-CT should be used.
- When MRI and FDG PET-CT are not feasible, CT scan is an alternative imaging modality in NPC staging.
