> **Context:** Introduction and overview of Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma (NPC) burden and clinical significance.

## 1.1 Disease Overview

| Aspect | Detail | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Epithelial malignant tumour of the nasopharynx | |
| **Global Burden** | Constitutes only 0.7% of cancers worldwide | Level I |
| **Incidence Rate** | ~1 per 100,000 people globally | Level III |
| **Gender Pattern** | Men are 2 to 3 times more likely to develop NPC than women | Level III |
| **Endemic Regions** | Southeast Asia, Southern China, and North African countries | |
| **Ethnic Demographics**| Most commonly occurs among Chinese | |

## 1.2 Local Context (Malaysia)

- NPC is the **4th most common cancer** among Malaysians, making up 5.2% of all cancers. [Level III]

## 1.3 Clinical Challenges

| Challenge Area | Description | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnostic Delay** | Usually diagnosed late due to misleading, trivial presentations | Poor survival outcome |
| **Trivial Symptoms** | Often presents as: <br>• Painless neck lump <br>• Blood-stained saliva/nasal secretion <br>• Unilateral mild ear block | Delays patient health-seeking behaviour [Level III] |
| **Management Goals** | Early diagnosis is essential for prompt access to radiation therapy | Improved patient outcomes |
| **Advanced Disease** | Intermediate or advanced disease poses a difficulty in balancing treatment efficacy against toxicity | Aim is to minimise side effects without compromising survival |

## 1.4 Rationale for Guidelines

The development of an evidence-based CPG for NPC is timely and essential due to:
- High disease burden of NPC in Malaysia
- Variation in current clinical practices
- Significant resource implications and costs
- Lack of standardized local guidelines
