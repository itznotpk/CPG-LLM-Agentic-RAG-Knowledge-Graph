> **Context:** Typical presenting symptoms of NPC and the criteria for early referral.

## 3.1 Clinical Presentation

Healthcare providers need to be aware that NPC patients often present with nonspecific symptoms and signs in the head and neck region. A proper clinical workup which begins with a detailed history of the presenting complaints is pertinent in diagnosing NPC.

### 3.1.1 Common Symptoms

*Evidence Level: III*

| Symptom Category | Frequency | Typical Characteristics | 
|---|---|---|
| **Neck lump/mass** | 42% - 80.8% | Always painless, can be unilateral or bilateral | 
| **Nasal symptoms** | 26% - 49.8% | Blood-stained nasal discharge or saliva, unilateral nose block, epistaxis, or bad breath |
| **Ear symptoms** | 11% - 48.4% | Ear block, deafness, tinnitus, or pain (usually unilateral but can become bilateral as disease progresses) |
| **Ophthalmo-neurologic** | 11% - 14.6% | Unilateral headache, facial numbness, diplopia, ptosis, trismus, dysphagia or hoarseness of voice |

> **Cranial Nerve Note:** The most common cranial nerve involvement is the 5th, followed by 6th, 3rd, 4th, and others.

*(For visual examples, refer to Appendix 3: Clinical Presentations below)*

### 3.1.2 Diagnostic Delays

- **Advanced Presentation:** The majority of NPC patients in Malaysia present with advanced disease (Stages III/IV) at the time of diagnosis (75% - 85%). 
- **Cause of Delay:** This is widely attributed to a lack of awareness regarding NPC symptoms and signs among both patients and doctors. [Level III]

## 3.2 Referral

There is no retrieved evidence on definitive referral criteria for patients with NPC. In view of the widespread delay in diagnosis, the CPG DG used consensus methods to emphasize the importance of urgent referral to Otorhinolaryngology services. Early referral is crucial for establishing the diagnosis so patients can receive definitive treatment promptly.

### Recommendation 1

- Patients presenting with any of the following symptoms should be referred to Otorhinolaryngologists as soon as possible to rule out nasopharyngeal carcinoma:
  - Painless neck lump (unilateral/bilateral)
  - Blood-stained nasal discharge/saliva
  - Unilateral ear block or hearing loss
  - Headache
  - Facial numbness
  - Diplopia
