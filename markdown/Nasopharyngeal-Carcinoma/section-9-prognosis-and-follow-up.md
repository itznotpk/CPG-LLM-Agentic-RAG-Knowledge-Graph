> **Context:** Survival statistics for NPC and the recommended follow-up schedule and evaluation.

## 9.1 Prognosis

The outcome of multimodality treatment regimen has improved survival, though outcomes vary significantly by stage at presentation. Different prognostic categories (based on the difference in failure patterns) can be defined across different stages, as these groupings have important implications for the selection of appropriate treatment strategies.

### Table 2: Prognosis of Different NPC Stages

| Stage (TNM) | Prognosis |
|---|---|
| **T1-2 N0-1** | Relatively good treatment outcome |
| **T3-4 N0-1** | Mainly local failure |
| **T1-2 N2-3** | Mainly regional and distant failure |
| **T3-4 N2-3** | Local, regional and distant failure |

### Poor Prognostic Factors
*Evidence: Level II-3, Level III*
- Male gender (p<0.05) — *The 5-year Locoregional Free Survival (LRFS) for males is 33.3%*
- Tumour with lymph node involvement (N-stage, p<0.05) — *The 5-year LRFS for N1 patients is 35.0%*
- Overall advanced stages

### Patterns of Failure
- **Distant metastasis** is the most common mode of failure in NPC, followed by local recurrence.
- While a small percentage of locally recurrent NPC can be salvaged, the vast majority of distant metastasis succumbs to the disease.
- **Survivability Note:** Patients with non-disseminated NPC (6.88%) can survive two years or more after distant metastasis is diagnosed. [Level II-3]

## 9.2 Follow-up

Radiotherapy acute toxicities usually take about one to two months to resolve and tumour will regress maximally within two to three months. Hence, patients need to be reviewed post-radiotherapy to assess acute toxicities and manage accordingly.

### Aims of Follow-up
The aims of following-up patients after NPC treatment are:
- To assess the response to treatments.
- To manage side effects and complications which may arise due to the disease process or from the treatment [Level III] *(Refer to Section 8: Management of Complications)*.
- To provide surveillance and early detection of locoregional relapses, which are amenable to radical salvage treatment [Level III].
- To detect the occurrence of a second primary cancer.
  - There is a **24% increased risk** in the development of a second cancer after NPC as compared with the general population (standardised incidence rate=1.24, 95% CI 1.15 to 1.33). [Level III]

> **Clinical Note:** The average interval between the occurrence of the first and the second cancers is **5.33 years**. The second primary cancers are typically oral/pharyngeal cancer, head and neck sarcoma, skin cancer, and salivary gland cancer. [Level III]

### Follow-up Procedures
The CPG DG and RC suggest the following procedures to be conducted on NPC patients during follow-up:
- Clinical examination of the nasopharynx including an nasoendoscopic examination to detect superficial tumours.
- Examination of the neck and other systems (thorax and abdomen).
- If post-radiotherapy cross-sectional imaging is required, it should be offered **no earlier than 3 months**.
- Cross-sectional imaging, as needed, to detect deep infiltrating tumours not associated with mucosal lesion during the initial 3–5 years post-treatment.

### Table 3: Follow-up Schedule of NPC Without Recurrence

There is no standard local follow-up schedule for NPC patients. However, the CPG DG and RC suggest the following schedule:

| Year after completion of treatment | Frequency of follow-up |
|---|---|
| **First year** | Every 1 to 2 months |
| **Second year** | Every 2 to 3 months |
| **Third year** | Every 3 to 5 months |
| **Fourth to fifth year** | Every 6 months |
| **After fifth year** | Every 6 to 12 months |

*\*Interval of follow-up may be adjusted based on clinical judgement.*
