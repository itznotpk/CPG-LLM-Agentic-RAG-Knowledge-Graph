> **Context:** Primary treatment guidelines for newly diagnosed, recurrent, and advanced NPC, focusing on radiotherapy and chemotherapy techniques.

Multidisciplinary team approach in the management of NPC cases is essential. The primary treatment for NPC is radiation therapy with or without chemotherapy.

## 6.1 Primary Cancer (Newly Diagnosed NPC)

NPC is highly radiosensitive, making radiation therapy (RT) the mainstay of treatment for non-disseminated cases. Chemotherapy acts as an adjunct setting (concurrent, adjuvant, or neoadjuvant).

### Radiation Therapy (RT) Modalities
- **Dose:** Radical RT doses usually consist of 66 - 70Gy in 33-35 fractions (once daily over 6-7 weeks; 5 days a week).
- **Evolution:** Conventional 2D-CRT evolved into 3D-CRT and, more recently, Intensity Modulated Radiotherapy (IMRT).

| Modality | Detail / Efficacy | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **IMRT** | Delivers higher doses to tumor cells while sparring critical structures (salivary glands, optic apparatus, spinal cord, etc). Improves local control and PFS for early/advanced stages. | Level II-2 |
| **IMRT** | Significant reduction in grade 2-4 xerostomia (HR=0.76) without compromising loco-regional control and overall survival. | Level I |

### Chemotherapy Addition Based on Stage

| Stage | Treatment Approach | Impact/Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage II** | Concurrent Chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) vs RT alone | Improves 5-year OS significantly compared to RT alone (94.5% vs 85.8%). Recommended by recent oncology guidelines. [Level I] |
| **Stage III, IVA, IVB (Locoregionally Advanced)** | Concurrent Chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) | Addition of chemotherapy yields ~20% improvement in 2- to 4-year survival. Concomitant trials show highest efficacy compared to induction/adjuvant schedules. [Level I] |

### Chemotherapy Timing Options

| Approach | Efficacy & Outcome | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Concurrent (CCRT)** | Significant benefit for 5-yr overall survival (OS). The most effective approach. | Level I |
| **Neoadjuvant (NACT)** | Shows benefit in disease-free survival (DFS) but *not* in OS or locoregional control. Requires stronger evidence. | Level I |
| **Adjuvant (AC)** | When compared with RT alone, AC+RT lowers locoregional failure by 29-39% but does not significantly improve OS. | Level I |

> **Adverse Events:** Common chemotherapy adverse events include neutropaenia, mucositis, nausea, and vomiting. *(Refer to Appendix 6: Chemotherapy Drugs and Side Effects below)*

### Recommendation 4

- Radiotherapy alone is the main treatment in Stage I nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC).
- Concurrent chemoradiotherapy should be offered in Stage II, III, IVA and IVB NPC.
- Intensity modulated radiotherapy is the preferred radiation technique in NPC.

## 6.2 Recurrent Cancer

Treatment for recurrent NPC depends on the clinical T stage at recurrence.

| Stage | Treatment Options | Overall Survival (5-Year) |
|---|---|---|
| **rT1 and rT2** | Endoscopic nasopharyngectomy or brachytherapy | Higher survival: rT1 (49.1% - 73%); rT2 (24.7% - 40%) |
| **rT3 and selected rT4 (including nodal recurrence)** | Conventional nasopharyngectomy, radical neck dissection, or re-irradiation | Lower survival |

### Surgical and Radiotherapy Risks for Recurrent Disease
- **Nasopharyngectomy Complications:** Palatal fistula, submandibular necrosis (transient). Permanent nerve injury (dysphagia/trismus) and massive bleeding from carotid artery injury. [Level II-3 / II-2]
- **Re-irradiation Options:** 3D-CRT, IMRT, and brachytherapy.
- **Re-irradiation Toxicity Risk:** Severe adverse effects include nasopharyngeal necrosis, cranial nerve palsy, trismus, hearing deficit, and temporal lobe necrosis. [Level II-2, Level III]

*Significant poor prognostic factors for post-nasopharyngectomy include advanced T stage, lymph node metastasis, skull base or parapharyngeal invasion, and positive surgical margins.*

### Recommendation 5

- In recurrent nasopharyngeal carcinoma, nasopharyngectomy or re-irradiation may be offered.

## 6.3 Advanced Disease (Distant Metastasis)

| Treatment | Indication & Outcomes | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Radiotherapy / Chemotherapy** | NPC patients with distant metastasis (Stage IVC) receiving treatment show better 1-year OS than untreated. Rad dose: 70.2 - 75.6 Gy. | Level III |
| **Multimodality Treatment** | Combination of chemo, RT, ± surgery significantly increases survival rate compared to chemotherapy alone or best supportive care. | Level III |
| **Palliative Care** | Very important for comfort. Palliative chemotherapy considered for patients with good ECOG status (0-2). | Consensus |

*(Refer to Appendix 7: ECOG Performance Status below. For pain management, refer to CPG Management of Cancer Pain.)*

### Recommendation 6

- Multimodality treatment including palliative care should be considered in advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma.

## Appendix 6: Chemotherapy Drugs and Side Effects

| CHEMOTHERAPY DRUGS | SIDE EFFECTS | REMARKS |
|---|---|---|
| Cisplatin | Gastrointestinal (nausea and vomiting). Blood (anaemia, thrombocytopaenia). Renal toxicity. Electrolyte imbalance. Auditory (tinnitus). Neurology (peripheral neuropathy). Hypersensitivity reaction. | Observe for cumulative renal toxicity. Prophylactic anti-emetics and corticosteroids should be given. Obtain baseline renal function. |
| Carboplatin | Blood (anaemia, thrombocytopaenia, neutropaenia). Gastrointestinal (nausea and vomiting). Hypersensitivity reaction. Electrolyte imbalance. Hepatotoxicity. | Obtain baseline renal function, then monitor renal function at every cycle. Prophylactic anti-emetics and corticosteroids should be given. |
| Fluorouracil | Gastrointestinal (diarrhoea, stomatitis). Blood (anaemia, leucopenia). Cardiovascular (angina, MI, arrhythmia). Dermatological (alopecia, hand-foot syndrome). | Prophylactic anti-emetics and corticosteroids should be given. Use with caution in patients who are receiving radiation. |
| Docetaxel | Blood (neutropaenia, anaemia, thrombocytopaenia). Cardiovascular (fluid retention). Dermatological (alopecia, nail changes). Gastrointestinal. Hypersensitivity. | Pre- and post-treatment with corticosteroid is recommended to decrease fluid retention and hypersensitivity reaction. |

*To monitor FBC, LFT, RP and serum electrolytes prior to every cycle of chemotherapy.*

## Appendix 7: Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status

| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | Fully active, able to carry on all pre-disease performance without restriction |
| 1 | Restricted in physically strenuous activity but ambulatory and able to carry out work of a light or sedentary nature |
| 2 | Ambulatory and capable of all self-care but unable to carry out any work activities; Up and about more than 50% of waking hours |
| 3 | Capable of only limited self-care, confined to bed or chair more than 50% of waking hours |
| 4 | Completely disabled; Cannot carry on any self-care; Totally confined to bed or chair |
| 5 | Dead |
