# SECTION 18: ALGORITHMS
## CPG MANAGEMENT OF ACUTE ST ELEVATION MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION (STEMI), 4TH EDITION, 2019

The following clinical algorithms are adapted from the Guidelines for Resuscitation Training for the Ministry of Health Malaysia, March 2016.

## Algorithm 1: Cardiac Arrest — Pulseless VT/VF, Asystole, PEA

**Assess Safety → Call for Help → Start BLS**

1. **Shockable Rhythm (Pulseless VT/VF):**
   - Defibrillate immediately.
   - Resume CPR immediately for 2 minutes after defibrillation.
   - If VF/VT persists after the second shock:
     - Adrenaline 1 mg IV every 3–5 minutes.
     - Amiodarone 300 mg IV bolus; consider further dose of 150 mg IV after the fifth shock.
   - Continue cycles of 2 minutes CPR → rhythm check → defibrillate if shockable.

2. **Non-Shockable Rhythm (Asystole/PEA):**
   - Resume CPR immediately.
   - Adrenaline 1 mg IV as soon as IV access is obtained, then every 3–5 minutes.
   - Continue cycles of 2 minutes CPR → rhythm check.
   - Identify and treat reversible causes (4 H's and 4 T's):
     - **H:** Hypoxia, Hypovolaemia, Hyperkalaemia/Hypokalaemia/metabolic, Hypothermia
     - **T:** Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade (cardiac), Toxins, Thrombosis (pulmonary/coronary)

## Algorithm 2: Ventricular Tachycardia (VT) with Pulse

1. **Haemodynamically Unstable (Hypotension, Chest Pain, HF, Altered Mental Status):**
   - Synchronised cardioversion.
   - Sedation if the patient is conscious.
   - If cardioversion fails → Amiodarone 300 mg IV over 10–20 minutes, then consider repeat cardioversion.

2. **Haemodynamically Stable:**
   - Amiodarone 300 mg IV over 20–60 minutes.
   - Followed by 900 mg IV over 24 hours.
   - If VT persists → synchronised cardioversion.
   - Consider lignocaine 1–1.5 mg/kg IV if amiodarone is unsuccessful.

3. **Correct Underlying Causes:**
   - Ischaemia — consider urgent PCI or CABG.
   - Electrolyte abnormalities — correct potassium and magnesium.

## Algorithm 3: Atrial Fibrillation

1. **Haemodynamically Unstable:**
   - Synchronised cardioversion (100–200 J biphasic).
   - Sedation if the patient is conscious.

2. **Haemodynamically Stable:**
   - **Rate control:**
     - ß-blocker (e.g., metoprolol 5 mg IV over 2 minutes, may repeat to a total of 15 mg).
     - Digoxin (0.25–0.5 mg IV if LV dysfunction present).
     - Amiodarone (300 mg IV over 20–60 minutes, then 900 mg over 24 hours) — for both rate and rhythm control.
   - **Rhythm control:**
     - Amiodarone if haemodynamically impaired or LV dysfunction.
     - Cardioversion may be considered if onset < 48 hours.

3. **Anti-coagulation:**
   - Assess stroke risk using CHA₂DS₂-VASc score (see Appendix V).
   - Consider anti-coagulation with warfarin or DOAC.

## Algorithm 4: Bradycardia

1. **Assess ABC — Give oxygen if hypoxic, establish IV access, monitor ECG.**

2. **Identify and Treat Reversible Causes:**
   - Drug-induced (ß-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin).
   - Electrolyte abnormalities.
   - Inferior MI with vagal stimulation.

3. **If Symptomatic (Hypotension, Altered Consciousness, Shock, HF):**
   - Atropine 0.5 mg IV (may repeat every 3–5 minutes to a maximum of 3 mg).
   - If unresponsive to atropine:
     - Transcutaneous pacing.
     - Adrenaline infusion 2–10 µg/minute.
     - Consider isoprenaline infusion.
   - Arrange for transvenous temporary pacing if indicated.

4. **If Asymptomatic:**
   - Observe and monitor.
   - No urgent treatment required for first degree AV block or Mobitz type 1 (Wenckebach).

**Note:** In anterior MI with Mobitz type 2 or complete AV block — temporary pacing should be considered even if the patient is haemodynamically stable, as there is a risk of sudden deterioration.
