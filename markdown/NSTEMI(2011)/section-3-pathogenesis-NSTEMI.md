> **Context:** This section details the biological cascade of ACS, from vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque rupture and thrombosis through to the resulting degree of coronary occlusion and specific final clinical presentation.

ACS occurs due to atherosclerotic plaque rupture, fissure or ulceration with superimposed thrombosis and coronary vasospasm. Depending on the acuteness, degree of occlusion and the presence of collaterals, patients can present as UA, NSTEMI or STEMI.

The aetiology of the plaque fissure or rupture is still unclear. Possible causes include inflammation, infection, uncontrolled blood pressure and smoking. ACS occurring de novo is called Primary UA/NSTEMI.

Occasionally UA/NSTEMI is secondary to a precipitating condition, which is extrinsic to the coronary arterial bed. This is called secondary UA/NSTEMI and can occur due to:
- increased myocardial oxygen demand as occurring in fever, tachycardia and thyrotoxicosis
- reduced myocardial oxygen delivery in anaemia or hypoxemia
- reduced coronary blood flow due to hypotension

Secondary UA/NSTEMI is an important cause of ACS in the elderly.
