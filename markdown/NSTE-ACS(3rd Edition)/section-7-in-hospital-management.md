# SECTION 7: IN-HOSPITAL MANAGEMENT
## CPG MANAGEMENT OF NON-ST ELEVATION MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION (NSTE-ACS), 3RD EDITION, 2021

---

## 7.1 Emergency Department

Patients with NSTE-ACS should be given:
- **Aspirin 300 mg stat** (if not given earlier). [Grade I, Level A]
- **Oxygen** if oxygen saturation < 95%. [Grade I, Level C]
- **GTN** (as a tablet, spray or intravenous infusion) for ongoing or recurrent chest pains. [Grade I, Level C]
- **Intravenous morphine** with i.v. anti-emetics or i.v. fentanyl for severe chest pains. [Grade IIa, Level B]
- **Heparin** (i.v. infusion unfractionated or s.c. LMWH) or s.c. fondaparinux. [Grade I, Level A]

In the presence of ongoing chest pains and/or haemodynamic instability, urgent coronary angiography with view to revascularization should be considered.

In patients with recurrent/ongoing chest pain not due to ACS, other important clinical conditions should also be considered.

---

## 7.2 Level of Care

Patients with NSTE-ACS should ideally be admitted to a monitored unit:

- **Very-high-risk and high-risk patients** should be admitted to a coronary care unit (CCU) or equivalent monitored bed.
- **Intermediate-risk patients** may be monitored in a step-down unit.
- **Low-risk patients** may be observed in the ED observation unit or discharged with early outpatient follow-up.

### 7.3.1 Antiplatelet Therapy

#### 7.3.1.1 Acetylsalicylic Acid (ASA/Aspirin)

- Aspirin should be given at the time of diagnosis. [Grade I, Level A]
- Loading dose: 300 mg (non-enteric coated, chewed).
- Maintenance dose: 75–150 mg daily, lifelong.

#### 7.3.1.2 P2Y12 Inhibitors (Appendix VII)

**7.3.1.2.1 Clopidogrel**
- Loading dose: 300–600 mg.
- Maintenance dose: 75 mg daily.
- Should be given as part of DAPT for at least 1 year. [Grade I, Level A]

**7.3.1.2.2 Prasugrel**
- Loading dose: 60 mg.
- Maintenance dose: 10 mg daily.
- Should be given **after** the coronary angiogram before proceeding to PCI. [Grade I, Level B]
- Contraindicated in patients with prior stroke/TIA.
- Caution in patients ≥ 75 years and body weight < 60 kg (reduced maintenance dose of 5 mg daily may be considered).

**7.3.1.2.3 Ticagrelor**
- Loading dose: 180 mg.
- Maintenance dose: 90 mg twice daily.
- Can be administered early. [Grade I, Level B]
- No dose adjustment required in renal impairment.

#### 7.3.1.3 Timing of Initiation of DAPT

- No firm recommendations can be made about pre-treatment.
- Clopidogrel and ticagrelor, in general, can be administered early.
- Prasugrel should be given after the coronary angiogram before proceeding to PCI.

#### 7.3.1.4 Duration of DAPT

- Ideally all patients should be given DAPT for 1 year. [Grade I, Level A]
- Patients with high bleeding risk can be given DAPT for a shorter period of 3–6 months. [Grade IIa, Level A]
- The duration of DAPT will depend on the risk of bleeding versus the thrombotic risk.
- The PRECISE-DAPT score helps identify patients who may benefit from shorter DAPT duration.

#### 7.3.1.5 Switching a Potent P2Y12 to Clopidogrel

- Switching from a potent P2Y12 inhibitor (ticagrelor or prasugrel) to clopidogrel may be considered in specific clinical situations (e.g., cost, side effects, bleeding risk).

#### 7.3.1.6 Glycoprotein (GP) IIb/IIIa Inhibitors (Appendix VIII)

- GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors may be considered as bailout therapy in patients undergoing PCI with high thrombotic burden or procedural complications. [Grade IIa, Level C]
- Routine upstream use is not recommended. [Grade III, Level A]

---

### 7.3.2 Anticoagulant Therapy (Appendix IX)

#### 7.3.2.1 Heparin

**Unfractionated Heparin (UFH):**
- i.v. infusion with APTT-guided dosing. [Grade I, Level A]
- In medically treated patients, given for 2–8 days or until hospital discharge.

**Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH):**
- Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg s.c. twice daily. [Grade I, Level A]
- Dose adjustment required in renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min: 1 mg/kg once daily).

#### 7.3.2.2 Anti-Xa Inhibitors

**Fondaparinux:**
- 2.5 mg s.c. once daily. [Grade I, Level A]
- Has the most favourable efficacy-safety profile among anticoagulants.
- Requires additional UFH bolus at the time of PCI to prevent catheter-related thrombosis.

---

### 7.3.3 Anti-Ischaemic Drug Therapy

#### 7.3.3.1 ß-blockers (Appendix X)

- In patients who have angina/ischaemia, ß-blockers should be prescribed as first-line treatment. [Grade I, Level A]
- Consider long-term therapy (> 1 year) for patients with LVEF ≤ 40%. [Grade I, Level A]
- Routine administration (> 1 year) in all patients post NSTE-ACS with no angina/ischaemia and normal LV function. [Grade IIb, Level B]
- Initial dose: carvedilol 3.125 mg twice daily should be initiated. This should be slowly up-titrated till the target dose or the maximum tolerated dose is achieved.

#### 7.3.3.2 Inhibitors of the Renin Angiotensin System — ACEIs/ARBs

- Started on first day and continued long-term (> 1 year) for patients with LVEF ≤ 40%, anterior infarcts and diabetes. [Grade I, Level A]
- Routine administration in all patients post NSTE-ACS > 1 year. [Grade IIb, Level B]

#### 7.3.3.3 Lipid Modifying Drugs

- High dose statins should be initiated soon after diagnosis. [Grade I, Level A]
- Aim for LDL-C target of < 1.8 mmol/L (preferably < 1.4 mmol/L) or a 50% reduction in LDL-C levels from baseline, the lower the better.

#### 7.3.3.4 Nitrates (Appendix XII)

- GTN for ongoing or recurrent chest pains. [Grade I, Level C]
- Long-acting nitrates are recommended as add-on therapy in patients who remain symptomatic. [Grade IIa, Level B]

#### 7.3.3.5 Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs) (Appendix XIII)

- Non-dihydropyridine CCBs should be prescribed as first-line treatment in patients who have a contraindication to or intolerance of ß-blockers. [Grade I, Level A]

#### 7.3.3.6 Other Anti-Ischaemic Agents

- **Ivabradine** — May be considered in patients with normal sinus rhythm, especially those with a contraindication to or intolerance to ß-blockers and if the resting HR is above 70/min. [Grade IIa, Level B]
- **Ranolazine** — Improved angina symptoms, exercise tolerance, and decreased angina attacks and GTN consumption. [Grade IIa, Level B]
- **Trimetazidine** — Effective in providing angina symptom relief, reduction in the need for nitrates, and improving functional capacity. Useful as monotherapy and in combination. [Grade IIa, Level B]

## 7.4 Revascularization Strategies

There is a strong rationale for early revascularization in patients with NSTE-ACS who are at intermediate/high risk and very high risk for MACE. [Grade I, Level B]

The indication for coronary angiography and the timing for myocardial revascularization depends on: clinical presentation, risk scores, comorbidities, presence of high-risk features, frailty, cognitive status, estimated life expectancy, and functional and anatomic severity of the underlying CAD.

### 7.4.1 Routine Early Invasive Management

The rationale is to: confirm diagnosis, rapidly risk stratify, allow for earlier revascularization, and facilitate early discharge. [Grade I, Level B]

It has been shown to improve clinical outcomes, reduce recurrent ACS events, subsequent rehospitalisation and revascularization. However, no reduction in mortality has been observed.

### 7.4.2 Urgent vs Early Invasive Management

| Strategy | Timing | Indication | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Urgent invasive** | Immediate (<2 h) | Very-high-risk criteria | I, A |
| **Early invasive** | Within 24 h | At least one high-risk criterion | I, A |
| **Invasive** | Within 72 h | Intermediate-risk criteria | I, A |
| **Non-invasive assessment** | — | Low-risk patients | I, A |

### 7.4.3 Routine Early Conservative Management (Selective Invasive Therapy)

- This strategy can be advocated at non-PCI capable centres, in the elderly, frail patients, and patients with comorbidities. [Grade I, Level C]
- A conservative strategy is recommended for women who are stabilised and remain biomarker negative. [Grade IIa, Level A]
- An early invasive or conservative strategy are both reasonable for men who are stabilised and biomarker negative. [Grade IIa, Level A]

### Fractional Flow Reserve (FFR) Guidance

The PCI of non-culprit lesions especially if they are between 50–70%, should be guided by FFR measurement:
- FFR < 0.8 — intervene
- FFR > 0.8 — medical therapy

Wherever possible, patients who have undergone intervention should undergo complete revascularization either at the same sitting or as a staged procedure.
