## 2.1: Screening

[Level 2+] All adults should be screened **annually** using body mass index (BMI) measurement; a cut-off point of **≥23 kg/m²** should be used to initiate further evaluation of overweight or obesity.

However, measurement of body fat can be used as an alternative to BMI for screening for obesity. Truncal obesity can be an indicator of obesity-associated conditions, especially in those with lower BMI.

> **[Grade B]** Screening with BMI and WC measurements should be done yearly.

## 2.2: Diagnosis

The BMI is an international standard for measurement of obesity as recommended by the WHO; however, it was based on Europids and did not accurately reflect other ethnic groups. In the Asian population, morbidity and mortality occur at lower BMI and waist circumference.

**BMI Formula:**
**BMI (kg/m²) = Weight (kg) ÷ Height² (m²)**

[Level 1++] Asians generally have higher body fat percentages compared to Caucasians at similar BMI cut-off points.

[Level 1+] Evidence from Asian countries reveals that the risk of comorbidities such as T2DM, hypertension, and other cardiovascular (CV) risks, begin to increase at lower BMI. Hence, **lower BMI cut-off points** are used for overweight and obesity in the Asian population.

### BMI Classification For Adults (>18 Years Old)

**Table 2-1. BMI-Based Weight Classification For Adults (>18 Years Old)**

| Classification | BMI (kg/m²) | Risk of Comorbidities |
|---|---|---|
| Underweight | <18.5 | Low, but with increased risk of other clinical problems |
| Normal | 18.5–22.9 | Optimal |
| Pre-obese (Overweight) | 23.0–27.4 | Increased |
| Obese I | 27.5–32.4 | High |
| Obese II | 32.5–37.4 | Very high |
| Obese III | ≥37.5 | Extremely high |

> **[Grade A]** Cut-off BMI values that should be used are: pre-obesity (overweight) — 23 kg/m² and obesity — >27.5 kg/m²

### Limitations Of BMI

[Level 3] BMI cannot distinguish between muscle and fat weight. [Level 2+] The elderly tends to have higher body fat composition as a result of age-related loss of lean body mass (sarcopenia), which may be misclassified as healthy BMI — use of BMI for health risk assessment may be less accurate in this subgroup.

[Level 3] BMI also does not distinguish body fat distribution — a known determinant of metabolic risk. Assessment of **visceral adiposity** is a better parameter than total body fat alone as a predictor for T2DM, hypertension, metabolic syndrome, and CV mortality risk.

### Waist Circumference (WC) Classification

Abdominal or visceral fat is an independent risk factor for cardiovascular disease (CVD). WC is measured in the horizontal plane, **midway between the lowest ribs and the iliac crest**.

[Level 2+] WC measurement correlates well with abdominal fat content irrespective of BMI.

**Measuring WC correctly:**
- Position the tape measure at the midpoint between the lowest palpable rib and the iliac crest
- Measure horizontally at minimal respiration
- Record to the nearest 0.1 cm

The WHO recommends WC of ≥102 cm in men and ≥88 cm in women. However, [Level 2+] studies from Asian countries showed high prevalence of T2DM and CVD at lower cut-off points:

| Sex | Asian WC Cut-Off (Abnormal) |
|---|---|
| Men | ≥ 90 cm |
| Women | ≥ 80 cm |

[Level 2+] WC measurement is **most useful** in individuals in the normal and overweight (pre-obesity) BMI categories. For those with BMI >35 kg/m², it is **unnecessary** to measure WC as it loses its predictive value.

Other methods for measuring body fat (less practical in clinical settings):
- Skin-fold caliper measurement
- Hydrodensitometry
- Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA)
- Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)
- Bioelectrical impedance (BEI)

## 2.3: Assessment Of Obesity-Related Medical Conditions

Patients who are pre-obese and above should also be assessed for the following conditions:
- Hypertension
- T2DM and Prediabetes
- Metabolic syndrome
- Dyslipidaemia
- Obstructive Sleep Apnoea (OSA)
- Osteoarthritis/Degenerative joints/back pain
- Metabolic Associated Fatty Liver Disease (MAFLD)
- Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease
- Cancer

> **[Grade A]** Individuals diagnosed with pre-obesity and obesity should be assessed for associated conditions, co-occurring medical conditions, and current treatments that could cause obesity.

## 2.4: Targets For Control

[Level 1] The principal goals in obesity management are to:
- Prevent complications by trying to keep the patient metabolically healthy (if possible)
- Prevent or treat existing comorbidities
- Fight against stigmatisation of people with obesity
- Restore the individual's well-being, positive body image, and self-esteem

[Level 1] Treatment goals should be tailored to the complications and the expected weight loss ranges from 5–15%.

**Table: Treatment Targets Based On Diagnosis**

| Diagnosis | Weight Loss Target (%) | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Metabolic syndrome | 10 | Prevention of T2DM |
| T2DM | 5–15 | ↓ HbA1c; glucose-lowering drug reduction; diabetes remission if short duration |
| Dyslipidaemia | 5–15 | ↓ TG and LDL; ↑ HDL |
| Hypertension | 5–15 | ↓ BP and medications |
| PCOS | 5–15 | Ovulation; ↓ hirsutism and androgen levels; ↑ insulin sensitivity |
| Asthma | 7–8 | Improvement of FEV1 |
| Sleep apnoea | 7–11 | ↓ apnoea/hypopnoea index |
| MAFLD | 10–40 | ↓ intrahepatocellular lipids and inflammation |
