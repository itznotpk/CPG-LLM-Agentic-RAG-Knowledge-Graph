## 4.2: Pharmacotherapy

[Level 1++] When used as an adjunct to lifestyle intervention (diet, exercise, and behavioural modification), pharmacotherapy is effective in reducing weight by **at least 5%**.

[Level 1++] Pharmacotherapy should be considered when weight loss goals or weight maintenance **cannot be achieved with lifestyle interventions alone**. Anti-obesity treatments must only be used as an **adjunct** to lifestyle interventions — never alone.

A patient may require pharmacotherapy to:
1. Aid compliance with dietary restriction
2. Augment diet-related weight loss
3. Achieve weight maintenance after satisfactory weight loss

### Indications For Anti-Obesity Treatment

**Table: Indications For Anti-Obesity Treatments**

| Indication | Associated Risk Factors |
|---|---|
| **BMI 27.0–29.9 kg/m² with associated risk factors** | T2DM; Hypertension; Dyslipidaemia; OSA |
| **BMI ≥30 kg/m² with or without risk factors** | — |

> **[Grade A]** Pharmacotherapy for obesity is indicated in patients with BMI ≥30 kg/m² without comorbidities and BMI ≥27 kg/m² with comorbidity.

### General Pharmacotherapy Principles

[Level 1++] As obesity is a chronic disease, pharmacotherapy should be considered **long-term**. Short courses (≤6 months) do not result in sustained weight loss after medication discontinuation — once treatment ceases, **rebound weight gain** will occur. [Level 1+]

[Level 1++] A weight loss of **≤5%** is considered a clinically important outcome with positive impact on CV risk factors.

- If a patient does not respond with **>5% weight loss after 3 months** of initiating anti-obesity treatment, or if there are significant safety or tolerability issues, the drug should be discontinued and alternative medications or treatment approaches should be considered. [Level 4]

**Monitoring frequency:** [Level 4]
- Monthly for the first 3 months
- At least every 3 months thereafter

[Level 1++] Pharmacotherapy clinically leads to improvement in weight-related quality of life and physical functioning.

> **[Grade A]** Pharmacotherapy for obesity should be used only as an **adjunct** to diet, exercise, and behavioural modification — not alone.
> **[Grade B]** Anti-obesity treatments should be used with medical supervision and careful monitoring.

### Drugs Available In Malaysia

In Malaysia, the anti-obesity drugs available are: **orlistat, phentermine, high dose liraglutide, and semaglutide.**

Note: Phentermine, while not USFDA-approved for long-term management, is registered in Malaysia and should be used only for a duration of **3 months** and in a cyclical manner.

## 4.2.1: Anti-Obesity Drugs

### 1. Oral Orlistat (GI System — Lipase Inhibitor)

Orlistat is the only **non-systemically acting drug** available for long-term treatment. It is a selective pancreatic lipase inhibitor that inhibits the breakdown of dietary triglycerides into absorbable free fatty acids. As a result, ~30% of ingested triglycerides are excreted in the stools, creating a caloric deficit.

**Dose:** 120 mg TDS (with meals)

**Efficacy vs placebo:** [Level 1++]
- OR 2.69 for 5% weight loss
- OR 2.41 for 10% weight loss
- Mean weight loss in excess of placebo: **2.6 kg**
- % weight loss from baseline: 2.9–3.4%

[Level 1+] At 3–4 years of treatment, Orlistat together with lifestyle intervention or VLCD significantly reduced weight and maintained weight loss with minimal side effects.

[Level 1++] Orlistat also causes reduction in BP and glucose levels.

[Level 1+] Improvement in lipid profile (total cholesterol, LDL-C, triglycerides, and HDL-C).

**Side effects:**
- Abdominal discomfort (borborygmi and cramps)
- Liquid or oily stool
- Faecal incontinence and oily spotting
- Flatulence with discharge
- Reduced absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) — multi-vitamin replacements if used >12 months
- Mildly raised liver transaminase
- Increased urinary oxalate that might cause acute oxalate nephropathy
- Drug interactions: affects absorption of levothyroxine, cyclosporine, anticonvulsants, and antiretroviral drugs

**Contraindications:** Malabsorption syndromes, cholestasis, pregnancy/breastfeeding, concurrent use of cyclosporine, thyroxine, warfarin, anticonvulsants.

---

### 2. Oral Phentermine (CNS — Sympathomimetic Amine)

Noradrenergic sympathomimetic amine (dimethylphenethylamine hydrochloride). Approved for **short-term use only** (not more than 3 months, cyclical manner).

**Dose:** 15–30 mg OD

**Efficacy vs placebo:**
- [Level 1++] Mean weight loss: 4.43 kg (SMD 1.37, 95% CI –1.55, –1.19)
- [Level 1+] 86.5% of patients achieved 5% weight loss
- [Level 1+] 48.6% of patients achieved 10% weight loss

**Additional benefits:** [Level 1+] Reduced total cholesterol and LDL-C

**Side effects:** [Level 1+]
- Insomnia
- Palpitations
- [Level 1++] Increased heart rate (SMD=0.64; 95% CI 0.35, 0.92)
- Dry mouth

**Warnings:** Only indicated for short-term use (3 months); can be used cyclically; caution with poorly controlled BP and CAD; rare cases of pulmonary hypertension and regurgitant valvular disease; risk of abuse and dependence.

**Contraindications:** Pregnancy/breastfeeding, anxiety disorders, uncontrolled hypertension, seizures, hyperthyroidism, history of CVD (CAD, stroke, arrhythmias, heart failure).

---

### 3. Oral Phentermine/Topiramate Combination (CNS)

Phentermine releases norepinephrine; topiramate is a GABA receptor modulator (anticonvulsant). In combination, they increase energy expenditure, reduce energy efficiency and caloric intake.

**Dosing:** Starting: 3.75/23 mg OD → Recommended: 7.5/46 mg OD → High dose: 15/92 mg OD

**Efficacy vs placebo:** [Level 1++]
- OR 9.1 for 5% weight loss
- OR 11.3 for 10% weight loss
- Mean weight loss in excess of placebo: **8.8 kg**
- % weight loss from baseline: 8–11%

**Additional benefits:** [Level 1++] Reduction in BP (at higher dose), reduction in glucose and lipid levels.

**Side effects:** [Level 1++]
- Most common: paraesthesia, dry mouth, constipation, dysgeusia, insomnia
- Mood-related (depression, anxiety, irritability) and cognition-related disturbances at higher doses — use with caution in patients with depressive or anxiety disorders
- Non-significant increase in resting heart rate at high doses
- **Teratogenic (cleft lip/palate) — Pregnancy Category D**

**Contraindications:** Pregnancy (teratogenic), breastfeeding, hyperthyroidism, glaucoma, concurrent use with MAO inhibitors.

---

### 4. Oral Naltrexone/Bupropion Combination (CNS)

Naltrexone (opioid antagonist) blocks opioid-mediated POMC auto-inhibition; bupropion stimulates hypothalamic POMC neurons to secrete α-MSH, mediating anorectic effects.

**Dose:** High dose: 32/360 mg (2 tablets) QID

**Efficacy vs placebo:** [Level 1++]
- OR 3.9 for 5% weight loss
- OR 4.11 for 10% weight loss
- Mean weight loss in excess of placebo: **4.95 kg**
- % weight loss from baseline: 5–6%

**Additional benefits:** [Level 1++]
- Reduction in glucose levels and insulin resistance
- Reduction in requirement for glucose-lowering drugs in T2DM
- Reduction of lipid levels

**Side effects:** [Level 1++]
- Most common: nausea
- No increase in depression or suicidal ideations in trials (however, patients with serious psychiatric illnesses were excluded — use with caution in patients with known depression)
- Adverse effects on BP and heart rate

**Warnings:** Caution in patients with depression; boxed warning for suicidal behaviour and ideation; risk of seizure; careful monitoring of BP/HR; hepatotoxicity.

**Contraindications:** Pregnancy, uncontrolled hypertension, seizure disorders, anorexia nervosa or bulimia, drug/alcohol withdrawal, MAO inhibitors, chronic opioid use, glaucoma.

---

### 5. Subcutaneous Liraglutide 3 mg Daily (GLP1-RA)

Liraglutide is a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP1-RA) that improves glycaemic control by stimulating insulin secretion, suppressing glucagon secretion, and **reducing caloric intake by suppressing appetite**.

Doses up to 1.8 mg daily are approved for T2DM; **doses up to 3 mg daily are used for managing obesity**.

**Efficacy vs placebo:** [Level 1++]
- OR 5.09 for 5% weight loss
- OR 4.36 for 10% weight loss
- Mean weight loss in excess of placebo: **5.24 kg**
- % weight loss from baseline: 6–7%

[Level 1++] Liraglutide 3 mg demonstrated greater efficacy in weight loss compared to orlistat in a head-to-head RCT.

**Additional benefits:** [Level 1++]
- Reduction in HbA1c
- Reducing use of oral glucose-lowering drugs in individuals with T2DM
- BP reduction
- Reducing insulin resistance and lipid levels

[Level 1++] Liraglutide 1.8 mg daily has shown improvement in CV outcomes in patients with T2DM.

[Level 1-] Beneficial impact on MAFLD.

**Side effects:** [Level 1++]
- Most common: GI side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation)
- Increase in resting heart rate

**Warnings:** Risk of thyroid C-cell tumours (contraindicated in MTC/MEN2); warnings for acute pancreatitis/gall-bladder disease; hypoglycaemia in combination with insulin/secretagogues; post-marketing reports of acute kidney injury.

**Contraindications:** Caution in renal impairment; pregnancy; personal or family history of MTC or MEN2.

---

### 6. Subcutaneous Semaglutide 2.4 mg Weekly (GLP1-RA)

Semaglutide is a GLP1-RA. Doses up to 1 mg weekly for T2DM; **doses up to 2.4 mg weekly for managing obesity**.

**Dosing:** Starting: 0.25 mg weekly → increase every 4 weeks over 16 weeks → 2.4 mg weekly

**Efficacy vs placebo:** [Level 1++]
- OR 11.2 for 5% weight loss
- OR 14.7 for 10% weight loss
- OR 19.3 for 15% weight loss
- OR 26.9 for 20% weight loss
- Mean weight loss in excess of placebo: **12.7 kg**
- % weight loss from baseline: 14.9%

**Additional benefits:** [Level 1++]
- Reduction of HbA1c
- Lowering BP
- Reducing lipid levels

[Level 1++] Semaglutide 0.5–1.0 mg weekly improved CV and renal outcomes in T2DM patients.

[Level 1-] Low dose semaglutide also has beneficial impact on MAFLD.

**Side effects:**
- Most common: GI side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation)
- Very rare: acute pancreatitis and cholelithiasis
- [Level 1++] Transient worsening of retinopathy in T2DM patients with baseline retinopathy — patients with obesity and T2DM with retinopathy should be monitored closely and co-managed with an ophthalmologist

**Contraindications:** eGFR <15 mL/min; pregnancy; personal or family history of MTC or MEN2.

### Summary Table: Pharmacotherapy For Obesity

| Drug | Dose | Weight Loss vs Lifestyle | Most Common Side Effects | Key Contraindications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phentermine | 15–30 mg OD | 3.6 kg | Headache, ↑BP, palpitations, insomnia, dry mouth | Pregnancy; uncontrolled HTN; CVD; seizures; anxiety disorders |
| Orlistat | 120 mg TDS | 2.63 kg (2.9–3.4% baseline) | Oily stool, liquid stool, flatulence, oil leakage | Pregnancy; malabsorption; cholestasis; drug interactions |
| Phentermine/topiramate | 7.5/46–15/92 mg OD | 8.8 kg (8–11% baseline) | Paraesthesia, dry mouth, constipation, dysgeusia, insomnia | Pregnancy (Class D); hyperthyroidism; glaucoma; MAO inhibitors |
| Naltrexone/bupropion | 32/360 mg QID | 4.95 kg (5–6% baseline) | Nausea, constipation, headache | Pregnancy; seizures; anorexia/bulimia; opioid use; MAO inhibitors |
| Liraglutide (SC) | 3 mg daily | 5.24 kg (6–7% baseline) | Nausea, vomiting | Pregnancy; MTC/MEN2; renal impairment |
| Semaglutide (SC) | 2.4 mg weekly | 12.7 kg (14.9% baseline) | Nausea, vomiting | eGFR <15; Pregnancy; MTC/MEN2 |

**Key learning points:**
- The 3 most effective medications for weight loss are: **high-dose semaglutide > combination phentermine/topiramate > high-dose liraglutide**
- Phentermine monotherapy: short-term only (≤3 months), cyclical
- GLP1-RAs (liraglutide and semaglutide) have the added benefit of improving glycaemic control, MAFLD, and cardiorenal outcomes in patients with both T2DM and obesity
