## 3.1: Psychological Aspect Of Obesity

Persons with overweight and obesity struggle with various mental health issues such as **weight bias, stigma, and discrimination** — negative attitudes, beliefs, and behaviours including prejudice and stereotype labelling such as being lazy, unmotivated, lacking willpower and discipline.

[Level 1+] Stigmatising being overweight and obese is not helpful as it is associated with reduced motivation to engage in healthy behaviours, avoidance of exercise, non-adherence to diet and medication, body image dissatisfaction, reduced quality of life, low self-esteem, and poor overall wellbeing.

[Level 2+] In addition to poor health outcomes, weight discrimination may shorten life expectancy.

[Level 1-] A recent systematic review found that obesity is associated with:
- Depressive disorders
- Anxiety disorders (OR 1.27–1.40)
- Personality disorders (OR 1.2–1.95)
- Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (OR 1.44)
- Eating disorders (OR 4.5)

There is a **bidirectional relationship** between obesity and depression: people with obesity have a higher risk of developing depression (OR 1.21–5.8), while people with depression have a higher risk of becoming obese (OR 1.18–3.76), with a stronger association observed in women.

**Clinical Responsibilities:**
- Healthcare providers should identify if patients with obesity have pre-existing mental health issues and refer to a psychiatrist for diagnosis and optimisation
- Screen for binge eating disorder using the **Binge Eating Scale (BES)** tool
- The patient's psychosocial comorbidities should be reviewed during each visit

## 3.2: Psychological And Behavioural Interventions

[Level 1++] Psychological and behavioural interventions for overweight and obesity aim to help people with obesity sustain lifestyle changes that improve self-esteem, health, functioning, and quality of life.

[Level 1++] The majority of these interventions are cognitive-based and are typically used in combination with lifestyle interventions. When behaviour therapy is combined with diet and exercise, it results in greater weight reduction compared to diet or exercise alone (WMD –2.3 kg, 95% CI –1.4, –3.3).

[Level 1++] Increasing the intensity of the behavioural intervention further increases weight reduction. These types of multicomponent approaches incorporated by trained health providers are more effective in the long term compared to a single component approach.

### Multicomponent Approaches

**i. Enhancing Communication And Avoidance Of Stigmatisation**

[Level 1++] Communication is vital in obesity management. Approaches may include:
- Communicating with empathy without negative judgements and bias
- Recognising that obesity has a complex multifactorial aetiology and is not fully under voluntary control
- Asking the individual first if they are willing to speak about their weight before introducing the discussion
- Acknowledging that an individual with obesity has already been chronically exposed to negative experiences when navigating the health system
- Avoiding the use of inappropriate or hurtful words to maintain a positive therapeutic relationship
- Referring to the individual as a "person with obesity" rather than "an obese person"

**ii. Psychoeducation**

[Level 1++] When educating patients, the emphasis is to work on achievable behavioural and psychological goals to achieve improved health, function, and quality of life — not just on the amount of weight loss.

**iii. Motivational Interviewing**

[Level 1++] Motivational interviewing is a collaborative, goal-oriented, and patient-centred guided-style counselling method depending on where the patient is on the Stages of Change (precontemplation → contemplation → preparation → action → maintenance → relapse).

Basic steps:
- **Engagement** — listen and understand the patient's background, perspective, concerns, past experiences, strengths, and vulnerabilities
- **Focus** — focus on one behaviour at a time
- **Evoke** — evoke the patient's internal motivation by connecting healthy behavioural change to their goals, values, and concerns
- **Plan** — once the patient is ready to change, make a joint decision to address the specific situation(s) where behavioural change is needed

**iv. Behavioural Strategies**

[Level 1++] Behavioural strategies improve adherence to lifestyle intervention programmes in adults with obesity. Healthcare providers can be trained to effectively implement the wide range of behavioural interventions available.

**Behavioural and cognitive interventions include:**

- **Goal setting and action planning (SMART goals):** Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic and Relevant, Time-based. Break goals into small, manageable documented steps.

- **Self-monitoring:** Monitoring of diet, exercise, and mood to increase self-awareness; feedback enhances motivation.

- **Stimulus control:** Techniques to modify the external environment to make it conducive for supporting behavioural changes.

- **Problem solving (IDEAL approach):**
  - **I**dentify problems and consider them as opportunities to develop solutions
  - **D**efine value-based goals in problematic situations
  - **E**xplore possible strategies to achieve goals
  - **A**nticipate outcomes and **A**ct on the strategies
  - **L**ook back at the effects and **L**earn from experience

- **Cognitive reframing:** Technique to see a situation in a different, more rational perspective. Example: "I only lost 1 kg this month. This is hopeless" → "I successfully lost 1 kg this month. Even though the amount is small, I also lost an inch off my waist, and I feel energised. This is still an achievement."

- **Cognitive restructuring:** Used under therapist's guidance — involves noticing irrational thought patterns, questioning assumptions, gathering evidence, analysing evidence, and generating rational alternative explanations.

- **Relapse prevention and management:** Early identification of high-risk situations and lapses; devising a plan to overcome high-risk situations and adapting to new coping mechanisms. Relapses are common in weight management.

**v. Psychological Interventions**

[Level 1] Psychological interventions such as **Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)** and **Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)** are effective. ACT focuses on value-directed actions and commitment to multicomponent behavioural interventions.

[Level 1++] CBT when combined with a diet or exercise intervention was found to increase weight loss compared to diet or exercise alone (WMD –4.9 kg; 95% CI –7.3, –2.4). Both CBT and ACT are intensive — patients may need referral to a mental health professional.

> **[Grade A]** Multicomponent psychological and behavioural therapy approaches should be integrated in managing people with obesity:
> - Enhancing communication and avoiding stigmatisation
> - Psychoeducation
> - Motivational interviewing and behavioural strategies
> - Psychological interventions

Stages Of Change Model
| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| Precontemplation | Not yet acknowledging the problem |
| Contemplation | Aware of problem, considering change |
| Preparation | Actively planning change |
| Action | Implementing new behaviours |
| Maintenance | Sustaining change over time |
| Relapse | Returning to old behaviours |

| Level | Study Design |
|---|---|

| 1+ | Well-conducted meta-analyses, systematic reviews, or RCTs with a low-risk of bias |
| 1- | Meta-analyses, systematic, or RCTs with a high-risk of bias |
| 2+ | Well-conducted case-control or cohort studies with low-risk of confounding |
| 3 | Non-analytic studies |
| 4 | Expert opinion |
