## 1.1: Introduction

Obesity is a **complex and chronic disease** that has a heterogeneous presentation. The excess and unwanted body fat accumulation or adiposity leads to many complications and increased risk of premature morbidity and mortality.

[Level 1++] Studies have shown that overweight and obesity increase the risk of chronic health conditions and reduce quality of life. Hence, like any other chronic disease, early recognition, appropriate and effective individualised intervention, and long-term support are essential.

[Level 1+] Not only are overweight and obesity associated with chronic diseases like type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), hypertension, dyslipidaemia, metabolic syndrome, osteoarthritis, and obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), they also increase the risk of several types of cancers.

[Level 1++] Apart from the impact on reducing quality of life and well-being, the physical, psychosocial functioning and perceptions of health of persons with obesity are greatly affected. Persons with obesity experience stigma and discrimination at the workplace and at healthcare facilities.

**Economic burden in Malaysia:**
- The total cost of obesity-related healthcare spending in Malaysia is the highest (19.36%) compared to other ASEAN countries (<10%).
- The estimated productive years lost for males associated with obesity is 6–11 years and for females 7–12 years, with a negative impact on the overall economic status of the country.

## 1.2: Epidemiology

The increasing global prevalence of obesity is alarming. The **National Health Morbidity Survey (NHMS) 2019** (n=16,688) determined that the prevalence of overweight and obesity in Malaysia were **32.3% and 33.7%** respectively (based on Asian BMI cut-off scales). **Based on NHMS 2019, one in two Malaysians is overweight or obese.**

[Level 3] Generally, the prevalence of obesity increases with age.

Childhood and adolescent obesity in Malaysia (NHMS 2019):
- 29.8% of children aged 5–17 years were either overweight or obese (of which 15.0% were adolescents)
- Prevalence of obesity alone among children and adolescents has steadily risen (3.9% in 2011 to 14.8% in 2019)

## 1.3: Obesity As A Disease

Obesity is a disease because it meets the definition — it decreases life expectancy and impairs normal functioning of the body, has characteristic signs and symptoms, increases morbidity, and can be caused by genetic factors. Obesity is a health threat associated with an increased risk of other chronic diseases including T2DM, fatty liver, cardiovascular disease, OSA, stroke, dementia, and cancer.

The overproduction of leptin secondary to excess adipose tissue and increased food intake leads to abnormal energy expenditure. Obesity also contributes to mechanical problems (hip and knee joint pain) and reproductive problems (infertility, miscarriage risk).

[Level 1++] Obesity has led to an increased risk of preventable early death — 29.5% for men and 14.6% for women compared to those with a normal BMI (19% for men and 11% for women).

## 1.4: Impact Of Weight Loss On Associated Medical Conditions

For persons with weight-related medical conditions, reducing weight benefits their health and reduces the risk of additional obesity-related complications.

[Level 1++] A modest weight loss of **5% to 10%** is associated with improvement in blood pressure and high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol. This is even more significant in those with a higher BMI where moderate weight loss improves glucose, triglycerides, and LDL cholesterol levels. [Level 1++]

Weight loss of **10% to 15%** is needed to improve other clinical conditions such as OSA, osteoarthritis, and steatotic hepatitis.

[Level 3] Real-world data on the benefits of weight loss revealed the greatest benefit for reduction of cardiovascular risk factors that can be translated into reduction of morbidity and mortality.

### Treatment Targets By Comorbidity

[Level 1] The expected weight loss depends on the pathology and ranges from 5–15%.

**Table: Treatment Targets Based On Diagnosis In The Medical Management Of Patients With Obesity**

| Diagnosis | Weight Loss Target (%) | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Metabolic syndrome | 10 | Prevention of T2DM |
| T2DM | 5–15 | ↓ HbA1c and glucose-lowering drugs; diabetes remission if short duration |
| Dyslipidaemia | 5–15 | ↓ TG and LDL; ↑ HDL |
| Hypertension | 5–15 | ↓ BP and medications |
| PCOS | 5–15 | Ovulation; ↓ hirsutism and androgen levels; ↑ insulin sensitivity |
| Asthma | 7–8 | Improvement of FEV1 |
| Sleep apnoea | 7–11 | ↓ apnoea/hypopnoea index |
| MAFLD | 10–40 | ↓ intrahepatocellular lipids and inflammation |

*Note: Weight loss is dependent on the nature of the comorbidity.*
