## 4.1: Comprehensive Lifestyle Modification

[Level 4] The goal of lifestyle interventions for obesity treatment is to achieve weight loss through a decrease in calories consumed and an increase in energy expended.

## 4.1.1: Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT) For Adults

Treating obesity requires achieving a state of **negative energy balance**. The goals of MNT are to facilitate weight loss, prevent weight gain, improve cardiometabolic outcomes, and optimise nutrient adequacy.

### Weight Loss Goals

[Level 4] Realistic weight loss goals are:
- **Up to 1 kg per week**
- **Up to 10% of baseline body weight** (over 6–12 months)
- **A total of 3–5%** of baseline body weight in the presence of CV risk factors (e.g., hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, hyperglycaemia)

[Level 1++] MNT for weight management reduced BMI (–1.5 kg/m², 95% CI –1.74, –1.26), improved percentage weight loss (–4.01%), WC reduction (–3.45 cm), systolic BP reduction (–3.04 mmHg), and FPG reduction among individuals with T2DM.

### Energy Restriction

[Level 1++] Energy restrictive diets with 300–1000 kcal/day deficit, or the recommended 1200–1800 kcal/day energy intake in metabolically healthy obese adults, resulted in significant reductions in BMI, BP, and triglycerides.

Calorie restriction should be individualised based on age, sex, readiness level, physical activity level, presence of comorbidities, and patient preference. To achieve an initial weight loss of 0.5–1 kg/week: [Level 4]
- **Women:** 1,200–1,500 kcal/day
- **Men:** 1,500–1,800 kcal/day
- **Alternative:** Reduce daily energy by 500–750 kcal from the usual daily intake

[Level 4] The use of **very low calorie diet (VLCD)** (<800 kcal/day) as part of a comprehensive treatment plan should be prescribed **under the supervision of a medical team that includes a dietitian**.

### Intermittent Fasting

There are various types of intermittent fasting (time-restrictive diets or alternate day fasting). The degree of weight loss depends on the cumulative caloric restriction and not the duration of the fast.

[Level 1-] Alternate day fasting (consumption of 25% and 125% of usual energy intake on fast day and non-fast day respectively) produced significant weight loss (6.0%, 95% CI –8.5%, –3.6%) in a 12-month intervention, with no significant difference compared to daily energy restriction (5.3%) for weight loss or CV risk improvement.

### Macronutrient Modification

**Low carbohydrate or balanced isocaloric diet:**
[Level 1++] Both short- and long-term studies comparing low carbohydrate diet (<45% of total energy combined with high fat or high protein) and balanced isocaloric diet (40–65% carbohydrate) showed **no difference in weight loss** at 3–6 months (–0.74 kg) and at 1–2 years (–0.48 kg), and no significant difference in CV risk factors.

**Low fat or low carbohydrate diet:**
[Level 1+] Both groups produced weight loss (low-fat: –5.3 kg; low-carbohydrate: –6.0 kg at 12 months) with no significant difference between them. Both diets improved lipid profiles and lowered BP, insulin, and glucose levels. LDL-C reduction significantly favoured the low-fat diet; HDL-C increase and triglyceride reduction significantly favoured the low-carbohydrate diet.

**Glycaemic index (GI) and glycaemic load (GL):**
Low GI/GL diets showed greater body weight reductions in adults with BMI ≥30 kg/m² (–0.93 kg, 95% CI –1.73, –0.12) and reduced FPG and fasting insulin compared to high GI/GL diets.

**Meal replacement:**
[Level 1++] Meal replacement-based plans that replaced ≥60% of the total daily energy intake, replacing 3 meals/day or for 3–6 months, resulted in greater weight loss than conventional food-based low energy diet.

> **[Grade A]** MNT provided by a dietitian is recommended for adults with overweight and obesity, especially those with comorbidities.
>
> **[Grade C]** Dietary prescriptions should be individualised to the individual's motivation level, age, sex, comorbidities, physical activity level, and weight loss goals.
>
> **[Grade A]** Calorie restriction diets should be combined with either low carbohydrate or low fat intake.
>
> **[Grade A]** Meal replacement or intermittent fasting may be used as alternative methods of calorie restriction.

## 4.1.2: Physical Activity

[Level 1++] Physical activity and exercise integrated with other components of lifestyle modification such as energy restriction is significant in reducing weight and improving health.

[Level 1-] Physical activity or exercise **alone** without dietary intervention or calorie reduction produces only a small impact on weight loss.

**Expected weight loss from physical activity:**

| Aerobic Physical Activity | Expected Weight Loss |
|---|---|
| <150 min per week | No weight loss or minimal weight loss |
| 150–225 min per week | Weight loss of 2 to 3 kg |
| 225–420 min per week | Weight loss of 5 to 7.5 kg |
| 200–300 min per week | Weight maintenance after weight loss |

[Level 1++] Moderate-intensity physical activity of at least **150 minutes/week** has demonstrated weight maintenance. [Level 1++] A longer duration of more than **300 minutes/week** is associated with weight maintenance after weight loss.

### Types Of Physical Activity

**Table: Categories Of Physical Activity/Exercises With Examples**

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Aerobic training | Low impact: swimming, cycling, walking, rowing (supervised); Higher impact: running, jumping rope; HIIT (alternating short bursts of high-intensity exercise and recovery) |
| Resistance training | Free weights (dumbbells, barbells, kettlebells); weight machines; resistance bands; suspension equipment; bodyweight exercises (walking lunges, push-ups, side planks) |
| Lifestyle physical activity | Getting more steps using a pedometer; climbing stairs instead of using the elevator |

[Level 1+] There is no specific weight loss benefit associated with a particular kind of physical activity; however, **higher intensity activity has better effects on weight loss** compared to lower intensity activity.

| Exercise Type | Range of Expected Weight Loss | Chance of Clinically Significant Weight Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Aerobic exercise training only | 0 to 3% | Possible, but only with high exercise volumes |
| Resistance training only | 0 to 1% | Very unlikely |
| Aerobic and resistance training | 0 to 3% | Possible, but only with high volumes of aerobic exercise |
| Caloric restriction combined with aerobic exercise | 5 to 15% | Possible |

### Prescription Of Physical Activity — FITT Formula

For an appropriate exercise prescription, use the **FITT formula:**
- **F** = Frequency
- **I** = Intensity
- **T** = Time (duration)
- **T** = Type of exercise

[Level 3] For adults who have never exercised or are not physically active, prescribe **small amounts of physical activity** and slowly increase frequency, intensity, and duration over time. [Grade C]

**A regimen of physical activity should start with:**
1. Increasing the number of steps (walking) or movement and daily chores
2. Followed by a gradual progression of aerobic exercise training
3. Strength or resistance exercise is introduced
4. Flexibility exercises incorporated to attain the full range of joint motion

**Safety precautions:**
- Progressing too rapidly results in muscle soreness, fatigue, increased cardiac risk, and decreased motivation
- Each exercise period should include warm-up and cool-down periods
- Encourage drinking plenty of water before, during, and after exercise
- Advise to stop when there are abnormal signs (chest pain, excessive breathlessness)

**Physical activity in persons with obesity >65 years old:**
[Level 3] Physical activity or exercise is encouraged among those above 65 years of age to reduce weight gain and minimise risk of sarcopenia. [Grade C] Key principles for older adults:
- Start with small amounts and gradually increase frequency, intensity, and duration
- Introduce muscle strengthening activities at moderate or greater intensity involving all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week
- Encourage them to limit sedentary time
- Ensure multicomponent physical activity that emphasises functional balance and strength training on 3 or more days a week to enhance functional capacity and prevent falls

> **[Grade A]** Physical activity should be encouraged in all individuals with obesity:
> - A minimum of **150 minutes/week** (30 minutes/day) progressing to **300 minutes/week** (60 minutes/day) of moderate intensity
> - Or at least **75–150 minutes/week** of vigorous intensity is required for weight loss.
>
> **[Grade B]** Combination of both moderate- and vigorous-intensity activity throughout the week should be encouraged to maintain weight loss.
>
> **[Grade B]** Both resistance and aerobic exercise or activities should be emphasised to improve physical function and well-being.
