## 6.1: Prevention Of Overweight And Obesity In Adults

Obesity is a chronic disorder that requires not only continuous care and support but should be offset by preventive strategies.

Primary prevention measures include:
- Promoting healthy eating
- Improving physical activity (reducing physical inactivity)
- Managing daily stress
- Improving the sense of well-being and self-esteem

[Level 2++] A systematic review of prevention of overweight and obesity using education and financial strategies for dietary, exercise, and psychological approaches provided moderate quality evidence for preventing weight gain in normal-weight individuals.

[Level 2++] Primary prevention of further weight gain through dietary advice and increased physical activity in overweight patients without other comorbidities is an appropriate target compared to weight loss.

### 6.1.1: Low-Calorie Diet And Orlistat For Prevention Of Obesity

[Level 1++] Low-calorie diet is cost effective compared to low-calorie diet and orlistat combination. Hence, **intervention using low-calorie diet should be recommended as the first option** by policy makers in combating obesity for the population. Incremental costs per QALY:
- Low-calorie diet only vs no intervention: €17,900 per QALY
- Low-calorie diet + orlistat vs low-calorie diet only: €58,800 per QALY

### 6.1.2: Pharmacotherapy For Prevention Of Obesity In Adults

There is **no pharmacotherapy** that has shown significant benefits in preventing overweight and obesity in individuals with normal BMI at risk of obesity.

- [Level 1+] Women with PCOS who were not overweight or obese showed no reduction in BMI following metformin therapy of 12 weeks to 6 months
- [Level 1++] Individuals with psychotic disorders prescribed antipsychotic medications may experience modest weight loss and metabolic improvements from 8–12 weeks of metformin
- [Level 1+] A systematic review of GLP1-RAs in individuals with normal BMI showed no significant difference in weight change

> **[Grade A]** Fundamental preventive measures that should be implemented against the onset of obesity are promoting healthy eating, improving physical activity, and implementing stress management practices.
>
> **[Grade B]** Healthcare providers should offer weight management interventions for preventing weight gain in normal-weight or overweight adults.

## 6.2: Prevention Of Obesity In Children And Adolescents

The primary intervention in the management of paediatric obesity should be **prevention**. Preventive interventions should be designed to **modify the environment surrounding the child**. Primary prevention actions begin from the prenatal age into adolescence with actions at individual, family, and community level.

### 6.2.1: Early Life Interventions

[Level 1+] Preconception and prenatal interventions are of major importance in the prevention of childhood obesity.

[Level 1+] **Breast feeding should be encouraged** for its numerous health benefits and its role in obesity prevention.

[Level 2+] Adherence to dietary guidelines during weaning is recommended. Solid foods and beverages other than breast milk or infant formulas should be **introduced no earlier than 6 months** — evidence suggests early introduction of weaning diet may be linked to childhood obesity.

[Level 1+] Systematic reviews demonstrate a beneficial effect using interventions focused on individual- or family-level behaviour changes through counselling provided through home or community visits.

### 6.2.2: Lifestyle Interventions

Lifestyle interventions remain the first-line approach for prevention of childhood obesity. Preventive measures must target the **family and school environment**.

**Family environment:**
Research indicates the need for parental involvement and a whole family approach. There should be support for home activities that encourage children to be more active, eat nutritious foods, and spend less time on screen-based activities.

**School environment:**
School-based programmes can be standardised across multiple sites and reach large populations. Measures to promote obesity prevention within schools:
- School curriculum including healthy diet, importance of physical activity, and positive body image
- Increasing physical activity sessions and development of fundamental movement skills
- Improving the nutritional quality of foods in schools
- Supporting teachers and other staff to implement health promotion strategies

**Diet and physical activity interventions:**
- Multicomponent interventions focusing on dietary, physical activity, and behavioural aspects are useful
- Implementation of multidisciplinary programmes should involve a team (psychologist, nurse, dietitian, physiotherapist, and exercise specialist)

**Evidence for combined interventions by age group:**

*Children ≤5 years old:* [Level 1++] Combined diet and physical activity interventions contributed to modest effects:
- BMI Z-score reduction of 0.07 units
- BMI reduction of 0.11 kg/m²

*Children aged 0–6 years (diet + PA + behavioural):* [Level 1++] Greater BMI Z-score reductions:
- –0.3 units at 6–12 months
- –0.4 units at 12–18 months
- –0.3 units at 2 years

*Children 6–12 years:* [Level 1++] Combined diet + physical activity reduced BMI Z-score by 0.05 units. [Level 1++] Combined diet + PA + behavioural for ≥6 months: reduced BMI by 0.53 kg/m², BMI Z-score by 0.06 units, and body weight by 1.45 kg.

*Adolescents 13–18 years:* [Level 1++] Multi-component interventions (diet + PA + behavioural) reduced BMI by 1.18 kg/m², BMI Z-score by 0.13 units, and body weight by 3.67 kg. Favourable effect reported up to 24 months of follow-up.

*Children and adolescents 0–18 years:* [Level 1++] Combined interventions of diet and physical activity were linked with reductions in BMI and BMI Z-scores.

### 6.2.3: Environment

Preventive measures for obesity will need to include **changes in the environment**. Availability and accessibility to greenspaces, parks, recreational facilities, and sidewalks were found to be associated with increased physical activity levels and reduced sedentary behaviour in children.

> **[Grade B]** Breast feeding should be encouraged and adherence to dietary guidelines during weaning should be advocated.
>
> **[Grade B]** Multicomponent interventions should be used in preventing obesity: dietary, physical activity, and behavioural aspects.
>
> **[Grade C]** Family and parental involvement should be encouraged.
>
> **[Grade C]** School-based programme with community engagement should be advocated.
>
> **[Grade D]** Environmental changes should be made to support obesity prevention.

## 7. Implementing The Guidelines

Implementation of these CPG helps in providing quality healthcare services. Various factors and resource implications should be considered.

### Facilitating Factors

- Availability of CPG to healthcare providers (hardcopies and softcopies)
- Regular conferences and updates on management of obesity led by the Malaysian Endocrine and Metabolic Society
- Public awareness campaigns about obesity
- Involvement of governmental/non-governmental organisations (Bahagian Kawalan Penyakit and Bahagian Pembangunan Kesihatan Keluarga, MoH; MEMS)

### Limiting Factors

- Lack of awareness about obesity as a chronic disease among healthcare providers and individuals living with obesity
- Lack of awareness on the importance of treatment of obesity
- Misconception that obesity is solely attributed to lifestyle and is the responsibility of the individual
- Lack of resources at the school level to educate children/adolescents
- Lack of access to evidence-based effective and safe treatments for obesity

### Potential Resource Implications

- Ensure widespread distribution of CPG to healthcare providers
- Provide regular training to healthcare providers via effective seminars and workshops
- Involve multidisciplinary teams at all levels of healthcare
- Explore opportunities to work with school healthcare teams in addressing paediatric overweight and obesity

## 8. Clinical Indicators For Quality Management

Two clinical audit indicators are proposed:

### Indicator 1: Weight Reduction

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Percentage of people with obesity (at 6 months follow-up) who reduced weight by 5%** | **>50%** for primary care and tertiary care |
| **Formula** | (Number with weight loss ≥5% ÷ Total at 6 months follow-up) × 100% | |

### Indicator 2: Comorbidity Screening

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Percentage of people with obesity screened for complications** | **>85%** for primary care and tertiary care |
| **Formula** | (Number screened for complications ÷ Total number with obesity attending facility) × 100% | |
