## 9.1: Continuous Subcutaneous Insulin Infusion (CSII)

An insulin pump or continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII) is a small device that administers subcutaneous insulin continuously. It has been used in non-pregnant diabetic patients, especially in T1DM.

[Level I] In a Cochrane systematic review, there is **no evidence to support the use of CSII over multiple daily injections (MDI)** for pregnant women with pre-existing diabetes and GDM. The RCTs used in the review were small and of low quality.

**Key points:**
- CSII does not confer additional maternal or fetal benefits compared with MDI.
- However, women who are **already using a CSII device can continue** it throughout pregnancy.

## 9.2: Corticosteroids

Corticosteroids are administered during pregnancy to **accelerate fetal lung maturation** (used when preterm delivery is anticipated). They are known to **elevate plasma glucose levels** and worsen glycaemic control in diabetes in pregnancy.

[Level III] In two observational studies, both fasting and postprandial plasma glucose were elevated after corticosteroid administration in pregnant women with diabetes. Most patients had to increase their insulin doses to less than double their regular doses.

There is no evidence on the use of oral or inhaled corticosteroids for other indications in diabetic pregnancy.

**Management when corticosteroids are given:**
- Women with diabetes who are given corticosteroids during pregnancy need **regular plasma glucose monitoring** (at least four times a day for 48 hours from the first dose).
- Insulin dose should be **adjusted accordingly** to maintain glucose targets.

## 9.3: Fasting (Ramadhan)

Pregnant women with diabetes should be **strongly advised against fasting** during Ramadhan. However, some women may still wish to observe this religious obligation.

[Level III] In a local Malaysian study on pregnant women with diabetes who fasted during Ramadhan, most of those on NPH insulin (79.2%) were able to fast for more than 15 days without any hypoglycaemia or fetal complications. Therefore, **once or twice daily NPH insulin is safe and tolerable** for this group of pregnant women.

[Level II-2] Women with pre-existing diabetes and GDM who had good glycaemic control prior to Ramadhan continue to have good glycaemic control throughout and after Ramadhan.

## 9.4: Recommendation 15

> **[Grade C]** Pregnant women with diabetes should consult their healthcare providers if they wish to observe fasting.

Key Points For Special Conditions
| Condition | Key Message |
|---|---|
| CSII (Insulin Pump) | No proven benefit over MDI. May continue if already using prior to pregnancy. |
| Corticosteroids | Check CBG at least 4 times/day for 48 hours from first dose. Adjust insulin dose accordingly. |
| Fasting (Ramadhan) | Strongly advise against fasting. If patient insists, consult healthcare provider. NPH once or twice daily is generally safe in those with good pre-Ramadhan control. |

Level Of Evidence (US/Canadian Preventive Services Task Force 2001)
| Level | Study Design |
|---|---|
| I | Evidence from at least one properly randomised controlled trial |
| II-1 | Evidence obtained from well-designed controlled trials without randomisation |
| II-2 | Evidence obtained from well-designed cohort or case-control analytic studies, preferably from more than one centre or group |
| II-3 | Evidence from multiple time series with or without intervention. Dramatic results in uncontrolled experiments could also be regarded as this type of evidence |
| III | Opinions of respected authorities based on clinical experience; descriptive studies and case reports; or reports of expert committees |

Grades Of Recommendation (GRADE Principles)
| Grade | Definition |
|---|---|
| A | Strong recommendation; based on Level I evidence (RCT/meta-analysis) |
| B | Moderate recommendation; based on Level II-1 or II-2 evidence |
| C | Weak/conditional recommendation; based on Level II-3, III, or expert consensus |
