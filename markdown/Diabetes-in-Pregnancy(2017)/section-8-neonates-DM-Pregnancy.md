## 8.1: Background

Optimal glycaemic control during pregnancy reduces the incidence of perinatal morbidity and mortality. Women with diabetes should deliver in hospitals where **neonatal resuscitation services** are available.

Recognised neonatal complications of mothers with diabetes include:
- **Macrosomia** (birth weight >4 kg)
- **Traumatic birth injuries** (related to macrosomia and shoulder dystocia)
- **Neonatal hypoglycaemia**
- **Hyperbilirubinaemia** (jaundice)
- **Polycythaemia**
- **Hypocalcaemia**
- **Hypomagnesaemia**

## 8.2: Neonatal Management

The following management steps apply to all neonates of mothers with diabetes:

### 8.2.1: Rooming-In

The neonate should **stay with the mother** unless there are clinical complications or signs that warrant admission for special or intensive care.

### 8.2.2: Neonatal Blood Glucose Monitoring

Perform **blood glucose testing routinely at 2–4 hours after birth** to detect neonatal hypoglycaemia.

Perform additional blood tests for the following if **clinical signs** are present:
- Polycythaemia
- Hyperbilirubinaemia
- Hypocalcaemia
- Hypomagnesaemia

### 8.2.3: Feeding

Early and frequent feeding should be encouraged to prevent and correct neonatal hypoglycaemia. Breastfeeding is preferred.

### 8.2.4: Discharge Criteria

Neonates should only be discharged **after 24 hours of delivery**, provided that:
- They are maintaining **satisfactory CBG levels**, AND
- They are **feeding well**.

If the neonate is not maintaining satisfactory glucose levels or has clinical complications, admission to a neonatal unit is warranted.
