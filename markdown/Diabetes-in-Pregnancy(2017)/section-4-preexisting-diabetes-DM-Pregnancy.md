## 4.1: Preconception Care And Counselling

Women with pre-existing Type 1 DM (T1DM) and Type 2 DM (T2DM) benefit from preconception care because it reduces the incidence of congenital malformation, preterm delivery, and perinatal mortality. Preconception care also lowers HbA1c levels in the first trimester. [Level II-2]

However, the role of preconception care in women with a history of GDM remains unclear due to lack of evidence. [Level I]

### 4.1.1: Pre-Conception Goals

Before conceiving, women with pre-existing diabetes are advised to:
- Reduce weight if overweight or obese
- Follow a recommended exercise schedule of **150 minutes per week**
- Keep blood pressure **<130/80 mmHg**
- Keep HbA1c to the **optimal target (<6.5% or 48 mmol/mol)**
- Undergo screening for **diabetic retinopathy** and **nephropathy** prior to conception
- Undergo **cardiovascular risk assessment** if on contraception with multiple cardiovascular risk factors, before withdrawal of contraception
- Take **folic acid supplementation three months before** withdrawal of contraception

### 4.1.2: Components Of Multidisciplinary Preconception Care

Preconception care, provided by a **multidisciplinary team**, should consist of: [Level III]
- Discussion on timeline for pregnancy planning
- Lifestyle advice (diet, physical activities, smoking cessation, and optimal body weight)
- Folic acid supplementation
- Appropriate contraception
- Full medication review (discontinue potentially teratogenic medications)
- Retinal and renal screening
- Relevant blood investigations

Women with pre-existing diabetes should be informed of glycaemic control targets and empowered to achieve control **before conception**. They should also be counselled on the risks and expected management strategies during pregnancy.

### 4.1.3: Medication Review Before Pregnancy

- Women with T2DM who are planning a pregnancy should **switch from oral antidiabetic agents (OAD) to insulin** for glycaemic control. However, patients who are already on metformin may **continue** treatment.
- Women with pre-existing diabetes who also have **polycystic ovarian syndrome** may continue metformin for ovulation induction.
- Prior to conception or upon detection of pregnancy, the following medications **must be discontinued:**
  - Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors
  - Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs)
  - Statins

## 4.2: Contraception

Women with pre-existing diabetes are encouraged to have a **planned pregnancy**. Their choice of contraception should be based on personal preferences and any risk factors according to the Medical Eligibility Criteria.
- The use of **oral contraceptive pills** is allowed.
- **Intrauterine contraceptive device (IUCD)** is the preferred contraceptive method.
- **Caution** should be exercised when using Depo-Provera (medroxyprogesterone acetate injectable) as it may worsen glycaemic control. [Level III]

## 4.3: Glycaemic Control Before Conception

[Level III] NICE guidelines recommend that women with pre-existing diabetes planning pregnancy should aim for HbA1c **<6.5% (48 mmol/mol)** if this is achievable without causing hypoglycaemia. Any reduction in HbA1c level towards the target is likely to reduce the risk of congenital malformations.

Women with HbA1c level **>10% (86 mmol/mol)** are advised **not to become pregnant** because of the associated risks.

[Level I] For each 1% decrement in HbA1c, the risk of pre-eclampsia reduces by 12% in the pre-pregnancy period up to 53% at 34 weeks of gestation in T1DM.

Women with pre-existing diabetes who plan to become pregnant should increase the frequency of self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) to include fasting, pre-prandial, and postprandial levels.

## 4.4: Folic Acid Supplementation

Folic acid (FA) is the synthetic form of folate. The main function of folate is to act as a co-enzyme in one-carbon transfer during the methylation cycle, essential for synthesis of nucleic acids (forming part of DNA) and neurotransmitters. Folate also plays an essential function in protein synthesis, metabolism, and processes associated with cell multiplication and tissue growth.

[Level I] Daily FA supplementation (alone or in combination with other vitamins and minerals) in women who become pregnant or are ≤12 weeks pregnant is effective in preventing neural tube defects (NTDs) compared with no intervention/placebo or vitamins and minerals without FA (RR=0.31, 95% CI 0.17 to 0.58). It also has a protective effect for recurrence of NTDs (RR=0.34, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.64). However, there is no significant evidence of preventive effect on cleft palate, cleft lip, congenital cardiovascular defects, or miscarriages.

[Level II-2] Lack of periconceptional (during a month before conception or first three months of pregnancy) use of vitamins or supplements containing folic acid is associated with an excess risk of NTDs in diabetes.

All women with diabetes should be counselled regarding intake of foods high in folic acid, folate-fortified foods, and appropriate FA supplementation of **4–5 mg per day** during the preconception period and in the first 12 weeks of gestation. [Level III] FA supplementation should be taken **at least three months prior to conception**.

## 4.5: Recommendation 4

> **[Grade C]** Preconception care of women with pre-existing diabetes which involves a multidisciplinary team should be fully implemented in all healthcare facilities.
>
> **[Grade A]** A supplement of 5 mg folic acid per day should be given to women with diabetes who plan to become pregnant, starting at least three months prior to conception and continuing until 12 weeks of gestation.
