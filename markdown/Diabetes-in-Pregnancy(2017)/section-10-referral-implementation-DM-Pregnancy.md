## 10.1: Referral To Secondary/Tertiary Care

All women with **pre-existing diabetes** should receive continuous specialist care to detect complications, review medications, and optimise glycaemic control. [Level III]

Similarly, women with **GDM who have poor glycaemic control** and **fetal complications** should receive specialist care.

Referral to a **dietitian** is necessary to ensure a balanced diet and achievement of good glycaemic control. Assessment for retinopathy and nephropathy should be performed regularly during pregnancy.

**Postpartum referrals:**
- Women with pre-existing diabetes or GDM should be referred back to their health facilities after delivery to continue with their diabetes care.
- **Ophthalmology review** should be performed at least **six months after delivery**.

## 10.2: Recommendation 16

> **[Grade C]** Pregnant women with pre-existing diabetes and women with GDM who have poor glycaemic control or fetal complications should be referred to secondary or tertiary care.

## 10.3: Implementing The Guidelines

Implementation of this CPG helps provide quality healthcare services based on best available evidence applied to local scenario and expertise.

### 10.3.1: Facilitating Factors

Existing factors that support implementation of these recommendations:
- Availability of CPG to healthcare providers (hardcopies and softcopies)
- Regular conferences and updates on management of diabetes in pregnancy involving professional societies (Malaysian Endocrine and Metabolic Society, Obstetrics and Gynaecology Society of Malaysia, Family Medicine Specialist Association, Academy of Family Physician Malaysia, National Diabetes Institute)
- Public awareness campaigns on diabetes in pregnancy during World Diabetes Day and similar events

### 10.3.2: Limiting Factors

Existing barriers to implementation:
- Different levels of care and wide variation in practice due to differences in expertise, facilities, and financial constraints
- Lack of awareness among high-risk women about their risk of developing diabetes in pregnancy

### 10.3.3: Potential Resource Implications

To implement this CPG, dedicated efforts should be made to:
- Ensure widespread distribution of the CPG to healthcare providers
- Provide regular training to healthcare providers via effective seminars and workshops
- Involve multidisciplinary teams at all levels of healthcare
- Strengthen the National Obstetric Registry

## 10.4: Clinical Audit Indicators

Two clinical audit indicators are proposed to monitor quality:

### Audit Indicator 1: Screening At Booking In Women At Risk Of GDM

**Target:** >85%

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Percentage of women at risk of GDM screened with 75 g OGTT at booking | (Number of women at risk of GDM screened with 75 g OGTT at booking in a period ÷ Total number of women at risk of GDM at booking in the same period) × 100% |

### Audit Indicator 2: Postpartum OGTT In Women With GDM

**Target:** >70%

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Percentage of women with GDM who had OGTT at six weeks postpartum | (Number of women with GDM who had OGTT at six weeks postpartum ÷ Total number of women with GDM) × 100% |

Referral Criteria Summary
| Condition | Referral Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Pre-existing diabetes (any type) | Continuous specialist care throughout pregnancy and postpartum |
| GDM with poor glycaemic control | Refer to secondary/tertiary care |
| GDM with fetal complications | Refer to secondary/tertiary care |
| Abnormal renal function (creatinine ≥120 µmol/L, ACR >30 mg/mmol) | Refer to nephrologist |
| Diabetic retinopathy detected | Refer to ophthalmologist |
| Postpartum ophthalmology review | At least 6 months after delivery |
