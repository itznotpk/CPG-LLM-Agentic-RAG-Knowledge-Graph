## 1.1: Background And Epidemiology

The National Health Morbidity Survey 2015 showed that the prevalence of diabetes in Malaysia was 17.5%. [Level III] Generally, the prevalence of diabetes increases with age. This is a concern especially in the reproductive age group as it will adversely affect fertility and pregnancy outcomes.

Diabetes in pregnancy is associated with significant risks to both the woman and her developing fetus. The National Obstetric Report involving 14 tertiary hospitals showed the incidence of diabetes in pregnancy was 8.66% in 2011 and 8.83% in 2012. The caesarean section rates in this group of patients were approximately 13% in both 2011 and 2012. In both years, approximately 16% of babies born to diabetic mothers weighed 4 kg or more. There was a two-fold increase in macrosomia in diabetic patients in both years compared to non-diabetic patients, which is a contributory factor to the increased caesarean section rates. [Level III] These figures were obtained from only a few tertiary hospitals and the actual prevalence is expected to be higher across Malaysia.

There are wide variations in local clinical practice management of diabetes in pregnancy at different levels of care. This CPG therefore provides a comprehensive evidence-based guideline in the screening and management of diabetes and its complications in pregnancy, to assist healthcare providers in delivering high quality care.

## 1.2: Objectives

The objectives of this Clinical Practice Guideline (CPG) are to provide evidence-based recommendations on diabetes in pregnancy covering:
- Screening and diagnosis
- Management (pre-pregnancy, antenatal, intrapartum, and postpartum periods)

## 1.3: Target Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Women with diabetes planning for pregnancy
- Pregnant women at risk of diabetes
- Pregnant women with pre-existing diabetes and gestational diabetes mellitus

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnant women with secondary causes of diabetes

## 1.4: Target Group / Users

This CPG is intended to guide those involved in the management of diabetes in pregnancy at both primary and secondary/tertiary care levels, in public and private practice, including:
- Medical officers and specialists
- Allied health professionals
- Trainees and medical students
- Patients and their advocates
- Professional societies

## 1.5: Healthcare Settings

Applicable in outpatient, inpatient, and community settings.

## 1.6: Types Of Diabetes In Pregnancy

Diabetes in pregnancy encompasses two main categories:
- **Pre-existing diabetes:** Women with Type 1 or Type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed before the current pregnancy. This group requires specialised preconception counselling, structured antenatal follow-up, and attention to existing complications such as retinopathy and nephropathy.
- **Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM):** Any degree of glucose intolerance first recognised during pregnancy. This may resolve after delivery or persist as Type 2 diabetes.
- **Overt diabetes in pregnancy:** When a woman is found to have blood glucose values meeting the diagnostic threshold for diabetes (FPG ≥7.0 mmol/L or RPG ≥11.1 mmol/L with symptoms) during the first trimester. This should be managed as pre-existing diabetes.
