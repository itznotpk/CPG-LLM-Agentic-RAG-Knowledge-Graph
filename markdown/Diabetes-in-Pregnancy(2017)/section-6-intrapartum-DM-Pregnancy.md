## 6.1: Background And Rationale

Maternal hyperglycaemia during labour increases the risks of **neonatal hypoglycaemia** and **fetal distress**. [Level III]

Insulin requirements decrease during delivery due to less caloric intake and increased metabolic demands during labour. Adequate glucose must be provided during labour to meet its high energy requirement. [Level III]

[Level I] A small RCT among pregnant women with diabetes requiring insulin compared titrated insulin infusion versus alternating intravenous fluid regime with insulin as needed. There was no difference in intrapartum maternal glycaemic control and neonatal hypoglycaemia between the two approaches. There is no other evidence on the optimal method of maintaining glycaemic control during delivery.

## 6.2: CBG Target During Labour

The target capillary blood glucose (CBG) during active labour is **4.0–7.0 mmol/L**.

Maternal CBG outside this range should be corrected promptly to avoid:
- CBG <4.0 mmol/L: Risk of maternal and neonatal hypoglycaemia
- CBG >7.0 mmol/L: Risk of neonatal hypoglycaemia, fetal distress, and metabolic complications

## 6.3: Monitoring Frequency

| Patient Category | CBG Monitoring Frequency |
|---|---|
| Women on insulin treatment (T1DM, T2DM, GDM on insulin/metformin) | Every 1–2 hours during labour |
| Women not on insulin (GDM on diet alone) | Every 4 hours |

## 6.4: Management By Patient Category

### Category 1 — Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM)
- Start **intravenous (IV) dextrose infusion** immediately at the onset of established labour (T1DM women cannot produce endogenous insulin and require a continuous glucose supply).
- Proceed to intravenous insulin infusion and titration (Algorithm C).
- Monitor CBG hourly.

### Category 2 — T2DM or GDM on Insulin or Metformin
- **Stop** subcutaneous insulin injections and oral metformin at the onset of active labour.
- Check CBG every 1–2 hours.
- If CBG >7.0 mmol/L: Start IV insulin infusion and proceed to Algorithm C.
- If CBG 4.0–7.0 mmol/L: Continue monitoring.
- If CBG <4.0 mmol/L: See hypoglycaemia management below.

### Category 3 — GDM on Diet Alone
- Check CBG every 4 hours.
- If CBG remains 4.0–7.0 mmol/L: Continue monitoring without intervention.
- If CBG 7.1–10.0 mmol/L: Repeat CBG in 1 hour. If still >7.0 mmol/L → start IV insulin infusion (Algorithm C).
- If CBG >10.0 mmol/L: Start IV insulin infusion immediately (Algorithm C).
- If CBG <4.0 mmol/L: See hypoglycaemia management below.

## 6.5: Hypoglycaemia Management During Labour (CBG <4.0 mmol/L)

- Inform the doctor immediately.
- If **symptomatic:** Give bolus IV 20 mL of D50% (50% dextrose).
- If **asymptomatic:** Offer nourishing fluid.
- Repeat CBG in 30 minutes and reassess using the CBG result table.

## 6.6: IV Insulin Infusion Protocol

When IV insulin infusion is required (for T1DM or when CBG >7.0 mmol/L):

**Initiation Rate:**
- T1DM: 0.01–0.02 unit/kg/hour
- T2DM/GDM: 0.05–0.07 unit/kg/hour
- If insulin requirement exceeds 0.1 unit/kg/hour → refer to endocrinologist/physician immediately.

**Titration Table:**

| CBG (mmol/L) | Action |
|---|---|
| Drop >2.0 from previous reading | Reduce infusion by 1.0 unit/hour |
| 4.0–7.0 (target range) | Maintain current dose |
| 7.1–8.5 | Add 0.5 unit/hour |
| 8.6–10.0 | Add 1.0 unit/hour |
| >10.0 | Add 2.0 units/hour |

Check CBG hourly after each adjustment. If CBG drops below 4.0 mmol/L at any point: withhold insulin infusion and apply hypoglycaemia management above.

**Preparation of IV Insulin Infusion Solution:**
Dilute insulin to a concentration of 1 unit/mL using either:
- 20 units short-acting human insulin (actrapid) made up to 20 mL with 0.9% saline in a 20 mL syringe, OR
- 50 units short-acting human insulin made up to 50 mL with 0.9% saline in a 50 mL syringe.
Administer via a syringe pump.

**Dextrose-Potassium Chloride (KCl) Co-Infusion:**
Based on serum potassium (K+) results, prepare a dextrose-KCl mixture for concurrent infusion:

| K+ Result | Amount of KCl to add to 500 mL of 5% Dextrose Solution |
|---|---|
| <3.5 mmol/L | 3 g (39 mmol) |
| 3.5–4.5 mmol/L | 2 g (26 mmol) |
| >4.5 mmol/L | 1 g (13 mmol) |

*Note: One ampoule of 10 mL KCl 10% contains 13.4 mmol of K+.*

Maintain a constant infusion of dextrose-KCl over 6 hours at a rate of 83 mL/hour. Use one dedicated IV cannula with both infusions run concurrently through infusion pumps connected via a 3-way stopcock.

## 6.7: Recommendation 13

> **[Grade C]** In women with diabetes, capillary blood glucose should be maintained at 4.0–7.0 mmol/L during labour and delivery.
>
> **[Grade C]** Monitoring of capillary blood glucose during labour and delivery in women with diabetes should be done:
> - 1– to 2-hourly in women on insulin treatment
> - 4-hourly in women not on insulin treatment

*For full algorithm details, refer to Section 0: Algorithm B (Intrapartum Glucose Monitoring) and Algorithm C (Insulin Infusion and Titration).*
