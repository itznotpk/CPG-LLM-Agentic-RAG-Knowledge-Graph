- Cardiac   disease in pregnancy is relatively common; 0.55% and 0.45% of all pregnancies in 2013 and 2014 respectively: It is major cause of non-obstetric morbidity and maternal mortality.
- AII women in the reproductive age group who have cardiac disease should undergo preconception cardiac assessment and counselling by their family physicians, cardiologists, obstetricians, maternal fetal medicine specialists andlor geneticists. (Flowchart 1 pg. 27)
- They  should be risk   stratified   using the modified WHO and NYHA classification Table IA1B &amp; 2, pg. 18-21)

## 3.1.1 Maternal risk

Pregnant women with cardiac disease are at risk of adverse maternal and fetal outcomes

- c Their risk should be assessed before conception or early in the pregnancy to optimise the outcome of the pregnancy.

Maternal cardiovascular risk can be assessed the modified World Health Organisation (WHO) classification using

- WHO Class II Small increased risk of maternal mortality or moderate increase in morbidity.
- WHO Class I - No detectable increased risk of maternal mortality and nol mild increase in morbidity.
- WHO Class II-Ill &amp; Ill \_ Significantly increased risk of maternal mortality or severe morbidity. Expert counselling required If pregnancy is decided upon, intensive   specialist cardiac and obstetric   monitoring needed throughout pregnancy; childbirth; and the puerperium
- WHO Class IV Extremely high risk of maternal mortality or severe termination should be discussed . If pregnancy continues; care as for

The functional capacity of the patient can be classified using the NYHA functional class.10 Table 2, pg. 21)

Risk stratification is accomplished by considering disease specific outcome predictors" and risk scores from large studies of pregnant women with heart disease. '2,13 The maternal and fetal risk magnifies if the disease complexity increases and when there are additional cumulative risk factors. 13,14

Hence a comprehensive risk assessment of maternal and fetal outcomes taking into consideration the factors listed below should be undertaken

Factors to be considered during maternal cardiovascular risk assessment include:

- Lesion specific outcome; e.g. Ventricular septal defect (VSD)
- Complexity of the disease; e.g. VSD with aortic regurgitation (AR)

## 3.1.2 Fetal outcomes and risk

Fetal  outcome should also be considered when planning the pregnancy. Prematurity; fetal growth restriction (FGR); small for gestational age and fetal loss is increased in pregnant patients with cardiac disease. Fetal complications range between 20-27.8%.12,13,16 Neonatal mortality is 4 times higher compared to a normal pregnancy: 12

The maternal predictors of poor neonatallfetal outcome in women with cardiac disease are as follows:12,19,20

- Cyanosis (oxygen saturation 85%)
- Mechanical prosthetic valves
- Left heart obstructive lesions
- Use of heparin or warfarin during pregnancy
- Smoking during pregnancy
- Twinl multiple pregnancy

Some maternal cardiac conditions have an increased risk of   genetic transmission to the (e.g. Marfan syndrome). 17,18 There is also an increased risk of congenital heart disease (CHD) in the fetus if either parent has CHD. (Section 3.2.1, pg. 48) fetus
