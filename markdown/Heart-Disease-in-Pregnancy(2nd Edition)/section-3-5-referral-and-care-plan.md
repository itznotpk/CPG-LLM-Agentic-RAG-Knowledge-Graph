## Mode of Delivery

- Induction of labour may be achieved mechanically with Foley catheter or using PGE2 (Caution: there is a 1-5% risk of uterine hyperstimulation) .
- Timing of delivery should be individualised according to the women's cardiac status, fetal wellbeing and Bishop score
- Spontaneous labour is usually quicker than induced and carries a higher chance of successful delivery
- Caesarean section should be reserved for:
- Vaginal delivery is preferred with assisted second stage
- women on oral anticoagulants who have not been switched to heparin at least 2 weeks before delivery
- patients with acute or chronic aortic dissection
- patients with Marfan syndrome and an aortic diameter 45 mm
- functional class NYHA III &amp; IV
- LVEF
- severe obstructive cardiac lesions
- severe pulmonary hypertension and Eisenmenger syndrome
- &gt;obstetric indications

## Other considerations:

- The lateral decubitus position to avoid aortacaval compression is the preferred position in labour.
- Prolonged and difficult labour should be avoided.
- Epidural analgesia is the analgesia of choice in labour. This should be done with a low dose of local anaesthetic with increments given very slowly to avoid acute hypotension
- Paediatric team to be present during delivery .
- Antibiotic prophylaxis in high risk patients (section 4.10.3, pg. 120)

## 3.5.1 Specialist referral and level of care

When pregnancy is confirmed, the patient should be seen at the nearest health care facility:  This patient should be referred as soon as possible for cardiac assessment and risk stratification

Under   the Ministry of   Health Obstetrics Risk Stratification Charts, heart disease is coded red. (Colour coding Appendix F pg. 157)

The level of care will depend upon the maternal cardiovascular risk stratification .

- Women with WHO risk class |-Il and NYHA &amp; Il can be managed in their local hospital after at least one review by the physicianlcardiac specialist:. This review enables the primary team t0 anticipate any potential risk(s) to ensure good antepartum; intrapartum and postpartum care. (Table 3, pg. 22)
- Women with WHO risk class II-III, III &amp; IV and NYHA III &amp; IV should be followed up in tertiary centre by a multidisciplinary team with expertise in managing high risk pregnancies. Table 3, pg. 22)

Frequency of follow up at the specialist centre will depend on the maternal risk, baseline cardiovascular and functional status;   progression of the pregnancy; onset of symptoms 22-24) Policies of the respective centres, patient socioeconomic &amp; geographical  factors and patient  preferences  should also be considered

## 3.5.2 General pregnancy care plan

## 3.5.2.1 Antepartum care

The aims of antepartum care are:

- to assess and risk stratify the maternal cardiovascular status
- to review maternal medications for potential fetotoxicity
- to offer termination of pregnancy if indicated
- wherever possible; to optimise maternal cardiovascular status
- fetal assessments and surveillance
- to formulate an individualised pregnancy care plan which includes time; place and mode of delivery (pg. 29)

## General Principles in the Management of Cardiac Disease in Pregnancy

## Once pregnancy is confirmed:

- Detailed medical and surgical history should be taken.
- Early booking is advised.
- A medical report of the cardiac diagnosis and current status should be requested from the attending physicians
- The core members of the multidisciplinary team should be family medicine specialist(s)   obstetricians, maternal fetal medicine (MFM)   specialists cardiologists, andlor obstetric anaesthetists\_ Where appropriate; neonatologists, cardiac anaesthetists, cardiothoracic surgeons andlor intensivists should also be involved in the care
- All pregnant women with cardiac disease should undergo risk stratificationl functional class assessment to determine the frequency and the level of antenatal care. Tables 1-5, pg. 18-24)
- Patient information; education and advice about how cardiac disease will affect the pregnancy; birth and postpartum assessment are to be discussed. Wherever possible the family should also be involved in the counselling.
- Appropriate care can be shared between district and tertiary hospitals to provide combined follow up by relevant   specialists according to the maternal cardiovascular risk.
- An individualised pregnancy care plan should be formulated. (pg. 29)
- At  every antenatal cardiac visit, the   following   parameters should be assessed and documented:
- Termination of pregnancy should be considered in patients with WHO class IV.
- Symptoms NYHA functional class
- Vital   signs pulse rate and rhythm; BP (should be measured manually with 44), oxygen saturation
- Signs of heart failure e.g. gallop rhythm; lung crepitations
- Cardiovascular signs clubbing, polycythaemia; cyanosis; anaemia; murmurs etc
- Fetal assessment:
- Nuchal translucency scan 11 to &lt; 14 weeks
- Genetic karyotyping; if indicated
- Fetal echocardiogram is advisable in women with CHD43,44
- Fetal anomaly scan at 18 22 weeks
- Iron and folic acid   supplements should be  prescribed to all   patients
- For anticoagulation in pregnancy; refer to section 4.9, pg. 114.
- Correct anaemia if present.
- Admission and rest may be required in patients with NYHA/ WHO Class





## General Principles in the Management of Cardiac Disease in Pregnancy

- Correct   factors that may contribute to cardiac decompensation e.g. infection; arrhythmias and hypertension
- Depending on the severity of the cardiac condition; the women may need to be seen at closer intervals. (Tables 3-5, pg. 22-24)

- The pregnancy care plan addresses:
- Mode of delivery

- anaesthetic considerations

## 3.5.2.2 Intrapartum care

## Management should be an obstetrician led care.

- Monitoring includes:
- Oxygen saturation oxygen supplementation to be given if oxygen saturation is 95%
- Haemodynamic cardiac monitor ECG, pulse and non-invasive BP monitoring
- Continuous fetal monitoring
- Strict input output chart.
