## 4.2 Congenital heart disease (CHD)

Patients with CHD can be grouped into:

- Cyanotic heart disease unrepaired Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF), complex cyanotic heart disease
- Acyanotic heart disease atrial septal defect (ASD), ventricular septal defect   (VSD); patent  ductus arteriosus   (PDA) Coarctation of aorta (COA)
- Surgically repairedl  palliative intervention Fontan, post TOF repair; Rastelli, cyanotic heart disease with aortopulmonary shunt

The risk of an adverse cardiovascular event occurring depend upon the underlying cardiac   lesion and how the pregnant   patient   adapts to the haemodynamic and hormonal changes that occur. 12,19 will

Pregnancy in acyanotic heart disease is generally well tolerated, except in the presence of:12,13

- pulmonary hypertension
- poor ventricular function
- obstructive valve lesions

Patients   with good surgical  repair and no haemodynamically   significant residual lesion tolerate pregnancy well. 12,19

Patients with cyanotic heart disease have higher maternal and fetal morbidity. In these patients pregnancy is high risk. (WHO cardiovascular risk

- Cyanosis oxygen saturation 85%. In these patients, fetal loss and FGR are common.
- Pulmonary hypertensionl Eisenmenger syndrome maternal mortality is high and pregnancy is contraindicated12,88
- History of arrhythmias atrial tachyarrhythmias increase maternal and to a lesser extent fetal morbidity and mortality
- Ventricular dysfunction if LV (LVEF 40%) andlor RV function' is impaired, maternal morbidity and mortality is increased
- Left heart obstruction peak LV outflow tract gradient 30 mmHg13,14,19

## Management of Pregnancy in Specific Cardiac Disease

Generally; obstetric complications are higher in pregnant women with CHD; up to 339/12,13,14,19,89 Common adverse obstetric events include:

- preterm labour
- premature rupture of membrane
- fetal loss
- postpartum haemorrhage
- pregnancy induced hypertension and preeclampsia

Adverse fetal outcomes are greater in patients with CHD compared to other forms of cardiac disease. 12,13,14,19,89,90 These include:

- prematurity
- fetal growth restriction (FGR)
- stillbirth
- miscarriage
- fetal and perinatal mortality
- risk of having CHD in the fetus (section 3.2, pg. 47)

Fetal risk is also amplified in the presence of obstetric risk factors such as maternal smoking and multiple gestations. 13,14,19

## 4.2.1 Pregnancy care plan

The importance of planned pregnancy cannot be overemphasized The level of maternal and fetal surveillance should be determined prior to the pregnancy.

- Pre-pregnancy cardiac assessment and counseling?   should be stressed (section 3.1, pg. 45, Table IA 1B &amp; 2, pg. 18-21)

Based on the maternal cardiovascular risk, an individualised pregnancy care plan should be instituted (Table 3-5 pg. 22-24, 28)

It is important to note that this risk to mother and fetus is additive It will increase if there are additional obstetric or non-cardiac risk factors such as nulliparity; smoking, NYHA functional class 2 2, and younger maternal age group. 13,14,19,89

## Some specific considerations:

- Preconception counselling: This is important for all patients with known or history of CHD (refer section 3.2, pg. 47).
