- At 36 weeks, warfarin should be switched to:
- LMWH or UFH
- Women on LMWH should be switched to IV UFH at least 36 hours before induction of labour or caesarean section. This should be discontinued 4-6 hours before planned delivery and restarted 4-6 hours after vaginal delivery or 6-12 hours after LSCS there are no bleeding complications
- Oral anticoagulation can be resumed after 24 hours if there are no bleeding concerns
- For patients with mechanical heart valves, a stable INR should be achieved prior to discharge.

## 4.9 Anticoagulation in pregnancy

## 4.9.1 Indications for anticoagulation

The general indications are:

- Mechanical heart valves
- Atrial fibrillation
- Pulmonary arterial hypertension
- Eisenmenger syndrome (use with caution-may have bleeding diathesis)
- Congenital heart disease cyanotic heart disease, Fontan circuit
- Hypercoagulable states e.g. antiphospholipid syndrome
- Previous historylcurrent history of thromboembolic disease

Management of these patients will depending upon the indication vary

## 4.9.1.1 Mechanical heart valves

Prosthetic valve   thrombosis is rare complication with an estimated incidence of 0.1-5.7% per patient year. 195 During pregnancy; this risk is much higher (as high as 10%) due to the prothrombotic state. 196,197 Changes in drug metabolism may also affect control of INR compared to the non-pregnant state

There is no optimal anticoagulation regimen during pregnancy for patients with mechanical heart   valves because therapeutic   option has   its drawbacks. Anticoagulant regimens in pregnant patients with prosthetic heart valves are associated with similar risks of maternal bleeding but with different embolic risks every

The goal of anticoagulation in a patient with mechanical heart valve(s) is to prevent valve thrombosis which is a lethal event for both the mother and the fetus The risk of prosthetic valve thrombosis is lowest (&lt; 4%)198 in patients receiving oral anticoagulants (OAC) and greatest in those receiving subcutaneous unfractionated heparin (UFH) throughout the pregnancy (up to 33%).199 It is about 5-10% in those patients receiving unfractionated heparin (UFH) in the first trimester and OAC in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters

AII anticoagulation regimens also carry an increased risk of fetal teratogenicity; fetal wastage, miscarriage and haemorrhagic complications. 196,198,200-207 Bleeding complications include:

- Intracranial bleeding in the fetus during vaginal delivery if the mother is on OAC as warfarin can cross the placenta\_
- Retroplacental   bleeding   leading to premature birth or fetal death-

Studies consistently show that if warfarin is used in the 1sttrimester there is a lower rate of valve thrombosis but higher fetal losses. 198,208 With  heparin based therapy in the 1st trimester; the reverse occurs. 198,208 In a contemporary study; half of the valve thrombosis occurred in the 1sttrimester and almost all were in patients who were on heparin based therapy.208

- Anticoagulation regimes in these patients with mechanical heart valves should be individualised based on the patients' preferences, maternal and fetal risk.
