## 3.4 Clinical findings of cardiac disease in pregnancy

## 3.4.1 Symptoms and signs

Women with pre-existing undiagnosed cardiac disease and those with new onset (whether or not pregnancy-related) disease, are likely to present normal pregnancy may mimic that of cardiac disease (Table 9, pg. 42) yet

- Thus a careful history and thorough physical examination by the primary healthcare provider are important:.
- History: In the history; the following should be elicited:
- Past medical history of being told to have cardiac murmurs, having undergone cardiac surgery or procedures
- of breathlessness, palpitations, near faints and chest pains In patients with known cardiac disease; the WHO Risk Score and the NYHA Functional Class (Table IA 1B &amp; 2, pg. 18-21) should be documented. History
- Family history of cardiac disease, arrhythmias or sudden death
- Clinical examination: Patients with normal pregnancy may have physiological flow murmurs (Table 9, pg.  42) The following should be performed:
- The heart   rate and blood pressure (BP) should be measured manually.
- Mercury   sphygmomanometer remains the standard   for recording BP in pregnancy: gold
- aneroid devices are available; care should be taken to use those that have been shown to be reliable in pregnancy and to have these calibrated periodically. If only
- Increasingly aneroid devices are being used and the accuracy of these devices are variable4o,41 The automated ones are commonly not reliable in pregnancy.
- In pregnancy; Korotkoff V is taken as the diastolic BP. If the DBP according to this means is zero (up to 15%), then the Korotkov sound IV (the quietening of the blood flow murmur) should be used. If phase IV is used, this should be carefully noted in the clinical records to avoid confusion in future

## General Principles in the Management of Cardiac Disease in Pregnancy

- One should monitor for new onset or change in murmurs
- In patients with breathlessness and in those with known cardiac disease one should examine for signs of heart failure A slight increase in resting heart rate and slightly raised jugular venous may be "normal" findings in a pregnant patient. pulse
- In patients with heart disease in pregnancy; the oxygen saturation should be measured by pulse oximetry. (normal oxygen saturation &gt; 95% on room air)
