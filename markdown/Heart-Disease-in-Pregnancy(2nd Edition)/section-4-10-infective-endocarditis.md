## 4.10 Infective endocarditis in pregnancy

Infective endocarditis (IE) during pregnancy is rare,219,220 but the maternal mortality rate has been reported to be as high as 33%, with a fetal mortality of 29%.220 However, the prognosis has been improving over time. Currently; the maternal mortality ranges still persists at 14.39.221

## 4.10.1 Aetiology

Worldwide; there has been a decrease in rheumatic heart disease as an underlying cardiac risk factor. Survivors of CHD and intravenous drug users are now an increasing risk factor associated with endocarditis.221 In Malaysia; as data is sparse, we can only postulate that rheumatic heart disease is still a significant underlying condition for the occurrence of IE. The most commonly identified pathogens are still streptococci and staphylococci.  The mitral valve is still the commonest valve involved in IEMost cases (about 75%) occur during the second and third trimester. 222

## 4.10.2 Management

- Treatment for IE in pregnancy is similar to the non-pregnant state with multidrug regimes Please refer to the National Antibiotic Guidelines (2014) of the Ministry of Health, Malaysia for current antibiotic recommendations.223 Surgical   intervention has been  reported in about  50% of cases, mostly occurring after delivery.221,222 The timing of surgery is usually delayed until the infection has been eradicated. 224 Most common indications for surgery are:
- refractory sepsis,
- congestive heart failure,
- embolic complications and vegetation size; or
- a combination of the above factors

Wherever   possible; cardiac   operations   should be deferred until after 28 weeks due to possible risks of fetal death related to immaturity. 225
