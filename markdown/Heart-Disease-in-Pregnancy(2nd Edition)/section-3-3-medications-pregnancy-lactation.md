## 3.3 Medications in pregnancy and lactation

The background risk of major birth malformations (such as FGR, fetal death;, anencephaly; mental retardation or a defect requiring surgery for correction etc. ) in the general population; is usually cited as 1-3% of pregnancies. 30 If minor malformations (such as ear tags and extra digits) are also included, the rate may be as high as 7-10%.30 These birth defects may be due to genetic; environmental or yet unknown causes. Drugs have been estimated to account for only 2-3% of these malformations. 30,31

Many maternal medical diseases increase fetal risks. These include conditions such and cancer. During counselling, it is important that the pregnant patient and family understand the:

- background risk of congenital malformations in the general population
- baseline risk associated with the maternal medical condition
- risk, if any; of genetic transmission to the fetus
- risk, if any; associated with the drug

The United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) had formulated Pregnancy Risk Classification; classifying drugs according to the available evidence of safety in pregnancy (Appendix A, pg. 151). From 30t June 2015 however; FDA has proposed a new Pregnancy and Lactation Labelling Final Rule (PLLR) where the previous Risk Categories are replaced with Pregnancy Exposure Registries. 32 This new labelling includes:

- summary of the risks of the drug during pregnancy and lactation using
- relevant   information health care providers make prescribing decisions and counsel women about the use of drugs during pregnancy and lactation
- a discussion of the data supporting that summary; and

The information on drug safety in pregnancy is limited. About 91% of the medications approved by FDA for use in adults from 1980-2010 lacked sufficient data to determine the risk of birth defects if used during pregnancy. 33

Some medications may be more detrimental in different   stages of the pregnancy. First trimester exposure to Angiotensin Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (ACE-I) has not always been linked to adverse outcomes although fetotoxicity has been clearly documented during 2nd and 3rd trimester exposure. 34-38
