## 4.8 Heart failure in pregnancy

The treatment of heart failure (HF) in pregnant women is more difficult than in non-pregnant women; and should always involve multidisciplinary team approach\_ 194 The HF may develop for the first time in pregnancy or pregnancy may occur in patient who had HF previously and still has a depressed myocardial function. (LVEF 409)

Common causes for the development of new onset HF in pregnancy are:

- Peripartum cardiomyopathy or other cardiomyopathies (section 4.6, pg. 100)
- Decompensation in patient with known cardiac disease due to the stress of the pregnancy

## 4.8.1 Preconception counselling

Patients with LVEF 30% and those in NYHA Class Ill and IV should be strongly advised termination should be considered. Table IA 1B &amp; 2, pg. 18-21)

## 4.8.2 Antenatal care

The principles of management of HF in pregnancy are similar to that in the non-pregnant state and as outlined in the 3rd Ed Malaysian CPG on Management of Heart Failure. 164 There are however a few exceptions:

- Nitrates andlor Hydralazine are used for preload and afterload reduction
- Diuretics are the first line therapy in patients who are fluid overloaded
- ß-blockers can be used cautiously
- Digoxin is safe in pregnancy
- Ivabradine should not be used in pregnancy
- Angiotensin   Converting Enzyme Inhibitors   (ACE-I) and Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARB) are contraindicated in pregnancy. ACE-I (enalapril and captopril) can be used in the postpartum period.
- Spironolactone is best avoided (FDA Category C) in pregnancy and during breast feeding

## 4.8.4 Postpartum care

After delivery; careful monitoring of haemodynamic status should be done for at least 24 h, or longer in high risk patients. In patients with severe cardiac lesions, haemodynamics may be abnormal up to 10 after delivery. days

- These   patients should be evaluated postpartum to assess  the need for corrective surgery.
- The risk of recurrence of HF in subsequent pregnancies should also be made known to the patient (section 4.6.1.1, pg. 101)

Follow-up visit at 6 weeks postpartum should be attended by the multidisciplinary full cardiac assessment should be done, and appropriate contraception should be advised.

## message: Key

- Patients with LVEF 30% and those in NYHA Class Ill and IV should be strongly advised not to get pregnant
- Heart failure that develops during pregnancy can be managed with the judicious use of diuretics, digoxin; nitrates; ß-blockers andlor hydralazine.
