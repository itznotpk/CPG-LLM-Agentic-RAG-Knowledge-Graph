## Key Points for Anticoagulants and Antiplatelet in Regional Anaesthesia

1. **Perioperative use of anticoagulants and antiplatelets in providing Regional Anaesthesia are to follow current practice guidelines.**

2. **Considerations should be made to balance the risk and benefits of use of anticoagulants and antiplatelet perioperatively.**

---

## 5.3.1 Background

Bleeding complications following the use of either neuraxial or peripheral nerve block procedures are a **rarity**.

## 5.3.2 Evidence-Based Guidelines

Current advisory states that the use of various regional anaesthesia (RA) approaches in patients who are on concurrent anticoagulant and antiplatelet therapy, is to follow **latest evidence-based guidelines** to enhance safety.

## 5.3.3 Risk-Benefit Assessment

**Risk-benefit assessment should be considered** in patients on anticoagulant or antiplatelet agents when RA is considered.

### Factors to Consider

| Risk Factors | Benefit Factors |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Type and intensity of anticoagulation | Quality of anaesthesia and analgesia |
| Time since last dose | Opioid-sparing effects |
| Type of block (neuraxial vs peripheral) | Reduced systemic complications |
| Patient's bleeding history | Patient preference |
| Anatomical considerations | Avoidance of general anaesthesia |

## 5.3.4 Neuraxial Procedure Guidelines

**Reference guide to minimise risk of spinal hematoma with neuraxial procedures:**
https://med.stanford.edu/content/dam/sm/pain/documents/neuraxial-procedure-v2-3.26.19.pdf

### General Principles

1. **Know the pharmacokinetics** of each anticoagulant/antiplatelet agent

2. **Calculate appropriate time intervals** between last dose and block insertion

3. **Calculate appropriate time intervals** between block removal and next dose

4. **Document catheter insertion and removal times**

5. **Monitor for signs and symptoms of spinal haematoma:**
   - New or progressive motor weakness
   - Sensory deficits
   - Bowel or bladder dysfunction
   - Back pain

6. **Maintain high index of suspicion** - early recognition is critical for neurological recovery
