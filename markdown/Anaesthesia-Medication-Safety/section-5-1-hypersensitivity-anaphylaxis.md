> **Context:** This section covers perioperative hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis, including diagnosis using mast cell tryptase, ANZAAG grading, PEN-FAST scoring for penicillin allergy, β-lactam cross-reactivity, HKLAAC referral pathway, managing multiple drug allergies, food allergies relevant to anaesthesia, and urgent surgery in suspected allergy cases.

---

## Key Points for Perioperative Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis

1. **Serum mast cell tryptase level should be done** when perioperative anaphylaxis is suspected to confirm diagnosis.

2. For patients with **suspected antibiotic allergy, risk stratification using Penicillin Allergy Clinical Decision Rule (PEN-FAST)** should be performed.

3. **Choice of alternative antibiotics** should be based on estimated cross-reactivity rate, type of surgery, and National Antimicrobial Guideline.

4. **There is no evidence to avoid propofol** in egg, soy, or peanut allergy.

5. Patients with **previous suspected perioperative anaphylaxis not yet investigated** presenting for urgent surgeries - avoid all prior exposures if known.

6. **Regional anaesthesia should be the mode of choice** wherever applicable.

7. If general anaesthesia is necessary, **NMBAs should be avoided if possible**.

---

## 5.1.1 Introduction

a. Perioperative hypersensitivity (POH) reactions are **unexpected, unpredictable and potentially life-threatening** during surgery and anaesthesia.

b. **Severity ranges from mild to severe** (see Table 4 below). Extreme cases may be fatal despite prompt treatment.

c. **Estimated incidence:** 1 in 18,600 to 1 in 353 anaesthetic procedures with geographical variability.

d. **Most common causes worldwide:** Neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBA) and antibiotics.

e. **Life-threatening anaphylaxis (grades 3-4):** Estimated 1 in 10,000 anaesthetic procedures.

### Table 4: ANZAAG Grading of Perioperative Anaphylaxis

| Grade | Clinical Features |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Grade 1** | Mucocutaneous signs only (erythema, urticaria, peripheral angioedema) |
| **Grade 2** | Multi-organ manifestations: mucocutaneous signs combined with hypotension, brady/tachyarrhythmia, and/or bronchospasm |
| **Grade 3** | Life-threatening hypotension and/or high airway pressure requiring immediate specific treatment to avoid progression to cardiac arrest or significant hypoxia |
| **Grade 4** | Cardiac arrest |

## 5.1.2 Management of Perioperative Hypersensitivity Reactions

**Three key steps depending on severity:**
1. Timely diagnosis
2. Appropriate dosing of adrenaline (depends on severity)
3. Appropriate intravascular volume replacement

**Management guides:**
- https://anzaag.com/anaphylaxis-management/management-resources/
- https://anaesthetists.org/Portals/0/PDFs/QRH/QRH_3-1_Anaphylaxis_v5.pdf

## 5.1.3 Serum Mast Cell Tryptase (MCT)

MCT level should be done when perioperative anaphylaxis suspected to confirm diagnosis.

**Testing location:** Institute for Medical Research (IMR) and some private laboratories.

### Blood Sampling Protocol

| Sample | Timing |
|--------|--------|
| **First sample** | Following stabilisation, within 60 min up to 3 hours post reaction |
| **Second sample** | 24 hours post reaction |

**Sample handling:**
- Place blood in plain serum tubes
- Label with date and time of sampling
- Send immediately to hospital pathology lab for proper storage
- Store at 4°C for not longer than 4 days before sending to IMR

## 5.1.4 Referral to Anaesthetic Allergy Clinic, Hospital Kuala Lumpur (HKLAAC)

### Indications for Referral

- Unexplained cardiac arrest during anaesthesia
- Unexplained, unexpected hypotension requiring active treatment (especially if only responded to adrenaline)
- Unexplained, unexpected bronchospasm with significant desaturation, relatively resistant to treatment
- Generalised rash, flushing or urticaria
- Exhibited angioedema

### Referral Process

1. Preferably first MCT sample sent prior to referral
2. Phone and email consultation: Tel 03-26155555 (ext.1133/1153), Email: hklaac@gmail.com
3. Provide:
   - Completed HKLAAC referral form (Appendix 2)
   - Description of reaction and timing
   - Blood test details and timing
   - Contact details (anaesthesiologist, surgeon, patient - at least 2 phone numbers)
   - Legible softcopies of GA form, vital signs, recovery room/PACU chart, drug charts

## 5.1.5 Managing Suspected POH Cases

a. **Previous uninvestigated perioperative reaction is a known risk factor** for recurrence.

b. **Refer to HKLAAC for investigations.** Postpone elective procedures until results available.

c. **Skin testing performed 4-6 weeks post reaction.** Risk of false negatives if performed earlier.

d. Report issued to patient and referring anaesthesiologist/surgeon after testing.

e. **Patient receives:**
   - Medic Alert bracelet application form
   - ADR and Allergy Card application forms for Pharmacy Department

f. **If causative agent identified:** Avoid it and follow HKLAAC recommendations.

g. **Latex or disinfectant allergy:** Educate patient to inform all healthcare personnel.

h. **NMBA allergy:** Avoid causative NMBA and cross-reactive NMBAs. If negative to alternative NMBA, can use with caution.

## 5.1.6 Managing Suspected β-lactam Antibiotic Allergy

a. **Unconfirmed penicillin allergy** results in poorer outcomes, increased resistant infections, prolonged hospitalisation.

b. **Careful evaluation of antibiotic allergy history** including prior tolerance is essential.

c. **Use PEN-FAST risk stratification** to identify low-risk patients not requiring formal allergy testing.

d. **PEN-FAST score ≥3:** Refer to allergist/immunologist or dermatologist for skin testing.

e. **If surgery urgent or referral not feasible:**
   - Avoid penicillin or cephalosporin group
   - Use alternative non-β lactam antibiotics where indicated

### Table 5: β-Lactam Cross-Reactivity in Penicillin-Allergic Patients

| Drug Class | Estimated Cross-Reactivity |
|------------|---------------------------|
| 1st Gen Cephalosporins (cefadroxil, cephalexin) | 1.9 - 7.9% |
| 2nd Gen Cephalosporins (cefuroxime, cefoxitin) | 1.9% |
| 3rd Gen Cephalosporins (ceftriaxone, cefotaxime, ceftazidime) | 0.7% |
| 4th/5th Gen Cephalosporins (cefepime, ceftaroline) | Not available |
| Carbapenem (imipenem, meropenem, ertapenem) | ≤1% |
| Monobactam (aztreonam) | Negligible (except with ceftazidime - identical side chains) |

f. **Dual allergy (reaction with another cephalosporin with dissimilar side chain)** occurs in ~4% of patients.

g. **Cross-reactivity with 3rd/4th-gen cephalosporins and carbapenems is low/very low.**

h. **Antibiotic test dose not effective** in preventing anaphylaxis (NAP6). Administer antibiotics **prior to induction** to simplify treatment and diagnostic workup.

## 5.1.7 Managing Cases with Multiple Allergies

a. **Multiple drug allergies** = reactions to two or more chemically unrelated drugs.

b. **Patients with hypersensitivity to many drugs in same class** (e.g., antibiotics, NSAIDs) should NOT be considered as multiple drug allergies.

c. Patients labelled as multiple drug allergies **might have underlying chronic urticaria** (spontaneous or inducible).

d. **Referral pathway:**
   - Refer directly to allergist/immunologist or dermatologist for investigation
   - Only refer to Anaesthetic Allergy Clinic when multiple allergies include IV anaesthetic agents, local anaesthetics, or other perioperative agents

e. Patients with chronic urticaria usually on antihistamines and/or steroids. **Continue these perioperatively** to prevent flare-ups.

---

## 5.1.8 Managing Cases with Food Allergies

| Food Allergy Concern | Evidence-Based Guidance |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Egg, soy, or peanut allergy** | **No evidence to avoid propofol** |
| **Fish or shellfish allergy** | NOT related to iodine |
| **Seafood allergy** | **No evidence to avoid iodinated drugs** |
| **Fish allergy / NPH insulin use** | **No evidence to avoid protamine** (usual precautions apply) |

## 5.1.9 Managing Suspected POH Cases Planned for Urgent Surgeries

### Information Gathering

a. **Try to get as much information** about the reaction and anaesthetic procedure as possible.

b. **If prior exposure information available:** Avoid all exposures before the reaction and use alternatives whenever possible.

### When No Information is Available

| Strategy | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Alternative technique** | Regional anaesthesia may be technique of choice (fewer drugs used) |
| **Minimise IV agents** | Use as few drugs as possible |
| **General anaesthesia** | Avoid NMBAs if possible |
| **When NMBA absolutely indicated** | Exercise caution and vigilance |
| **Avoid if possible** | Latex and chlorhexidine |
| **Penicillin allergy suspected** | Avoid penicillin/cephalosporin group; use non-β lactam alternatives |

### NMBA Considerations (When Absolutely Indicated)

Insufficient Malaysian data; international published data suggests:

| NMBA | Risk Level |
|------|------------|
| **Suxamethonium, Rocuronium** | Higher risk of anaphylaxis |
| **Pancuronium, Cis-atracurium** | Lowest risk |

**Cross-reactivity patterns:**
- Most frequently observed with rocuronium
- Less frequently with cis-atracurium
- Suxamethonium anaphylaxis cross-reacts with cis-atracurium in 10% and rocuronium in 20%
- Cross-reactivity between atracurium and cis-atracurium: ~50%

### Additional Measures for Urgent Surgery

- **Resuscitation drugs and equipment** must be readily available
- **Pre-treatment with steroids and antihistamines** may be considered (no strong evidence of prevention)
