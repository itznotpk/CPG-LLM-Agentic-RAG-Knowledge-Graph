## Key Points for Acute Pain Service

1. **Institutions should implement formal protocols and quality assurance programmes** to safely individualise pain management.

2. **Effective communication with the primary team** prevents double prescriptions.

3. **Patients on opioids should be closely monitored** for potential complications.

4. **Institutions should standardise PCA equipment** to minimise medication errors and ensure only authorised providers access and program pumps.

5. **Familiarity with epidural infusion protocols, proper handover, anticoagulation timing consideration, and readiness with resuscitation equipment/medications** should be done to prevent and manage potential complications.

6. **Epidural infusion lines and syringes are to be clearly labelled "For Epidural Use Only,"** stored separately from other infusions, and connected promptly by the clinician to minimise wrong-route administration risk.

## 3.3.1 Acute Pain Management in the Ward

a. **Institutions should have formal protocols and quality assurance programmes** for effective acute pain management.

b. **Communication with primary team is crucial** to prevent double prescription.

c. **Patients needing individualised therapy:**
   - Sleep-disordered breathing
   - Renal or hepatic impairment
   - Opioid tolerance or substance use disorder
   - Cognitive behavioural conditions

d. **Regular assessments and documentation** of analgesia, monitoring, and adverse effects are crucial to safely individualise treatment regimens.

e. **All patients on opioids must be monitored for opioid-induced ventilatory impairment.**

f. Medications in multimodal analgesia regimens should not increase risk of adverse effects or interactions with other analgesic medications.

g. **Routine prescription of modified release opioids is best avoided** unless:
   - Demonstrated need exists
   - Close monitoring is available
   - Cessation plan is in place

h. **Implementation of standardised monitoring and protocols** in various pain management techniques is recommended.

## 3.3.2 Patient-Controlled Analgesia (PCA)

a. **Anaesthesia healthcare professionals and ward staff should be familiar with:**
   - PCA pump use
   - Routes of administration
   - Institutional protocols for PCA
   - Monitoring for potential complications

b. **Limit variability of PCA equipment** within institution to minimise medication error.

c. **Meticulous programming required:**
   - Initial loading dose
   - PCA dose
   - Lockout interval
   - Continuous infusion rate
   - One and four-hour limits

d. For intravenous PCA, **medication line must be connected to fluid infusion line with anti-reflux valves**.

e. **Access to medication and pump programming** should only be by anaesthesia healthcare providers with key or code to pump (to prevent tampering).

f. **Routinely check PCA devices** for compromise to locking mechanisms or pump malfunction.

g. Acute pain service team should **verify orders and ensure correct drug delivery**.

## 3.3.3 Epidural Infusion in Ward

### a. Personnel, Staffing and Ward Environment

i. **Staff should be familiar with:**
   - Epidural infusion in ward
   - Institutional protocols
   - Monitoring for potential complications

ii. **Responsibility for epidural infusion lies with initiating practitioner** who must ensure effective handover to ward staff before transfer.

iii. **Anticoagulation considerations:**
   - Dose, timing, and therapeutic effect must be considered during insertion and removal of catheter
   - Also when instituting anticoagulation while epidural is in situ

iv. **Resuscitation readiness:**
   - Oxygen
   - Resuscitation equipment
   - Specific medications (20% Intralipid emulsion for LAST, naloxone) must be readily available

> **Note:** 20% Intralipid is critical for treating Local Anaesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST). Initial bolus: 1.5 ml/kg over 1 min, followed by infusion at 0.25 ml/kg/min. Maximum dose: 12 ml/kg. Watch for LAST signs: circumoral numbness, tinnitus, seizures, cardiovascular collapse.

### b. Epidural Drug Administration

i. **Epidural infusion system should be closed** with no injection ports. Anti-bacterial filter must be at junction of epidural catheter and infusion line.

ii. Bolus injection should use pump or strict aseptic technique and **only by trained staff**.

iii. **Epidural infusion lines should be clearly marked.**

iv. **Limited range of epidural solutions** should be agreed and approved through local formulary.

v. **Epidural infusions labelled "For Epidural Use Only"** and stored separately from other infusions.

vi. **Use lowest effective concentration of LA** to preserve motor function.

vii. **Connect epidural infusions to catheter as soon as possible** by clinician to minimise wrong-route administration errors.

viii. **Regularly examine insertion site** for catheter migration, leaks, and inflammation.
