## Key Points for Local Anaesthesia and Nerve Injury

1. **All local anaesthetics are potentially neurotoxic** with the ability to reduce neural blood flow in a concentration- and time-dependent manner.

2. **Neurotoxicity is made worse with the concomitant use of epinephrine.**

3. **Use the lowest concentration of local anaesthesia solution** to achieve the desired block effect.

---

## 5.4.1 Introduction

a. The incidence of postoperative prolonged neurological deficit is generally **very low**.

b. **All local anaesthetics are potentially neurotoxic** with the ability to reduce neural blood flow in a concentration- and time-dependent manner.

c. **Highest risk** occurs with the use of high concentration LA with concomitant use of epinephrine.

d. **Axonal damage may occur**, particularly if there is:
   - Intra-fascicular injection
   - High concentration
   - Prolonged duration of exposure

e. **Neurotoxicity comparison:**
   - Ester group LAs are more toxic than amide group
   - Ropivacaine has the **lowest potential for neurotoxicity**

---

## 5.4.2 Mitigation of LA-related Risk of Peripheral Nerve Injury

### a. Proper Block Technique

Proper conduct of block procedures reduces the risk of peripheral nerve injury (PNI) secondary to LA injection.

### b. Use Lowest Effective Concentration

The **least concentrated LA solution to achieve an efficient block** should be administered.

| Indication | Recommended Concentration |
|------------|--------------------------|
| **Surgical anaesthesia** | 0.5% Ropivacaine - usually efficacious with favourable recovery profile |
| **Perioperative analgesia** | 0.375% Ropivacaine - usually efficacious in multimodal analgesia setting |

### c. Caution with Epinephrine

The use of epinephrine in peripheral nerve blocks is associated with **increased risk of ischaemic axonal injury**.

**Suggested uses for epinephrine (use with caution):**
- Adjunct to reduce rapid absorption with large doses of LA
- Intravascular marker to aid early detection of LAST

**When avoiding epinephrine:**
- Blocks near compromised vascular supply
- Patients with peripheral vascular disease
- Digital nerve blocks
- When using high concentration LA

## Comparative Neurotoxicity of Local Anaesthetics

| Local Anaesthetic | Relative Neurotoxicity |
|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Ropivacaine** | Lowest |
| **Levobupivacaine** | Low |
| **Bupivacaine** | Moderate |
| **Lidocaine** | Moderate |
| **Procaine (ester)** | Higher |
| **Chloroprocaine (ester)** | Higher |

---

## Risk Factors for LA-Induced Nerve Injury

| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Concentration** | Higher concentration = higher risk |
| **Duration** | Prolonged exposure increases risk |
| **Injection site** | Intra-fascicular injection = highest risk |
| **Needle type** | Short-bevelled needles may reduce fascicular penetration |
| **Vasoconstrictor** | Epinephrine increases ischaemic risk |
| **Pre-existing neuropathy** | Increased susceptibility |
| **Diabetes mellitus** | Increased susceptibility |
