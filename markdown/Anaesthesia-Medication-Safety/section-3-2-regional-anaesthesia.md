> **Context:** This section covers safe use of medication in regional anaesthesia, including local anaesthetic dosing, neuraxial and peripheral nerve block workflow, contamination prevention, and LAST recognition.

---

## Key Points for Regional Anaesthesia

1. **A standardised and safe institutional regional anaesthesia workflow should be adopted** to reduce variation in practice and minimize errors from various stages of drug handling.

2. **Use of Local Anaesthetics (LA) for specific indication must take into consideration the volume and concentration** of such preparation.

3. **The maximum dose of LA drug mass is based on milligram-per-lean body weight (mg/kg).**

4. **Monitoring and vigilance are key elements** whenever any LA preparations are used.

---

## 3.2.1 Safe Administration of Drugs in Regional Anaesthesia (RA)

### General Measures

Steps and processes from purchasing, storage and supply should be adopted for all drugs including those used in regional anaesthesia, as specified in **Section 2: Key Principles of Safe Use of Medication in Anaesthesia**.

### Specific Measures Related to RA Techniques

| Measure | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Standardised workflow** | Adopt institutional RA workflow to reduce variation and minimize errors |
| **Pre-procedural checklist** | Use standard institutional checklist for RA |
| **Minimise distractions** | 'Sterile cockpit' concept during drug preparation and administration |
| **Direct drawing** | Drugs and diluent solutions drawn directly into syringes from ampoule - **never via gallipot/container** |
| **Sterile dilution** | Dilution performed in sterile field during procedure, prior to administration |
| **Two-person checking** | Recommended when practitioner draws from ampoule held by assistant |
| **Pre-packed sterile labels** | Use when available |
| **Consistency without labels** | If sterile labels unavailable, maintain consistency in syringe sizes, order of preparation, and use of medications |
| **Work surface layout** | Consistent layout should be institutional routine |
| **Sterile packaging** | Ampoules or prefilled syringes in sterile packs recommended to increase safety and reduce contamination |

### Contamination Prevention

1. In absence of sterile packaging, **clean ampoule necks with alcohol before breaking** to minimise bacterial contamination.

2. Risk of glass particle contamination is minimised by using **particulate filter straws**.

3. For continuous infusion, **pre-prepared sterile solutions by hospital pharmacies are preferred**.

4. Pre-prepared sterile solutions can be stored at **4-21°C for up to 14 days** while retaining physical-chemical characteristics without bacterial contamination.

5. **Replace LA solution after 72-hour infusion** as microbiological stability is maintained for 72 hours.

### Antisepsis Application

**Meticulous application of antisepsis solution recommended:**
- Avoid dripping, splashing or contamination of LA injectate
- Both chlorhexidine and alcohol are **neurotoxic**
- **Allow to air-dry thoroughly (2-3 minutes)** before block needle insertion

## 3.2.2 Dosing and Dilution of Local Anaesthetic for Regional Anaesthesia

### Volume and Concentration Considerations

The volume and concentration of LA prepared must consider:
- Block intention (anaesthesia vs analgesia)
- Desired onset
- Duration
- Recovery profile

### Maximum Dose

**The maximum dose of LA drug mass is based on milligram-per-lean body weight (mg/kg).**

### Special Situations

**Peri-articular knee injection:**
- Use of LA beyond maximum dose based on lean body weight has been safely described for ropivacaine

**Tumescent anaesthesia (Plastic & Reconstructive Surgery):**
- Similar for lignocaine use

### Monitoring Requirement

**Monitoring and vigilance are key elements for early detection and treatment of local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) in these situations.**

---

## LAST Recognition and Management (Essential Information)

### Signs and Symptoms of LAST

| CNS Symptoms (Early) | Cardiovascular Symptoms (Late) |
|---------------------|-------------------------------|
| Circumoral numbness | Hypotension |
| Metallic taste | Bradycardia |
| Tinnitus | Arrhythmias |
| Visual disturbances | Conduction blocks |
| Agitation/seizures | Cardiovascular collapse |

> **Warning:** Cardiovascular collapse can occur without warning CNS symptoms, especially with bupivacaine.

### Immediate LAST Management

1. **STOP** local anaesthetic administration
2. **Call for HELP** and request **Lipid emulsion 20%**
3. **Maintain airway** with 100% oxygen
4. **Treat seizures** with midazolam (avoid large propofol doses in CV compromise)
5. **Start lipid emulsion therapy** if cardiovascular instability

### Lipid Emulsion 20% Protocol

| Phase | Dose |
|-------|------|
| **Initial bolus** | 1.5 ml/kg over 1 minute |
| **Infusion** | 0.25 ml/kg/min |
| **Repeat bolus** | Two further boluses at 5-min intervals if CV instability persists |
| **Maximum total dose** | 12 ml/kg |

**Resuscitation drugs and equipment including Lipid emulsion 20% must be immediately accessible in all locations where LA is administered.**
