## Key Points for Section 4

1. **Use child's current body weight** for precise dosing and follow standard protocols to minimise medication errors.

2. **Dose adjustment among elderly** is based on age-related changes and organ function.

3. **Vigilant and mindful observation for complications** after drug administration in children and elderly is important.

4. **Adherence to strict protocols among obstetric patients** when administering and titrating epidural and neuraxial analgesia ensures safety, especially in high-risk obstetric patients.

5. **Anaesthetic drug dosing needs to be tailored to patients' weight categories** when managing extreme body weights and use objective monitoring to ensure effective and safe drug administration.

---

## 4.1 Paediatric Patients

1. **Use patient's body weight in kilograms.** Refrain from relying on estimated or past weights unless it's a life-threatening situation where delay could lead to serious harm.

2. **Utilise standard concentration and dilution** of drugs according to body weight groups to reduce medication errors.

3. **Identify patients at risk of respiratory depression:**
   - Premature infant <60 weeks post conceptual age
   - Obstructive sleep apnoea
   
   Tailor perioperative management accordingly.

4. **Off-label use of drugs** in paediatrics should follow institutional regulatory requirements.

---

## 4.2 Elderly / Older Patients

1. **Ageing is associated with pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic changes.** Physiologic reserve is reduced, and organ systems may be further compromised during surgical stress.

2. **Identify safe dosage ranges** taking into consideration organ function (e.g., renal or liver impairment).

3. **Age should be correctly set** in monitoring and drug delivery devices that use age adjustment (e.g., TCI pump and MAC).

4. **Preoperative frailty should be assessed** to determine safe use of medication or additional monitoring required.

5. **Considerations to prevent postoperative delirium or cognitive dysfunction:**
   - Avoid excessive anaesthetic depth during general anaesthesia
   - Avoid excessive sedation during regional anaesthesia
   - Avoid extremes of blood pressure and fluctuations
   - Avoid cerebral desaturation

6. **Use perioperative benzodiazepines and gabapentinoids judiciously.** Multimodal opioid-sparing techniques for postoperative pain management is recommended. **Dexmedetomidine** is recognised to have neuroprotective effects and considered to reduce delirium perioperatively.

---

## 4.3 Obstetric Patients

### 4.3.1 Epidural Labour Analgesia

a. **Only anaesthesia healthcare professionals** are allowed to titrate epidural labour analgesia dosage.

b. **Dilution of epidural labour analgesia concentration should be standardised** according to individual hospital protocol to reduce medication errors.

c. **Pre-prepared epidural labour analgesia cocktail by hospital pharmacy** is recommended.

### 4.3.2 Central Neuraxial Block for Caesarean Section

a. **Reduce dose of spinal anaesthesia in morbidly obese patients** as they are at risk of higher block.

b. **Do not administer morphine (intrathecal or epidural)** to patients with:
   - Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)
   - Morbid obesity

c. **No other sedative or parenteral opioids in first 24 hours** after receiving intrathecal or epidural morphine.

---

## 4.4 Extreme Body Weight Patients

### 4.4.1 General Principles

a. Extreme body weight (obese or underweight) is associated with **pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic changes**.

b. **Anaesthetic drug dosing should be modified** according to:
   - Lean body weight
   - Adjusted body weight
   - Ideal body weight
   - Total body weight
   
   *Depending on the drug used.*

c. **Anaesthetic drugs should be titrated to effect** whenever appropriate.

### 4.4.2 Obesity

a. In obese patients, **volumes of distribution, binding and elimination are unpredictable**.

b. Theoretically, lipophilic drugs have larger volume of distribution. However, changes are drug-specific, so generalisations are difficult.

c. This uncertainty necessitates **focus on clinical effect and titrate-to-effect**. Use of **objective clinical monitoring** (depth of anaesthesia, neuromuscular monitors) is useful.

d. **Hydrophilic drugs (e.g., NMBAs)** distributed mainly in central compartment → use **lean body weight** to calculate dose. Exception: **Total body weight for suxamethonium** (due to increased plasma cholinesterase activity).

e. **Multimodal opioid-sparing analgesia techniques** including regional anaesthesia are highly recommended. If opioids required, use short-acting agents when possible.

### 4.4.3 Underweight and Sarcopenia

a. With reduced total body mass, albumin concentration and volume of distribution, **careful drug dosing is crucial** to avoid overdose or toxicity.

b. **Consider reducing neuromuscular blocking drug dose** due to possible increased sensitivity. Use of neuromuscular monitors is recommended.

c. **Neostigmine can cause life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias** in severely malnourished patients.
