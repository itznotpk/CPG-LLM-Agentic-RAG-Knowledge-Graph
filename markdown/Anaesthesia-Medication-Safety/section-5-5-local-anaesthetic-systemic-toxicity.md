> **Context:** This section covers Local Anaesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST), including prevention strategies, recognition of symptoms, and the complete emergency management protocol with lipid emulsion therapy.

---

## Key Points for Local Anaesthetic Systemic Toxicity

1. **Monitoring and vigilance are key elements** whenever any local anaesthetic preparations are used.

2. **Resuscitation drugs and equipment including Lipid emulsion 20% must be accessible immediately.**

3. **Severity of clinical presentation is determined by the level of free plasma concentration** which are affected by several pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic factors.

4. **Practices that mitigate risk reduction shall always be employed** whenever any local anaesthetics are being administered.

5. **Caution with concurrent use of epinephrine.**

---

## 5.5.1 Introduction

a. Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity is a **rare but potentially fatal complication**.

b. When administering LA, anaesthesia healthcare professionals need to **remain vigilant and monitor for LAST**. With large volume and/or continuous infusion techniques, maintain a **high index of suspicion**.

c. It is a **joint responsibility of anaesthesia healthcare professionals and surgeons** to ensure the LA dose administered does not exceed the total safe dose.

d. **For every location where LA is administered**, resuscitation drugs and equipment including **Lipid emulsion 20%** must be made immediately accessible.

> **LAST Management Guide:** See Appendix 4 for detailed management protocol.

---

## 5.5.2 General Principles to Reduce the Risk of LAST

a. **Severity of clinical presentation** is determined by the level of free plasma concentration of LA, which is a balance between drug absorption and clearance.

b. **Free plasma levels are affected by several factors:**

| Factor Type | Examples |
|-------------|----------|
| **Drug factors** | Type of LA, addition of epinephrine, dose, pharmacokinetics (metabolism and excretion) |
| **Patient factors** | Age, comorbidity, pregnancy, pharmacodynamics (sensitivity and muscle mass) |
| **Technique factors** | Site of injection, use of ultrasound guidance, catheter technique |

c. **High lipid solubility LAs** are more potent but have higher risk of toxicities.

d. **CC/CNS Ratio (Cardiovascular Collapse to CNS toxicity ratio):**
   - Higher ratio = higher safety margin
   - **Levobupivacaine and ropivacaine** have higher safety margins than bupivacaine

---

## 5.5.3 Specific Principles to Reduce the Risk of LAST

### a. Use Ultrasound Guidance

The use of ultrasound for regional anaesthesia reduces risk of LAST by **nearly four-fold**.

### b. Weight-Based Dosing

Dosing in LA should be based on **lean body weight**.

### c. Lowest Effective Dose

Use the **lowest effective dose** based on:
- Body mass
- Site of injection
- Patient's age
- Medical history

### d. Dose Reductions (10-20%)

**Consider dose reductions when:**
- Using continuous techniques or requiring multiple boluses
- Extremes of age
- Cardiac dysfunction with decompensated heart failure
- Parturients
- Renal impairment with uraemia or acidosis
- Liver disease with concomitant cardiac or renal disease
- Metabolic disorders (diabetes mellitus, mitochondrial disease, carnitine deficiency, malnourishment)

### e. Injection Technique

**Inject in small volume aliquots with gentle aspiration before each injection:**

| Block Type | Recommendation |
|------------|----------------|
| **Peripheral nerve block** | Aliquots of <5 mL with 30-45 seconds pause between injections (longer in low cardiac output states) |
| **Epidural catheter** | Gentle aspiration prior to administration; test dose may detect intravascular catheter placement |

### f. Vigilance Throughout

LAST may still occur due to large volumes of LA infusion or catheter migration. **Vigilance must be practised throughout the duration of epidural catheter use.**

### g. Epinephrine Considerations

Addition of epinephrine in LAs may serve as:
- Intravascular marker of injection
- Prolonging duration of action of LA
- Reducing risk of LAST

**Test dose indicator:** 15 μg/ml of epinephrine in LA solution will increase:
- Heart rate by ≥10 beats per minute, OR
- Systolic blood pressure by ≥15 mmHg

*Note: Users must be aware of its limitations as a safety measure.*

## Signs and Symptoms of LAST

| CNS Symptoms (Early) | Cardiovascular Symptoms (Late) |
|---------------------|-------------------------------|
| Circumoral numbness | Hypotension |
| Metallic taste | Bradycardia |
| Tinnitus | Arrhythmias |
| Visual disturbances | Conduction blocks |
| Agitation | Asystole |
| Muscle twitching | Cardiovascular collapse |
| Seizures | |
| Loss of consciousness | |

> **Important:** CNS symptoms typically precede cardiovascular symptoms, but cardiovascular collapse can occur without warning in severe cases.

---

## LAST Management Protocol

### Immediate Management

**Step 1: STOP Local Anaesthetic Administration**

**Step 2: Call for HELP**
- Alert the team
- Request lipid emulsion 20%
- Prepare for resuscitation

**Step 3: Maintain Airway**
- Administer 100% oxygen
- Secure airway if needed
- Hyperventilate to correct acidosis

**Step 4: Treat Seizures (if present)**

| First Line | Alternative |
|------------|-------------|
| Midazolam | Propofol (small doses) |

**Avoid large doses of propofol in cardiovascular compromise**

**Step 5: Cardiovascular Support**

For cardiac arrest: Start CPR, use standard ACLS protocols with modifications:
- **Reduce adrenaline doses** (≤1 mcg/kg)
- **Avoid amiodarone and lidocaine**

### Lipid Emulsion 20% Therapy

| Phase | Dose |
|-------|------|
| **Initial bolus** | 1.5 ml/kg over 1 minute |
| **Infusion** | 0.25 ml/kg/min |
| **Repeat bolus** | Two further boluses at 5-min intervals if CV instability persists |
| **Increase rate** | Double to 0.5 ml/kg/min if needed |
| **Maximum total dose** | 12 ml/kg |

**Continue infusion for at least 10 minutes after cardiovascular stability restored.**

### Post-Resuscitation Care
- ICU admission for monitoring (minimum 12 hours)
- Document incident thoroughly
- Report to risk management
