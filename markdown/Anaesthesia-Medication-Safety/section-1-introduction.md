## 1.1 Background

Safe and effective use of medications is of paramount importance in anaesthesiology and critical care. Medications play a crucial role in:
- Inducing unconsciousness
- Managing pain
- Facilitating procedures
- Stabilising critically ill patients

**Unsafe medication practices and medication errors are the leading causes of avoidable harm in healthcare across the world.** Key statistics:
- 5% of all patients admitted to a hospital experience a medication error
- An average hospital has one medication error every 23 hours or every 20 admissions
- Medication errors result in severe patient harm, disability, and even death

Medication use in anaesthesiology and critical care is unique because the process of prescription, dosage calculation, preparation, administration, documentation, monitoring, and managing potential adverse effects are performed in a continuum treatment period in an environment where typical medication safety processes may require modification.

Often, medications are administered during emergencies in time-sensitive situations. The complexity and diversity of medications used in these settings pose high-stake challenges, requiring anaesthesia healthcare professionals to employ critical personal and team-based practices and techniques to mitigate the risk of medication-related errors.

---

## 1.2 Purpose

The primary purpose of this guideline is to **enhance patient safety** by providing anaesthesia healthcare professionals with:
- Evidence-based recommendations
- Educational resources to enhance knowledge and skill in safe medication management

Where standardising practices are recommended, consistency in medication preparation, dosing, administration, and monitoring should reduce variability in care and medication-related errors.

Protocols and streamlining medication workflows optimise work efficiency by ensuring timely and appropriate medication treatment. This improves patient outcome and throughput.

---

## 1.3 Scope

This guideline aims to provide guidance in the safe use of medication in anaesthesiology and critical care for anaesthesia healthcare professionals trained in this field across a diverse range of locations and facilities.

While this guideline references established international and national guidelines and protocols, it is specifically tailored for application in the **Malaysian healthcare context**.

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Anaesthesia healthcare professionals** | Specialists, medical officers, nurses and/or medical assistants trained in anaesthesiology and critical care |
| **Anaesthesia care** | Provision of various forms of anaesthesia, analgesia, sedation and critical care by trained professionals |
| **Medication use** | Medication practices directly affecting patient safety |

### Medication Safety Scope

The term 'medication use' in this guideline addresses:
- Medication practices directly affecting patient safety
- Drug storage and work processes unique to anaesthesiology and critical care
- Secure storage and proper disposal of abuse-potential medications
- Minimising environmental pollution through proper disposal of anaesthetic drugs

> **Note:** This guideline is tailored to local settings for use by anaesthesia healthcare professionals in Malaysia as of 2024. It is not exhaustive and does not supersede any institutional guidelines. It will be reviewed every five years or earlier when needed.
