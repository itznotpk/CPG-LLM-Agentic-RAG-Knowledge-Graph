> **Context:** This section covers malignant hyperthermia (MH) — a rare but potentially fatal anaesthetic emergency — including diagnostic features, management protocols, dantrolene reconstitution, and trigger-free anaesthesia.

---

## Key Points for Malignant Hyperthermia

1. **The diagnostic features are unexplained, unexpected increase in ETCO₂, heart rate and temperature.**

2. **Management focuses on stopping the process and treating its effects.**

3. **Patients with confirmed or suspected history should be provided with trigger-free anaesthesia.**

4. **Availability of dantrolene in the hospitals or the nearest hospital should be known.**

5. **Malignant hyperthermia cart should be available in hospitals that provide GA.**

6. **All suspected MH with Larach's score rank 4 and above should be reported to the malignant hyperthermia database in Hospital Kuala Lumpur.**

---

## 5.2.1 Introduction

a. Malignant hyperthermia (MH) is a **rare but potentially fatal anaesthetic emergency**.

b. **Estimated incidence:** 1:10,000 to 1:150,000 general anaesthetics. Genetically susceptible individuals are at risk when exposed to inhalational anaesthetics or suxamethonium.

c. The **cardinal clinical features** result from excessive carbon dioxide (CO₂) production due to skeletal muscle hypermetabolism.

### Diagnostic Features of MH

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **ETCO₂** | Unexplained, unexpected increase |
| **Heart rate** | Unexplained, unexpected increase |
| **Temperature** | Unexplained, unexpected increase |

---

## 5.2.2 Management of Malignant Hyperthermia

a. Management of suspected MH focuses on:
   - **Stopping the MH process**
   - **Treating the effects of MH**

b. **Management reference:** https://www.moh.gov.my/moh/resources/Penerbitan/Anestesiologi/Malignant_Hyperthermia.pdf

### Dantrolene Availability

c. Anaesthesia healthcare professionals should know **where dantrolene is kept** in their respective hospitals. In hospitals where dantrolene is not available on site, know where the nearest hospital has dantrolene.

d. **MSA website** has an updated list of hospitals where dantrolene is available: https://www.msa.net.my/index

e. All hospital pharmacies that do not have dantrolene should have established understandings with nearest hospitals regarding **immediate access to dantrolene** when needed.

### MH Cart Requirements

f. All hospitals providing GA are recommended to have the **MH cart**, including written instructions on dantrolene reconstitution.

### Dantrolene Reconstitution

| Step | Instructions |
|------|-------------|
| **Presentation** | 20 mg ampoules formulated with 3 g of mannitol |
| **Mixing** | Each vial mixed with 60 ml of sterile preservative-free distilled water |
| **Faster dissolution** | Empty several ampoules into a sterile dish and add a large volume of sterile water |
| **Prewarming** | Prewarming (not to exceed 38°C) the sterile water will speed solubilisation |
| **Minimum stock** | MOH Malaysia recommends at least 12 vials on the MH cart |

## 5.2.3 Malignant Hyperthermia Database in Hospital Kuala Lumpur

a. Currently there is **no MH registry in Malaysia**.

b. The Department of Anaesthesiology & Critical Care, Hospital Kuala Lumpur (HKL) keeps a database of voluntarily reported suspected MH cases since 2017.

c. Currently this database is not available for reference in managing patients with histories of suspected MH.

d. **Anaesthesia healthcare professionals are encouraged to report suspected MH** to HKL using the form in Appendix 3.

e. **All suspected MH with Larach's score-rank 4 and above should be reported** to the MH database in HKL.

### Patient Counselling

f. The primary anaesthesiologist should issue an **alert card or letter** to the patient and family regarding suspected MH. Counsel patient/family on confirmation of MH diagnosis by genetic testing or in vitro contracture testing (not available in Malaysia).

### Trigger-Free Anaesthesia

g. **All anaesthesia healthcare professionals should be familiar with trigger-free anaesthesia** for patients with history of confirmed or suspected MH.

**Triggers to avoid:**
- All volatile/inhalational anaesthetic agents (sevoflurane, desflurane, isoflurane)
- Suxamethonium

**Safe alternatives:**
- Total intravenous anaesthesia (TIVA)
- Regional anaesthesia
- Non-depolarising neuromuscular blocking agents
