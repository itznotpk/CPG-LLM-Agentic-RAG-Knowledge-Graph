> **Context:** This section covers safe waste management of anaesthetic medications, including disposal of controlled substances, sharps, and the environmental impact of waste anaesthetic gases (WAGs).

---

## Key Points for Section 6

1. **Safely discard used vials, ampoules, and syringes** in designated containers, following institutional protocols to prevent contamination and injury.

2. **Ensure controlled substances and hazardous drugs are disposed of** according to institutional regulations and environmental guidelines, avoiding potential abuse and minimising environmental harm.

3. **Use scavenging systems to vent waste anaesthetic gases (WAGs)** from the operating room and ensure adequate air exchange to reduce accumulation.

4. **In areas without scavenging systems, alternatively activated charcoal canisters should be used for WAGs.**

---

## 6.1 Disposal of Vials, Ampoules and Syringes

### 6.1.1 Responsibility

**Safely discard all used vials, ampoules, and syringes after each case.** It is the responsibility of anaesthesia healthcare professionals to discard items that had been used.

### 6.1.2 Partially Used Items

All partially used ampoules and syringes filled with drugs should be disposed appropriately to **minimise the risk of drugs being inadvertently administered to the next patient**.

### 6.1.3 Institutional Protocols

The proper disposal of sharps and contaminated items should follow **institutional protocols**.

### 6.1.4 Glass Ampoules

If recycling is not an option, used glass ampoules or bottles pose a **risk of sharp injury** and should be placed in a sturdy sharp bin/container appropriate for disposal.

### 6.1.5 Syringes with Needles

**Syringes with attached needles shall be discarded into sharps containers as one unit.**

---

## 6.2 Proper Disposal of Drugs

### 6.2.1 Controlled Substances

The disposal of medications with potential for diversion and abuse (opioids, benzodiazepines, propofol) should be carried out to **minimise this risk**:

| Action | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Witness disposal** | Required for unused controlled substances |
| **Document disposal** | In compliance with institutional regulations |
| **Leftover controlled substances** | Empty into medical waste bins (yellow bin) |
| **Empty vials** | May be kept for Dangerous Drug inventory according to institutional practice |

### 6.2.2 Environmental Considerations

Medications used in anaesthesia should be disposed of in a manner that **reduces potential environmental harm**. Follow:
- Hospital waste management policy
- Local legislative requirements

### 6.2.3 Propofol Disposal

**Important environmental considerations for propofol:**

| Issue | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Environmental persistence** | Unmetabolised propofol does not degrade in the environment |
| **Soil mobility** | Highly mobile in soil |
| **Bioaccumulation** | Accumulates in fat |
| **Aquatic toxicity** | Toxic to aquatic life |

**Proper disposal:**
- Unused propofol (in syringes, ampoules or infusion lines) should be disposed into the hospital waste management system to be **incinerated**
- **DO NOT empty propofol into the sink**

---

## 6.3 Scavenging, Inhalational Agent Absorber, Carbon Dioxide Absorber and Operating Room Environment Pollution

### 6.3.1 Waste Anaesthetic Gases (WAGs)

WAGs are vented to the outdoor atmosphere **virtually not metabolised**.

### 6.3.2 Minimising WAG Accumulation

| Method | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Scavenging system** | WAGs from breathing circuit should be vented out from OR through a scavenging system |
| **Air exchange rate** | Minimum total of 15 air changes per hour with minimum of 3 air changes of outdoor air per hour |

### 6.3.3 Remote Areas Without Scavenging

In remote areas where there is no scavenging system available, **canisters filled with activated charcoal** should be used for WAGs disposal.

**Note:** Activated charcoal canisters will adsorb halogenated anaesthetic gases but **NOT nitrous oxide**.

### 6.3.4 Nitrous Oxide Environmental Impact

Nitrous oxide is discharged to the environment by the scavenging system and is responsible for **most of the ozone depletion in anaesthesia practice**.

### 6.3.5 Carbon Dioxide Absorbers

Used carbon dioxide absorbers are disposed of in the **medical waste (yellow) bin**.

### 6.3.6 Medical Waste Handling

Handling and disposal of medical waste including the above wastes mentioned are to follow **institutional waste management protocols**.
