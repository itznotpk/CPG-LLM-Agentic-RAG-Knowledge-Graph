## Key Points for Section 6

1. **Patient's current medication should be reviewed and continued when necessary with clear instructions given to patient/guardian and nursing staff.**

2. **The anaesthesiologist should have working knowledge of drug pharmacology and potential adverse interactions with anaesthetic agents.**

3. **Pre-operative medication may include sedatives and acid aspiration prophylaxis.**

---

## 6.1 Review of Current Medication

The patient's current medication should be:
- **Reviewed**
- **Continued when necessary**

Clear instruction should be given to:
- Patient/guardian
- Nursing staff

**Documentation:** This should be documented in the patient's anaesthetic record.

---

## 6.2 Drug Interactions

The anaesthesiologist should have a **working knowledge** of the pharmacology of the patient's medication.

**Important considerations:**
- Potential adverse interactions of these drugs with anaesthetic agents should be recognized
- The anaesthetic technique should be **modified if necessary**

---

## 6.3 Premedication Drugs

Pre-operative medication may be prescribed to facilitate anaesthetic management:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Sedative premedication** | Benzodiazepines, opioids |
| **Acid aspiration prophylaxis** | H2 blockers, proton pump inhibitors, antacids |

---

## Common Premedication Regimens

### Adult Patients

| Drug | Dose | Route | Timing |
|------|------|-------|--------|
| Midazolam | 7.5-15 mg | Oral | 30-60 min pre-op |
| Lorazepam | 1-2 mg | Oral | Night before/morning of surgery |

### Acid Aspiration Prophylaxis (High-Risk Patients)

| Drug | Dose | Indication |
|------|------|------------|
| Ranitidine | 150 mg PO | Night before + morning |
| Omeprazole | 40 mg PO | Night before + morning |
| Sodium citrate | 30 ml | Immediately pre-induction |

> **Note:** Acid aspiration prophylaxis is particularly important for emergency surgery, obstetric patients, and those with gastroesophageal reflux.
