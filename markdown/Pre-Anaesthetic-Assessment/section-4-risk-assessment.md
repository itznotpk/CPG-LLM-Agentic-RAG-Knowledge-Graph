> **Context:** This section covers risk assessment, stratification, and disclosure, including the ASA Physical Status Classification, risk-benefit judgment, counselling high-risk patients, and factors for risk disclosure.

---

## Key Points for Section 4

1. **Multiple factors contribute to peri-operative outcome: patient's condition, surgical complexity, urgency, surgical skill, and anaesthesia factors.**

2. **The ASA physical status classification provides useful predictive value for overall operative mortality.**

3. **High-risk patients should be counselled about risks; alternative surgical and anaesthetic options should be discussed and documented.**

4. **Severity of complications (e.g., permanent disability or death) should be discussed even if unlikely to occur.**

---

## 4.1 Factors Affecting Peri-operative Outcome

The patient's pre-operative condition is not the only determinant of peri-operative outcome. Other contributing factors include:

| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Patient condition** | Pre-operative health status and co-morbidities |
| **Surgical complexity** | Nature and extent of procedure |
| **Urgency of surgery** | Elective vs emergency |
| **Surgical skill** | Technical expertise |
| **Anaesthesia factors** | Technique, monitoring, management |

---

## 4.2 ASA Physical Status Classification

The **American Society of Anaesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification** provides a useful means to convey information regarding the patient's pre-operative condition and has been found to have some **predictive value** when applied to overall operative mortality.

| ASA Class | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| ASA I | Normal healthy patient |
| ASA II | Patient with mild systemic disease |
| ASA III | Patient with severe systemic disease |
| ASA IV | Patient with severe systemic disease that is a constant threat to life |
| ASA V | Moribund patient not expected to survive without surgery |
| ASA VI | Declared brain-dead patient whose organs are being removed for donor purposes |

> **Note:** 'E' is added for emergency procedures (e.g., ASA IIIE)

---

## 4.3 Risk-Benefit Judgment

It is imperative that the anaesthesiologist be **knowledgeable and well-informed** to make a balanced judgment with regard to the risk-benefit ratio of anaesthesia and surgery for the high-risk patient.

---

## 4.4 Severity Consideration

The **severity of a particular complication** (e.g., one which results in permanent disability or death) should be considered in the discussion **even though it may be unlikely to occur**.

---

## 4.5 Counselling High-Risk Patients

Patients with **significant co-morbid conditions and high anaesthetic risks** should be counselled about such risks. The following should be addressed:

- Alternative surgical options
- Alternative anaesthetic options
- Discussion with patient and/or next-of-kin
- **Documentation** in consent form and patient's case notes

---

## 4.6 Factors for Risk Disclosure to Patients

| Factor | Consideration |
|--------|---------------|
| **Nature of matter disclosed** | More likely and more serious risks require disclosure |
| **Nature of procedure** | Complex intervention typically requires more information |
| **Patient's desire for information** | Patients who ask questions make known their desire for information and should be told |
| **Temperament and health** | Anxious patients and those with health problems or relevant circumstances (medical condition, occupation) may need more information |
| **Surrounding circumstances** | Information for elective procedures may be different from emergency settings |

---

## 4.7 Documentation of Risk Discussion

All risk discussions should be documented:
- In the **consent form**
- In the **patient's case notes**
- Discussion of **alternative surgical and anaesthetic options**

### Consent Requirements

A **separate, written consent for anaesthesia** should be obtained after:
- Due explanation of planned procedure
- Discussion of alternative options
- Risk information disclosure
- Patient questions answered

The consent form should be **signed, witnessed and dated**.

> **Validity:** Consent is valid for no more than 7 days. A fresh consent is required if circumstances change or procedure is delayed.
