> **Context:** This section covers detecting disease and assessing severity through history, physical examination, investigations (refer Table I: Routine Investigations, refer Table II: Investigations by Clinical Indication), ASA classification, and preoperative preparation.

---

## Key Points for Section 3

1. **Medical history should include concomitant conditions, medications (including herbal supplements), allergies, previous anaesthesia, and family history of anaesthetic complications.**

2. **Cardiovascular, respiratory, and airway examination are essential; other systems require attention as guided by history.**

3. **Investigations should be selectively performed for purposes of guiding or optimizing peri-operative management.**

4. **Only appropriate investigations deemed to have an influence on the patient's management strategies and outcome should be carried out.**

---

## 3.1 History

a. A patient's medical history provides **vital information** to identify disease that may affect peri-operative outcome.

b. Medical history should include:
   - Concomitant medical conditions
   - Past and current medication (**including herbal supplements**)
   - Alleged compliance to treatment
   - **Drug and food allergies**
   - Previous anaesthesia
   - Family history of anaesthetic complications

c. System review should focus on those **pertinent to anaesthesia and surgery**.

d. **Menstrual history** may be important in women of child-bearing age.

e. Useful information may be obtained from the patient's family doctor, relatives or previous medical records.

---

## 3.2 Physical Examination

a. Examination of the patient is an **essential part** of the pre-anaesthetic assessment.

b. Important systems for assessment:

| System | Priority |
|--------|----------|
| **Cardiovascular** | Essential |
| **Respiratory (including airway)** | Essential |
| Renal | As guided by history |
| Hepatic | As guided by history |
| Endocrine | As guided by history |
| Central Nervous | As guided by history |

c. The pathology relevant to surgery is a pertinent part of physical examination. Examples include:
   - Site, size and vascularity of tumour mass
   - Features of airway compression/obstruction
   - Endocrine abnormalities

---

## 3.3 Investigations

a. Investigations complement pre-anaesthetic history and physical examination. They should be **selectively performed** for purposes of guiding or optimizing peri-operative management.

b. Pre-anaesthetic investigations may be indicated for their impact on peri-operative anaesthetic care, such as:
   - Identification of a disease or disorder
   - Verification or assessment of a known disease, disorder or medical therapy
   - Formulation of specific anaesthetic plans and alternatives

c. The nature and extent of pre-anaesthetic investigations depend on:
   - Patient's age
   - Presence of any co-morbid condition
   - Nature and extent of the planned surgical procedure

d. **Routine investigations** (See Appendix) are solely based on age and nature of surgery, for asymptomatic patients without abnormal clinical findings.

e. In an era where **cost containment** is important, factors like cost-benefit and benefit-risk ratios will have to be taken into consideration.

> **Important:** See investigation tables below for routine and indicated tests.

---

## Table I: Routine Investigations (Asymptomatic Patients)

| Routine Investigation | Indication |
|-----------------------|------------|
| **Full blood count (FBC)** | Age > 60 years OR Major surgery* |
| **Renal profile** | Major surgery* |
| **Electrocardiogram (ECG)** | Male > 40 years OR Female > 50 years |
| **Chest X-ray (CXR)** | Age > 60 years OR Major thoracic/upper abdominal surgery |
| **Liver function test (LFT)** | Major surgery* in patients > 50 years |

> ***Major surgery** is empirically defined as one in which the cranium, thorax or abdomen is opened or when the anticipated blood loss is significant and exceeds 15% of total blood volume.

---

## Table II: Investigations by Clinical Indication

| Investigation | Indications |
|---------------|-------------|
| **Full blood count** | Anaemia, haematological disease, renal disease, chemotherapy |
| **Renal profile** | Renal/liver/cardiovascular disease, diabetes, abnormal nutrition, diarrhoea/vomiting, bowel prep, drugs affecting electrolytes |
| **Blood glucose** | Diabetes mellitus, severe liver disease |
| **ECG** | Heart disease, hypertension, chronic pulmonary disease, diabetes, renal disease |
| **Chest X-ray** | Significant respiratory disease, cardiovascular disease, malignancy |
| **ABG** | Septic/debilitated patients, moderate-severe pulmonary disease, respiratory difficulty, thoracic surgery |
| **Coagulation screen** | Haematological disease, severe liver disease, coagulopathy, anticoagulants, intrathoracic/intracranial procedures |
| **Lung function test** | Thoracic surgery, moderate-severe pulmonary disease |
| **LFT** | Hepatobiliary disease, alcohol abuse, tumour with liver metastases |
| **Thyroid function test** | Thyroid surgery, thyroid disease history, suspected endocrine abnormalities |
| **Cardiac function tests** | Significant cardiovascular disease, atypical cardiac symptoms, significant haemodynamic disturbances |

### Investigation Validity Periods

| Investigation | Validity |
|---------------|----------|
| FBC, Urea, Renal profile, Blood glucose | 1 week |
| ECG | 1 month |
| CXR | 6 months |

---

## 3.4 Others

a. Multi-disciplinary management, subspecialty referral and medical record retrieval may be helpful in the overall assessment of the patient.

b. Consideration should also be made with regards to the nature, extent and anticipated duration of surgery. If in doubt, it is essential to **communicate with the surgeon** to seek further clarification.

---

## 3.5 ASA Physical Status Classification

For risk stratification based on assessment findings:

| ASA Class | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| ASA I | Normal healthy patient |
| ASA II | Patient with mild systemic disease |
| ASA III | Patient with severe systemic disease |
| ASA IV | Patient with severe systemic disease that is a constant threat to life |
| ASA V | Moribund patient not expected to survive without surgery |
| ASA VI | Declared brain-dead patient for organ donation |

> **Note:** 'E' is added for emergency procedures (e.g., ASA IIIE)

---

## 3.6 Preoperative Preparation and Management

| Preparation | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Smoking cessation | Advice on cessation |
| Respiratory | Chest physiotherapy, breathing exercise, incentive spirometry |
| Mobility | Limb physiotherapy |
| Nutrition | Nutritional support, including haematinics |
| DVT prevention | Deep vein thrombosis prevention measures |
| Pain management | Peri-operative pain management planning |
| Fasting | Instruction on fasting requirements |
| Medications | Instruction on continuation or withholding of current medication |
| Blood products | Arrangement with Blood Bank - GXM, group and screen as appropriate |
| Post-op care | Arrangement for ICU or HDU admission if indicated |
