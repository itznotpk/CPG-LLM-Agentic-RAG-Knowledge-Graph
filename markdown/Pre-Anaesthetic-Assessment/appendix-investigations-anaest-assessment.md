> **Context:** This appendix contains the recommended pre-anaesthetic investigations (refer Table I: Routine Investigations for Patients Undergoing Anaesthesia, refer Table II: List of pre-anaesthetic investigations and their indications), validity periods for test results, and criteria for repeating investigations.

The nature and extent of pre-anaesthetic investigations depend on the patient's age, presence of any co-morbid condition, as well as the nature and extent of the planned surgical procedure.

Routine investigations, listed in Table I: Routine Investigations for Patients Undergoing Anaesthesia, are solely based on age and nature of surgery, for asymptomatic patients without abnormal clinical findings. For healthy patients undergoing short, minimally invasive procedures, investigations may not be necessary. Investigations listed in Table II: List of pre-anaesthetic investigations and their indications are for patients with abnormal clinical findings.

Table I: Routine Investigations For Patients Undergoing Anaesthesia
| Routine Investigation | Indication |
|---|---|
| Full blood count | Age > 60 yr<br>Major surgery* |
| Renal profile | Major surgery* |
| Electrocardiogram | Male > 40 yr<br>Female > 50 yr |
| Chest X ray | Age > 60 yr<br>Major thoracic / upper abdominal surgery |
| Liver function test | Major surgery* in patients > 50 yr |

*\*Major surgery is empirically defined as one in which the cranium, thorax or abdomen is opened or when the anticipated blood loss is significant and exceeds 15% of total blood volume.*

| Renal profile | 1. Renal disease<br>2. Liver diseases<br>3. Cardiovascular disease<br>4. Metabolic disease, e.g., diabetes mellitus<br>5. Abnormal nutritional states<br>6. Drugs that may alter electrolyte balance or exhibit enhanced toxic effects in the presence of electrolyte abnormality, e.g., digitalis, diuretics, corticosteroids |
| Blood glucose concentration | 1. Diabetes mellitus<br>2. Severe liver disease |
| Electrocardiogram (ECG) | 1. Heart disease, hypertension or chronic pulmonary disease<br>2. Diabetes mellitus<br>3. Renal disease |
| Chest X-ray (CXR) | 1. Significant respiratory disease<br>2. Cardiovascular disease<br>3. Malignancy |
| Arterial blood gases (ABG) | 1. Debilitated or septic patients<br>2. Moderate to severe pulmonary disease<br>3. Patients in respiratory difficulty<br>4. Patients scheduled for thoracic surgery |
| Coagulation screen | 1. Haematological disease<br>2. Severe liver disease<br>3. Coagulopathy due to any cause<br>4. Patients on anticoagulant therapy<br>5. Intra-thoracic/Intra-cranial procedures |
| Lung function test | 1. Patients scheduled for thoracic surgery<br>2. Moderate to severe pulmonary disease |
| Liver function test | 1. Hepatobiliary disease<br>2. History of alcohol abuse<br>3. Tumour with possible metastases to the liver |
| Thyroid function test | 1. Thyroid surgery<br>2. History of thyroid disease<br>3. Suspected endocrine abnormalities, e.g., pituitary tumour |
| Cardiac function tests | 1. Significant cardiovascular disease<br>2. Patients with atypical cardiac symptoms which pose a problem to diagnosis<br>3. Patients with significant haemodynamic disturbances |

Normal investigation results are valid for varying periods of time, ranging from 1 week (FBC, urea, renal profile, blood glucose concentration), 1 month (ECG) to 6 months (CXR).

Investigations should be repeated under the following circumstances:
- Appearance of fresh symptoms, such as chest pains, diarrhoea, vomiting
- Assessment for effectiveness of therapy, such as potassium supplement for hypokalaemia, insulin therapy for hyperglycaemia, dialysis for patients with renal failure, blood products for correction of coagulopathy
