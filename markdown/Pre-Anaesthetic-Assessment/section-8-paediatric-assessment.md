## Key Points for Section 8

1. **Pre-anaesthetic assessment for paediatrics includes birth history (for <6 months), developmental milestones, and feeding patterns.**

2. **A child with active RTI (fever, purulent discharge) should have surgery postponed for at least 2 weeks.**

3. **Clear fluids can be given up to 2 hours before surgery in children.**

4. **Non-drug measures (parental presence, rapport building) may reduce the need for sedative premedication.**

---

## 8.1 Pre-anaesthetic Assessment

A pre-anaesthetic visit is important for the anesthesiologist to:
- Get acquainted with the patient and family
- Gain their confidence
- Reassure the frightened child and anxious parents
- Explain the planned anaesthetic procedure, fasting, induction method, and post-operative pain management

Pre-anaesthetic evaluation of infants and children is similar to that of adults with additional features.

---

## 8.1.1 History (Additional Paediatric Features)

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Birth history** | For children <6 months: gestational age and weight, maternal health during gestation, events during labour/delivery, Apgar scores, neonatal hospitalization |
| **Developmental milestones** | Age-appropriate milestones |
| **Feeding pattern** | Breast/bottle feeding, solid food intake |
| **RTI history** | Recent respiratory tract infection, croup, asthmatic episodes |

---

## 8.1.2 Physical Examination (Paediatric)

| System | Features to Look For |
|--------|---------------------|
| **General** | Appearance, weight, height |
| **Congenital** | Any congenital anomalies |
| **Airway** | Potential airway/intubation problem |
| **Respiratory** | Evidence of RTI, signs of reactive airway disease |
| **Cardiovascular** | Presence of cardiac murmurs |
| **Neurological** | Abnormalities in muscle tones and strength, developmental milestones |
| **IV access** | Potential sites for venepuncture - mark out for EMLA application if IV induction planned |

---

## 8.1.3 Laboratory Investigations (Paediatric)

- Should be considered according to the **physical condition of the child** and the **nature of the surgery**
- Value of routine tests is questionable when surgical procedures do not involve significant blood loss
- **Minor surgery in a healthy child usually does not require any investigation**
- Group and cross-match blood and plasma if indicated

---

## 8.2 A Child with Respiratory Tract Infection (RTI)

### 8.2.1 Peri-operative Problems with RTI

- Laryngospasm
- Bronchospasm
- Airway obstruction by secretion

### 8.2.2 Age Factor

Incidence of peri-operative respiratory complications is **greater in infants less than 1 year old**.

### 8.2.3 Decision to Proceed

Recommendations for anaesthesia in a child with RTI depends on:
- Severity of RTI
- Nature of surgery (elective vs emergency, minor vs major)

### 8.2.4 Mild RTI - May Proceed

| Finding | Assessment |
|---------|------------|
| No fever | ✓ |
| Clear nasal discharge | ✓ |
| Mild cough | ✓ |
| Child active | ✓ |
| Feeding well | ✓ |

**Decision:** Can be anaesthetized for **minor surgical procedure without intubation**

### 8.2.5 Active RTI - Postpone

| Finding | Assessment |
|---------|------------|
| Fever | ✗ |
| Recent onset purulent nasal discharge | ✗ |
| Cough | ✗ |

**Decision:** Surgery should be **postponed for at least 2 weeks**

## 8.3 Pre-operative Fasting (Paediatric)

It has been shown that drinking clear fluid up to 2 hours before surgery does not increase residual gastric volume.

### Benefits of Liberal Clear Fluid Policy

- Decreases incidence of preoperative dehydration and possible hypotension during induction
- Prevents hypoglycaemia
- Results in less agitated child and happier parent

### College of Anaesthesiologists Guideline (2008)

| Substance Ingested | Fasting Time (hours) |
|--------------------|---------------------|
| **Clear fluids*** | 2 |
| **Breast milk** | 4 |
| **Formula milk, solids** | 6 |

*Clear fluids include water, glucose drink, cordial drink, clear fruit juice*

## 8.4 Premedication (Paediatric)

### 8.4.1 Purpose

Premedication is to relieve patient's anxiety and provide tranquility before the operation.

### 8.4.2 Individualization

Premedication must be individualized based on:
- Child's age and weight
- Expectation and emotional maturity
- Personality and anxiety level
- Co-operation
- Physiological and psychological status

### 8.4.3 Ideal Premedication Characteristics

| Characteristic | Requirement |
|----------------|-------------|
| Safety | Be safe |
| Administration | Easy to administer in acceptable formulation - **no intramuscular injection** |
| Onset | Rapid level of short term sedation |
| Separation | Facilitate smooth separation from parents |
| Induction | Facilitate smooth anaesthetic induction |
| Side effects | Minimal undesirable side effects |

### 8.4.4 Common Paediatric Premedication Drugs

| Drug | Dose | Route |
|------|------|-------|
| **Midazolam** | 0.5-0.7 mg/kg | PO |
| **Diazepam** | 0.2 mg/kg | PO |

### 8.4.5 Non-Drug Factors

While sedative premedication may be used to provide anxiolysis, other **non-drug factors** are equally important:
- Visit by the anaesthesiologist to establish rapport
- Explanation of the anaesthetic procedure
- **Allowing parents to accompany the child** to the operating theatre

> **Note:** These measures may reduce the need for sedative premedication.
