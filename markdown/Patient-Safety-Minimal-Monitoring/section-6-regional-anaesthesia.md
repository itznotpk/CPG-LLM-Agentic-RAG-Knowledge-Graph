## Key Points for Section 6

1. **Patients receiving regional anaesthesia must have the equivalent standard of care and monitoring as those undergoing general anaesthesia.**
2. **Pre-operative assessment should specifically enquire into contraindications for regional blocks.**
3. **Patient refusal is an absolute contraindication for regional anaesthesia.**
4. **Ultrasound guidance and nerve stimulators are encouraged for safe conduct.**

---

## 6.1 Equivalent Standard of Care

Patients who undergo regional anaesthesia **must receive the equivalent standard of care and monitoring** as for those undergoing general anaesthesia throughout the perioperative period.

### Required Intraoperative Monitoring Standards (from Section 3)

| Parameter | Minimum Standard |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Oxygenation** | **Mandatory** pulse oximetry with variable tone and audible low alarm limit. |
| **Circulation** | Observation of pulse, heart rate, and blood pressure (recorded regularly). **Continuous ECG display** is required. |
| **Ventilation** | Clinical observation (chest wall excursion, auscultation). |
| **Temperature** | Required when changes are intended, anticipated, or active warming is used. Continuous for neonates and paediatrics. |

---

## 6.2 Pre-operative Assessment for Regional Block

Apart from the routine preoperative assessment, **enquiry into any possible contraindications** to regional anaesthesia should be undertaken.

### Absolute Contraindications

- **Patient's refusal is an absolute contraindication** for regional anaesthesia.

---

## 6.3 Safe Conduct of Regional Blocks

**Ultrasound guidance with or without a nerve stimulator** is encouraged for the safe conduct of regional blocks when necessary.
