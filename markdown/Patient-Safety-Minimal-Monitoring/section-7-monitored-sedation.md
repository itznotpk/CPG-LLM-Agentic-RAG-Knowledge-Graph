## Key Points for Section 7

1. **Sedating a patient aims to bring about anxiolysis, amnesia, and cooperation.**
2. **Informed consent must be obtained prior to any form of sedation.**
3. **The person administering sedation must be knowledgeable in pharmacology, monitor the patient, and not assume the role of the operator.**
4. **The procedure location must be equipped to deal with cardiopulmonary emergencies.**
5. **A written record of drug dosages and monitored vital signs is mandatory.**

---

## 7.1 Objectives and Assessment

The main objectives of sedating a patient are to:
- Bring about anxiolysis
- Produce a degree of amnesia
- Maintain co-operation of the patient so that uncomfortable diagnostic and minor surgical procedures may be performed

A patient who is to be given any form of sedation for a procedure should be assessed and an **informed consent** must be obtained.

---

## 7.2 Qualifications and Roles

The person administering sedation:
- Must have **adequate knowledge of the pharmacology** of drugs used.
- Should be able to detect and manage appropriately any complications due to the actions of these drugs.
- Should be the one monitoring the patient.
- Must **not** assume the additional role of the operator.

---

## 7.3 Facilities and Equipment Requirements

The procedure should be performed in a location which is suitable in size and environment. It should be staffed and equipped to deal with any cardiopulmonary emergency. 

### Mandatory Facilities:
- An operating table or trolley which can be tilted to Trendelenburg position
- Adequate lighting and suction equipment
- Supply of oxygen and oxygen delivery devices
- Equipment to monitor the patient i.e., blood pressure (BP), pulse-oximeter, heart rate, and electrocardiogram (ECG).
- *The use of respiratory monitors such as capnography or chest wall impedance is highly recommended.*
- Equipment for laryngoscopy, endotracheal intubation, and ventilation
- Appropriate drugs for cardiopulmonary resuscitation and equipment for administration of drugs.

---

## 7.4 Documentation and Monitoring

A written record of the time and dosages of the drugs used must be kept as part of the patient's records. This record must also note the **monitored values of the patient's vital signs**.

---

## 7.5 Recovery and Handover Protocols

a. **Handover Protocol:** There should be a protocol on the handing over of care of the patient from the operating room staff to the recovery room staff, and from the recovery room staff to the ward staff or discharge. This should include further instructions on monitoring and management of expected and unexpected complications.

b. **Medical Assistance:** Extra medical staff should be immediately available in case of an emergency.

c. **Discharge Review:** The patient should be reviewed by the doctor before discharge from the recovery room.
