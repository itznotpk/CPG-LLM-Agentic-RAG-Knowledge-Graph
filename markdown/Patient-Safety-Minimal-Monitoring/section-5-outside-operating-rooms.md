> **Context:** This section covers anaesthesia administered outside operating rooms (e.g., radiology suites, MRI rooms, radiotherapy), requiring equivalent monitoring standards and suitable adaptations for hazardous environments.

---

## Key Points for Section 5

1. **Anaesthesia outside the operating room may be associated with greater risks due to physical separation, hazardous environments, or inability to use certain monitors.**
2. **Patients are entitled to receive the equivalent standard of care and monitoring as they would within the operating room.**
3. **Skilled and dedicated assistance is essential during general anaesthesia.**
4. **Suitable adaptations of techniques and equipment are required in hazardous environments.**

---

## 5.1 Risk Assessment and Vigilance

a. In many instances where anaesthetics are administered outside the operating room environment, the anaesthesia may be associated with **greater risks to the patient**. This may be due to:
   - Physical separation from the patient (e.g., radiological suites)
   - Specific hazardous environment (e.g., radiotherapy)
   - Inability to use certain monitors (e.g., MRI rooms)

b. For this reason, **greater vigilance** by the anaesthesiologist is required to maintain safety for the patient.

---

## 5.2 Standards of Care and Monitoring

Patients are entitled to receive the **equivalent standard of care** of monitoring during anaesthesia and recovery as they would receive within the operating room.

### Required Intraoperative Monitoring Standards (from Section 3)

| Parameter | Minimum Standard |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Oxygenation** | **Mandatory** pulse oximetry with variable tone and audible low alarm limit. For GA, continuous monitoring of oxygen concentration. |
| **Circulation** | Observation of pulse, heart rate, and blood pressure (recorded regularly). **Continuous ECG display** is required. |
| **Ventilation** | Clinical observation. **Capnography is mandatory** for general anaesthesia. |
| **Temperature** | Required when changes are intended, anticipated, or active warming is used. Continuous for neonates and paediatrics. |
| **Neuromuscular Function** | Peripheral nerve stimulator available if muscle relaxants are used. |
| **Anaesthetic Depth** | MAC indicator for inhalational agents. Monitors like BIS/Entropy if clinically indicated. |

---

## 5.3 Assistance and Recovery

a. **General Anaesthesia Support:** Where general anaesthesia is provided, **skilled and dedicated assistance** for the anaesthesiologist is essential.

b. **Recovery Areas:** Appropriate locations with adequate monitoring facilities need to be identified for the post-anaesthetic recovery of the patient.

---

## 5.4 Hazards and Adaptations

Where close physical monitoring is not possible due to specific hazards (e.g., radiation, magnetic resonance imaging), **suitable adaptations of techniques and equipment** to monitor the patient are required to provide an adequate level of anaesthetic care and safety for the patient.
