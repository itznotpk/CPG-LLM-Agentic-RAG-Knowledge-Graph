## Algorithm 1: Growth Hormone Therapy In Children

**Overview:** This algorithm guides the evaluation and treatment of children with suspected growth hormone deficiency (GHD), Turner syndrome (TS), and small for gestational age (SGA). It begins with identifying which children should be referred for specialist evaluation.

**Step 1: Identify Entry Category**

Three categories of children warrant referral to a paediatric endocrinologist:

**Category A — Suspect GHD** *(after exclusion of systemic diseases, skeletal disorders, hypothyroidism, and Turner syndrome)*
- Short stature (height <3rd percentile OR <target height range), AND
- Slow growth (defined as downward deviation of height >1 percentile band over not less than 12 months), AND
- Delayed bone age

**Category B — Turner Syndrome**
- Short stature (height <3rd percentile) AND/OR slow growth, AND
- Chronological age <12 years OR bone age <10 years

**Category C — Small for Gestational Age (SGA)**
- Age >4 years, AND
- Short stature (height <3rd percentile), AND
- Slow growth (downward deviation of height >1 percentile band over not less than 12 months)

**Step 2: Refer to Paediatric Endocrinologist**

All three categories above are referred to a paediatric endocrinologist for further evaluation.

**Step 3: For Suspected GHD — Perform GH Stimulation Tests**

A minimum of **two GH stimulation tests** using either ITT (insulin tolerance test), GST (glucagon stimulation test), or AST (arginine stimulation test) are required for diagnosis.

**Step 4A: Peak GH <10 mcg/L (Both Tests)**
→ Diagnose **GHD**
→ Proceed to MRI of hypothalamic-pituitary region AND assess for other pituitary hormone deficiencies
→ **Treat with rhGH + replacement of other pituitary hormone deficiencies**

**Step 4B: Peak GH >10 mcg/L**
→ Assess for history of cranial irradiation or hypothalamic-pituitary disease:
- **No history:** Continue to observe (no GH treatment indicated)
- **Yes, history present:** Perform MRI + assess other pituitary hormone deficiencies
  → Monitor growth
  → If **slow growth confirmed** → Treat with rhGH + replacement of other pituitary hormone deficiencies
  → If **no slow growth** → Continue to observe

**Step 5: For Turner Syndrome and SGA** (no stimulation test required for these conditions)
→ **Treat with rhGH**

## Algorithm 2: Management Of GH-Treated Children During Transition Period

**Overview:** This algorithm guides the re-evaluation of GH-treated children who have completed linear growth (reached final height) and are in the transition period (approximately 17–25 years old). The aim is to identify those with persistent GH deficiency who should continue GH therapy into adulthood.

*Modified from AACE Medical Guidelines for Clinical Practice for Growth Hormone Use in Growth Hormone Deficient Adults and Transition Patients — 2009 Update.*

**Step 1: Entry — GH-Treated Children During Transition Period With Possible Persistent GH Deficiency**

Three pathways are determined by the underlying aetiology and IGF-1 level:

**Pathway A — Organic Disease with ≥3 Pituitary Hormones Deficient AND Low IGF-1 (<2.5 percentile / -2SD)**
→ **No further stimulation testing required**
→ **Treat** (diagnose as persistent severe GHD)

**Pathway B — Organic Disease with 0, 1, or 2 Pituitary Hormones Deficient AND IGF-1 <50 percentile / 0SD**
→ Requires stimulation testing (proceed to Step 2)

Sub-pathway within B:
- MPHD (Multiple Pituitary Hormone Deficiency) AND/OR IGF-1 <50 percentile/0SD → **High suspicion for persistent GHD** → Proceed to stimulation test
- Normal IGF-1 (≥0SD) → **Low suspicion** → **Observe** (no treatment)

**Pathway C — Idiopathic GH Deficiency in Childhood OR Suspected Hypothalamic Origin**
→ Requires stimulation testing (proceed to Step 2)

**Step 2: Stimulation Test — ITT or GST**

| Test | Diagnostic Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Insulin Tolerance Test (ITT) | Peak GH ≤ 5.0 mcg/L | **Treat** (persistent severe GHD confirmed) |
| Glucagon Stimulation Test (GST) | Peak GH ≤ 3.0 mcg/L | **Treat** (persistent severe GHD confirmed) |
| Either test above threshold | — | **Observe** (GH not deficient in adulthood) |

**Key Notes:**
- ITT is the preferred test for re-evaluation during the transition period.
- Re-testing should be performed at least **1–3 months after cessation** of childhood rhGH therapy (serum IGF-1 may take up to 6 months to normalise after stopping rhGH).
- Sex steroid priming is NOT required for these tests in the transition period (unlike in childhood testing).

## Algorithm 3: Screening And Diagnosis For Adults With Possible Growth Hormone Deficiency

**Overview:** This algorithm guides the evaluation of adults presenting with clinical features of GH deficiency syndrome. It determines which adults require stimulation testing and which can proceed directly to treatment.

*Modified from AACE Medical Guidelines for Clinical Practice for Growth Hormone Use in Growth Hormone Deficient Adults and Transition Patients — 2009 Update.*

**Step 1: Entry — Adult With Clinical Features of GH Deficiency**

Three categories determine the diagnostic pathway:

**Category 1 — Organic Disease with ≥3 Hormones Deficient AND Low IGF-1 (<2.5 percentile / -2SD)**
→ **No further stimulation testing required**
→ **Treat** directly (diagnosis of severe GHD is confirmed)

**Category 2 — Organic Disease with 1 or 2 Hormones Deficient AND IGF-1 <50 percentile / 0SD**
→ Perform stimulation test (ITT or GST) — proceed to Step 2

**Category 3 — History of:**
- Head injury, OR
- Cranial irradiation, OR
- Subarachnoid haemorrhage or hypothalamic disease
→ Perform stimulation test (ITT or GST) — proceed to Step 2

**Step 2: Stimulation Test — ITT or GST**

| Test | Diagnostic Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Insulin Tolerance Test (ITT) | Peak GH ≤ 5.0 mcg/L | **Treat** (GHD confirmed) |
| Glucagon Stimulation Test (GST) | Peak GH ≤ 3.0 mcg/L | **Treat** (GHD confirmed) |

**Important Contraindications to ITT:**
- History of seizures
- History of strokes
- Presence of coronary artery disease
- Age >55 years
→ Use GST as an alternative in these patients.

**Key Notes:**
- ITT is the **gold standard** for diagnosing adult GH deficiency.
- In most circumstances, only **one** stimulation test is required for adult diagnosis (unlike children who require two tests).
- GHRH is not available in Malaysia; therefore, the ARG+GHRH test cannot be used.
- The diagnostic cut-off for adult GHD using an immunochemiluminometric assay is GH <5.1 mcg/L (96% sensitivity, 92% specificity).

|---|---|---|
| Children (2 tests required) | ITT / GST / AST | Peak GH < 10 mcg/L |
