## 6.1: Burns Patients

[Level I] Three RCTs conducted in Shriners Hospitals for Children of Texas on the use of low-dose rhGH (0.05 mg/kg body weight) in 178 severely burned children (TBSA burn >40%, age <18 years) showed that rhGH significantly increased:
- Body composition (height, bone mineral content, lean body mass, and weight)
- Cardiac function in the treatment group compared to placebo (12% ± 24 vs 1% ± 20)
- Strength measurements at 12 months
- The number of reconstructive procedures from hospital discharge to end of study was significantly **lower** in the rhGH group (1.8 ± 1.3 vs 4.1 ± 2.5)

Data is still limited for the use of rhGH therapy in children with burns.

There is **no retrievable evidence** regarding the usefulness of rhGH therapy in burns in adult patients.

> **Recommendations:**
> - rhGH therapy for burns in children should only be limited to **research purposes**. **(Grade B)**
> - rhGH therapy should **not** be used for burns in adults. **(Grade C)**

---

## 6.2: Critically Ill Patients

[Level I] Two RCTs clearly demonstrated that the administration of **high doses** of rhGH therapy to critically ill patients receiving prolonged intensive care was associated with **increased morbidity and mortality**.

However, [Level I; Level I] two smaller studies using **short-term, low-dose** rhGH in surgical patients showed protein sparing benefits with no major adverse effects apart from hyperglycaemia.

> **Recommendation:** rhGH therapy should **not** be used in critical illness. **(Grade A)**

---

## 6.3: Fibromyalgia

[Level III] A narrative review of 26 papers suggested that pituitary function was normal in fibromyalgia patients. The therapeutic efficacy of supplemental rhGH therapy in fibromyalgia requires further study.

[Level I] In a small non-placebo RCT using rhGH as concomitant treatment in **severe fibromyalgia associated with low IGF-1 levels**, the therapy group showed:
- 60% reduction in the mean number of tender points (3.25 ± 0.8 vs 8.25 ± 0.9)
- Similar improvements in FIQ score and EQ-VAS scale

The findings suggested there may be an advantage of using rhGH therapy in a subset of severe fibromyalgia patients with low IGF-1 serum levels. Further randomised placebo-controlled studies are needed.

> **Recommendation:** rhGH therapy should **not** be used for routine treatment of fibromyalgia. It **may be considered** in patients with low IGF-1 levels. **(Grade A)**

## 6.4: Anti-Ageing Therapy In Healthy Elderly

The safety, efficacy, and role of rhGH in healthy elderly individuals remain controversial. The use of rhGH as an anti-ageing agent in Europe and the United States has **not been approved**. Published literature is limited, generally non-randomised, small, of short duration, and using only limited surrogate parameters.

[Level I] A systematic review published in 2007 including 31 articles (total 220 participants) showed:
- rhGH therapy was associated with small changes in body composition: reduction in overall fat mass (-2.10 kg, 95% CI -2.80 to -1.35) and increase in lean body mass (2.10 kg, 95% CI 1.30 to 2.90)
- rhGH therapy had **no significant effect** on serum lipid levels or bone density
- **No evidence** that GH effects on body composition resulted in change in muscle strength, maximal oxygen uptake, or improvement in function
- Increased rate of adverse events: oedema, arthralgia, carpal tunnel syndrome, gynaecomastia, and increased risk of diabetes and impaired fasting glucose

[Level II-2] A recent systematic review showed no definite evidence that rhGH therapy benefited elderly non-GHD individuals. There is no data to suggest that rhGH therapy benefits cognition in the healthy adult population.

> **Recommendation:** rhGH should **not** be used as an anti-ageing therapy. **(Grade A)**

## 6.5: Sports

Exogenous rhGH therapy has been touted as an athletic performance enhancer, and its use for such a purpose has been **banned by professional sports regulatory authorities**.

[Level I] A systematic review published in 2008 of 44 articles on the effects of GH on athletic performance (303 subjects, mean age 27 ± 3 years, mean BMI 24 ± 2 kg/m²) showed:
- Although exogenous GH was associated with an **increase in lean body mass** (mean 2.1 kg, 95% CI 1.3 to 2.9), there was **no significant improvement** in actual strength and exercise capacity
- Two out of three studies measuring exercising lactate levels showed **significantly higher** exercising lactate levels in rhGH-treated subjects, suggesting GH therapy may **worsen exercise capacity**
- rhGH-treated subjects more frequently experienced soft tissue oedema and fatigue

> **Recommendation:** rhGH therapy should **not** be used to enhance athletic performance. **(Grade A)**

## 6.6: Infertility

There are no studies that looked at the use of rhGH alone in infertile women with normal pituitary function. Most evidence looked at rhGH as an adjunct to other treatments.

[Level I] A Cochrane systematic review of 9 RCTs specifically looked at the use of rhGH to improve outcomes of in-vitro fertilisation (IVF) in women. The review found:
- **No evidence** that routine addition of GH to IVF increased the livebirth rate
- An improvement was shown only in those who had **previously responded poorly** to IVF (OR=4.37, 95% CI 1.06 to 18.01)

[Level II-1] Another systematic review looking at women who had previously responded poorly to IVF: 5 of 6 studies using rhGH showed no significant improvement in fertility measures (serum oestradiol, duration of follicular phase, number of oocytes, pregnancy rate). The single study favouring GH was small and used historical controls.

> **Recommendation:** rhGH therapy should **not** be used in the treatment of infertile women with normal pituitary function. **(Grade B)**

---

## 6.7: Obesity

The endogenous secretion of GH in obese individuals is decreased, although the exact physiological role of GH in obesity is unclear.

[Level I] A recent meta-analysis of 24 RCTs on rhGH therapy in **simple adult obesity** (not associated with distinct clinical syndromes) showed:
- Treatment with **very high dosages** of rhGH led to very small improvements in waist-hip ratio and fat mass compared to placebo
- **No significant differences** in body weight, BMI, subcutaneous fat area, resting energy expenditure, respiratory quotient, or blood pressure
- Significant increase in adverse events associated with rhGH: arthralgia, paraesthesias, and oedema

The beneficial effects are quantitatively small and would **not justify** a clinically relevant role for rhGH therapy in obesity.

> **Recommendation:** rhGH therapy should **not** be used in the treatment of simple adult obesity. **(Grade A)**
