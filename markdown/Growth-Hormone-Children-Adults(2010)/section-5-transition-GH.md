## 4.0: Overview

The **transition period** refers to the period from adolescence after attainment of final height (FH) until 6–7 years later — approximately from **17 to 25 years old**. This period encompasses a broad set of physical and psychosocial changes. [Level III]

The primary goal of rhGH therapy in children is to achieve a FH consistent with the target height (TH). It is common practice to **discontinue rhGH after attainment of FH**. However, the **new goal of rhGH therapy in the transition period** is normalisation of metabolism and improvement in quality of life.

## 4.1: Who Should Continue GH Therapy?

[Level II-2] It has been reported that **71.9% of childhood-onset GHD (COGHD) children** continue to be GHD in adult life and are likely to require further rhGH therapy.

[Level II-1; Level II-3] However, a substantial proportion of children with isolated idiopathic GHD (IGHD) recover normal GH reserve by the time FH is attained, especially those previously diagnosed with partial GH deficiency. [Level II-3]

**Patients more likely to have ongoing GHD:**
- [Level II-3; Level II-1] MPHD, with or without structural pituitary or peri-pituitary disease
- [Level II-3] Previous cranial radiation therapy

[Level II-2; Level II-1] In the presence of **≥3 pituitary hormone deficiencies**, all patients had a pathological GH response during re-testing. [Level II-2] A prospective study demonstrated that the positive predictive values (PPVs) for GH deficiency with 3 and 4 pituitary hormone deficiencies were **96%** and **99%** respectively.

[Level II-2] In those exposed to cranial irradiation (median dose 58 Gy) during childhood, 48% did not fulfil the biochemical criteria (peak stimulated GH <3 mcg/L) for rhGH replacement in adulthood. [Level II-3; Level II-2] Only 61% of patients with COGHD and abnormal MRI findings remain severely GHD in adulthood. All those with associated MPHD continued to have severe GHD in adulthood.

**Who does NOT need re-testing:** [Level III]
The AACE 2009 guidelines recommend that **all patients with COGHD should be re-evaluated** at completion of growth, EXCEPT those with ALL three of the following:
- Organic hypothalamic-pituitary disease, AND
- ≥3 pituitary hormone deficiencies, AND
- Serum IGF-1 below -2 SD

Re-testing for GH deficiency should be performed by paediatric endocrinologists **at least 1–3 months after cessation** of rhGH therapy.

> **Recommendation:** All patients with COGHD should be re-tested at completion of growth **EXCEPT** for those with:
> - Organic hypothalamic-pituitary disease
> - AND ≥3 pituitary hormone deficiencies
> - AND serum IGF-1 below –2 SD **(Grade C)**

## 4.2: Re-Testing Of GHD Patients

There are limited data regarding the optimal pharmacologic agent for GH stimulation testing in the transition period. The functional dynamics of GH secretion are different in growing children versus those who have reached FH, leading to different definitions of GHD.

### Preferred Test

[Level III; Level III; Level III] The Insulin Tolerance Test (ITT) is recommended as the standard test to re-evaluate GH reserve in adolescents during the transition period.

[Level II-2] A mean peak GH response to ITT in those with high probability of permanent GH deficiency was significantly lower compared to controls.

**Diagnostic Cut-offs for the Transition Period:**

| Test | Threshold for GHD | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ITT | Peak GH ≤ 5.0 mcg/L | [Level II-1] Best diagnostic accuracy was found at ≤5.62 mcg/L |
| GST (Glucagon) | Peak GH ≤ 3.0 mcg/L | [Level II-2] Sensitivity 97%, specificity 88% |

[Level III] A Consensus Statement (2007) recommended a peak stimulated GH cut-off of **<6 mcg/L** for permanent GHD in young adults with COGHD.

**Glucagon Stimulation Test (GST)** is as good as ITT in assessing GH reserve in hypopituitary adults and is a good **alternative** when ITT is contraindicated. [Level II-2]

[Level II-3] Serum IGF-1 in a patient who has discontinued rhGH treatment may be helpful in identifying persistent GH deficiency. However, serum IGF-1 may not fall below normal levels until **6 months after** rhGH therapy is discontinued.

Different IGF-1 cut-off points for persistent GH deficiency have been reported: -1.7 SDS, <-2 SDS, and ≤-2.83 SDS.

> **Key Statements:**
> - ITT is the preferred stimulatory test to re-assess patients with COGHD during the transition period.
> - A peak stimulated GH value of **5.6 mcg/L or less** in response to ITT has the best accuracy for diagnosing permanent GH deficiency.
> - Glucagon test is a good alternative to ITT.

> **Recommendation:** ITT should be used to re-test COGHD patients during transition period. **(Grade B)**

## 4.3: Dose Of GH During Transition Period

[Level I] In a large RCT of young adults with GHD randomised to rhGH doses of 25 mcg/kg/day (paediatric dose), 12.5 mcg/kg/day (adult dose), or no GH treatment:
- No treatment differences between the two rhGH doses
- Significant improvement in body composition in both rhGH arms versus placebo
- Females required higher doses to normalise IGF-1 than males

[Level I] Using the same cohort for bone outcomes: no significant changes in bone turnover markers, bone mineral content, and BMD between the 2 doses, but significant improvement versus control. The lower paediatric dose was favoured for bone mass accumulation as the higher paediatric dose caused predominant bone resorption over bone formation.

[Level I] In an earlier smaller RCT comparing 12.5 vs 25.0 mcg/kg/day: improvement in lean body mass (LBM), fat mass (FM), and spine BMD was significantly better with the higher dose.

[Level III] The AACE 2009 guidelines recommend that the starting dose during transition should be approximately **50% of the dose between the paediatric dose required for growth and the adult dose**.

> **Recommendations:**
> - Starting dose during transition period: **0.0125–0.0250 mg/kg/day** **(Grade C)**
> - The dose of rhGH should be adjusted to maintain **normal serum IGF-1 level**. **(Grade A)**

## 4.4: Benefits Of Treatment During Transition Period

[Level II-2; Level II-2; Level I; Level II-2] Studies showed improvement in body composition and lipid profiles in young adults but **not muscle strength** with rhGH therapy during the transition period. rhGH treatment in GHD adolescents did not show any effect in terms of cardiac mass and function.

[Level I] As for QoL in COGHD patients, overall baseline QoL was not compromised in severely GHD patients during the transition period, but dimensions related to age-specific psychological problems were significantly worse than healthy subjects and appeared to **positively respond to rhGH therapy**.

**Bone Mineral Density:**
[Level I] A large RCT showed an increase in total BMD in rhGH-treated versus untreated GHD adolescents after 2 years. [Level I] A large multicentre RCT showed significant improvement in lumbar spine BMD in the treated group. [Level I] Another study documented that areal BMD continued to accrue in untreated GHD patients at a slightly but significantly lower rate than those treated.

However, [Level II-2; Level I] smaller studies did not demonstrate any benefit to BMD, lipid metabolism, body composition, cardiac function, muscle strength, or QoL with rhGH therapy during the transition period.

**Many studies have shown an improvement in body composition, lipid profile, bone mineral density, and QoL in rhGH-treated GHD adolescents during the transition period.**
