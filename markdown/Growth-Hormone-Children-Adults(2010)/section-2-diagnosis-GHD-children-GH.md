## 2.0: Overview

Diagnosis of GH deficiency in childhood is a **multifaceted process** requiring a combination of clinical and auxological criteria, biochemical tests, and radiological evaluation. [Level III]

## 2.1: Clinical And Auxological Criteria

[Level III; Level II] The GH Research Society Consensus Guidelines (2000) and the American Association of Clinical Endocrinologists (AACE, 2003) recommend the following clinical and auxological criteria to identify GH deficiency:

i. Severe short stature, defined as height >3 standard deviations (SD) below the mean
ii. Height >1.5 SD below mid-parental height (MPH)
iii. Height >2 SD below mean AND height velocity (HV) over one year >1 SD below the mean for chronological age (CA), OR a decrease in height by >0.5 SD over one year in children over 2 years of age
iv. In the absence of short stature, HV >2 SD below the mean over one year OR >1.5 SD below the mean sustained over two years
v. Signs of intracranial lesion
vi. Signs of MPHD (Multiple Pituitary Hormone Deficiency)
vii. Neonatal symptoms and signs of GH deficiency

### Malaysian Local Equivalents (NCHS Charts)

[Level III] In Malaysia, growth charts in SD are not available. National Child Health Statistics (NCHS) growth charts in centiles are available in most clinics. The following equivalents are used:
- **3rd percentile** represents **2 SD below the mean**
- **10 cm below the MPH** represents **1.5 SD below MPH**
- **One centile band** on the growth chart represents **2/3 of an SD**

**Mid-parental Height (MPH) Formulae:**

| Child's Sex | Formula |
|---|---|
| Boys | [Father's height (cm) + Mother's height (cm) + 13] ÷ 2 |
| Girls | [Father's height (cm) - 13 + Mother's height (cm)] ÷ 2 |

**Target height range = MPH ± 10 cm**

### Pitfalls in Auxology

Although auxology is a simple, non-invasive tool, it has limitations:
- Height measurements may be imprecise due to operator or equipment errors
- Height velocity (HV) errors can arise from seasonal variation and short measurement intervals
- Serial height measurements should be performed by **trained personnel** using appropriate tools, with intervals **not less than 3 months**

[Level II-2] No correlation was demonstrated between HV in two consecutive years in short normal children and GHD children, indicating severe imprecision of HV. [Level II-2] No significant difference in height standard deviation score (SDS) and HV was demonstrated between GHD and idiopathic short stature (ISS).

[Level II-3] Using HV less than 25th percentile to diagnose GH deficiency has high sensitivity (82%) but poor specificity (43%). **Auxology alone is inadequate for the diagnosis of GHD** — it must be interpreted in combination with other parameters.

## 2.2: Biochemical Tests

GH stimulation tests play a critical role in the diagnosis of GH deficiency. The most frequently used tests include the **insulin tolerance test (ITT)** and **glucagon stimulation test (GST)**. [Level II-2] Other stimulation tests using pharmacologic agents such as arginine, GHRH with or without arginine, levodopa, and clonidine have also been used. In Malaysia, only **ITT, GST, and arginine stimulation test (AST)** are available due to unavailability of other agents.

### Diagnostic Criteria

[Level III; Level II] Peak plasma GH level of **<10 mcg/L** during GH stimulation tests using pharmacologic agents is used as the biochemical criterion for GH deficiency. This cut-off of <10 mcg/L is arbitrarily defined.

A **minimum of two stimulation tests** indicating GH deficiency are required for biochemical confirmation of the diagnosis, because of the high frequency of false-positive results for a single test. [Level II-3]

### IGF-1 and IGFBP-3

Insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) and insulin-like growth factor binding protein-3 (IGFBP-3) are helpful but not definitive parameters:
- [Level III; Level II-3] IGF-1 values are specific but **not sensitive**; they are affected by nutritional status, sex steroid, thyroid hormone, and chronic diseases
- IGFBP-3 is age- and puberty-dependent; reference ranges standardised for age and sex are essential
- Both should be used as supporting investigations, not standalone diagnostics

### Special Situations

**GH Testing in Neonates:** GH level should be measured in neonatal hypoglycaemia in the absence of a metabolic disorder. [Level III] A random GH measurement <20 mcg/L in a polyclonal radioimmunoassay would suggest GH deficiency in the newborn. An IGFBP-3 measurement is of value for the diagnosis of GHD in infants.

**Peripubertal Period:** Low GH levels in stimulation tests frequently occur during the peripubertal period. [Level I; Level II-3] Sex-steroid priming prior to GH stimulation tests reduces the percentage of false-positive results for GH deficiency. Children with MPHD should receive **adequate adrenal and thyroid hormone replacement before** biochemical evaluation of GH deficiency.

### Insulin Tolerance Test (ITT) — Gold Standard

ITT is regarded as the gold standard for evaluation of pituitary growth hormone reserve. In ITT, insulin is administered intravenously to produce a nadir in the plasma glucose level of **≤2.2 mmol/L**. This is a potentially hazardous procedure and **must be done only by an experienced endocrinologist in a tertiary centre**.

### Pitfalls in Biochemical Tests

[Level III] GH in circulation consists of a wide variety of molecular isoforms. Assays using polyclonal antisera give higher results than those based on monoclonal assays. Results from **different immunoassays from different laboratories cannot be compared**.

[Level III; Level II-3] GH stimulation tests lack precision, accuracy, and have poor reliability. Factors affecting peak plasma GH level include puberty, genetic target height SDS, height SDS, weight SDS, bone age, and the pharmacologic agent used.

[Level II-2] Spontaneous GH secretion is more specific than pharmacologic testing but is resource intensive and time-consuming.

## 2.3: Radiological Evaluation

A plain radiograph of the left hand and wrist to assess osseous maturation (bone age) is helpful in making the diagnosis. A **delayed bone age of at least 2 years** compared to chronological age is a feature of GH deficiency, but it is not diagnostic.

[Level III] MRI or CT scan of the brain with particular attention to the hypothalamic-pituitary region should be carried out in **any child diagnosed with GH deficiency**. Presence of ectopic posterior pituitary, missing stalk and hypoplastic anterior pituitary (EMH) on MRI is a significant predictor of permanent severe GH deficiency with or without other pituitary hormone deficiency. A normal MRI does not exclude GH deficiency.

[Level III] Approximately 20% of subjects with GH deficiency had mutations of GH-1, GHRH-R, PIT-1, and PROP-1 genes causing permanent disease. All patients with these genetic causes had normal pituitary stalk and normal position of posterior pituitary while 50% had pituitary hypoplasia.

## 2.4: Diagnostic Criteria — Full Confirmation Requires ALL Three

**i. Clinical and Auxological Criteria:**
- Short stature as defined by:
  - Height <3rd centile on the NCHS chart, AND/OR
  - Height below target height (TH) range i.e. MPH minus 10 cm
- AND slow growth as defined by:
  - Downward deviation of height ≥1 centile band over at least 12 months

*Exception: Children with acquired hypothalamic-pituitary disease are considered to have fulfilled the above criteria if they have slow growth even in the absence of short stature.*

**ii. Biochemical Criteria:**
- A minimum of **TWO** GH stimulation tests using ITT, GST, or AST
- Peak GH level of **<10 mcg/L** is diagnostic
- Sex steroid priming is useful for peripubertal children
- Children with MPHD must have received adequate adrenal and thyroid hormone replacement before biochemical evaluation

**iii. Radiological Criteria:** [Grade C]
- Delayed bone age

**After confirmation of GH deficiency, subsequent investigations should include:** [Grade C]
- MRI of hypothalamic-pituitary region
- Genetic study (if facilities are available)

## 2.5: Criteria For Referral To Paediatric Endocrinologist

Patients with the following criteria should be referred to paediatric endocrinologists for further evaluation, confirmation, and appropriate therapy:
- Neonates/infants with features of GH deficiency (persistent hypoglycaemia with any of the associated features listed in Section 1.3)
- Children at risk of GH deficiency showing slow growth and/or short stature
- Children who fulfil the clinical and auxological criteria for GH deficiency
