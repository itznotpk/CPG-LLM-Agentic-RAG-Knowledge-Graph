**Summary of Updates**
- Importance of categorizing patients at initial visit, in particular with regard to presence of co-morbidities (i.e. CV, renal complications, quality of life assessments), as it will help in deciding management plans.

## 3.1: Initial Assessment
- At diagnosis of T2DM:
  - A detailed history which focuses on a few key issues which will affect treatment decision should be undertaken apart from assessing for symptoms and presentation of diabetes (refer to Table 3-1: Detailed Assessment of a Newly Diagnosed Patient with T2DM).
  - Full physical examination (including fundoscopy and monofilament test) and baseline investigations should be performed to assess for presence of ASCVD risk factors and complications of diabetes.
- Management is based on results of the above.
- [Level I] [Level III] Diabetes management involves lifestyle modification, medications and patient education to encourage self-care and empowerment.

Table 3-1: Detailed Assessment of a Newly Diagnosed Patient with T2DM
| Domain | Details to Assess |
|---|---|
| **T2DM history** | • Characteristics at onset (e.g. age, symptoms) <br> • Increased thirst (polydipsia) <br> • Polyphagia <br> • Polyuria and/or nocturia <br> • Malaise/fatigue <br> • Weight loss/gain — some patients may gain weight and develop diabetes e.g. in Cushing's Syndrome <br> • Altered vision <br> • Frequent and recurrent infections <br> • Assessment of frequency/cause/severity of past hospitalisations <br> • Assessment of history of macrovascular and microvascular complications: ASCVD symptoms, neurological symptoms, visual disturbances, renal related symptoms, foot problems, sexual dysfunction <br> • Screening for depression, anxiety and eating disorder <br> • Identifying existing social barriers and support |
| **Predisposition to T2DM** | • Age over 30 years <br> • Family history <br> • Ethnic group <br> • Overweight/obese <br> • Lifestyle: dietary habits, level of physical inactivity, smoking, alcohol consumption, occupation, sleep behaviour <br> • Hypertension <br> • Dyslipidaemia <br> • Obstetric history of large babies or gestational diabetes <br> • Women with polycystic ovarian syndrome <br> • Medications that impact weight and glycaemic status |
| **Co-morbidities** | • Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) <br> • Cognitive impairment/dementia <br> • Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) <br> • Pancreatitis <br> • Periodontal disease <br> • Low testosterone/hypogonadism in men <br> • Cancers |
| **Medications and vaccinations** | • Review of treatment regimens and response (if defaulted previously) <br> • Medication taking behaviour <br> • Medication intolerance or side effects <br> • Complementary and alternative medicine use <br> • Vaccination status (influenza and pneumococcal vaccination) |
| **Referrals** | • Annual dilated eye exam <br> • Family planning for women of reproductive age <br> • Registered dietitian for medical nutrition therapy <br> • Diabetes self-management education and support <br> • Dentist for comprehensive dental and periodontal examination <br> • Mental health professional, if indicated <br> • Immunisation <br> • Smoking cessation <br> • Cancer screening |
| **Risks affecting treatment decision and individualised target** | • Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) <br> • Heart failure <br> • Renal impairment (Diabetic kidney disease; DKD) <br> • Hypoglycaemia |

Table 3-2: Physical Examination of Newly Diagnosed Patient with T2DM
| System | Examination Components |
|---|---|
| **Weight/waist measurement** | • Body mass index (BMI) = weight (kg) / height² (m²) <br> • Waist circumference (WC) <br> • Growth/pubertal development in adolescents |
| **CV system** | • Blood pressure (supine and standing) <br> • Neck and peripheral pulses <br> • Precordial examination |
| **Eye** (Refer to Section 5.1: Retinopathy) | • Visual acuity with refraction (with corrected vision) <br> • Dilated retinal examination/photography (for retinopathy) <br> • Cataract |
| **Feet** (Refer to Section 5.6: Sexual dysfunction) | • Skin integrity <br> • Pressure areas/callus formation <br> • Ulcer <br> • Toenails <br> • Interdigital lesions <br> • Foot deformities (structure/Charcot's joint) <br> • Skin sensation <br> • Pedal pulses (dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial) |
| **Peripheral nerves** (Refer to Section 5.3: Neuropathy) | • Tendon reflexes <br> • Sensation: touch (e.g. with 10-g monofilament) <br> • Vibration (e.g. with 128-Hz tuning fork) |

Table 3-3: Investigations for a Newly Diagnosed Patient with T2DM
| Category | Investigations |
|---|---|
| **Baseline** | • Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) <br> • HbA1c <br> • Renal profile <br> • Lipid profile <br> • Liver function test <br> • Urinalysis for albumin, microalbuminuria if albuminuria is absent <br> • ECG |

**Aims of Treatment**
- The overall aims of management are to:
  - Improve quality of life
  - Reduce complications
  - Prevent premature death
- Patient and family members should be counselled by identifying and addressing concerns which may cause distress, thus adversely affecting management.

**Short-term aims**
- Relief of symptoms and acute complications

**Long-term aims**
- Achievement of appropriate glycaemic levels with lifestyle management including weight reduction (where appropriate) and maintain durability of glycaemic control
- Reduction of other CV risk factors
- Identification and treatment of chronic complications
- Optimise quality of life

- [Level I] Most of the microvascular complications of T2DM are related to the degree and the length of exposure to hyperglycaemia.
- [Level I] The legacy effect/metabolic memory of early glycaemic control is well established in reducing both micro- and macrovascular complications. This should be emphasized in all patients with newly diagnosed T2DM.
- [Level I] Multiple risk factor reduction (the ABC's for T2DM) has been shown to reduce long term complications and mortality sustained beyond the intervention phase. These are:
  - **A** — HbA1c (glycaemic control)
  - **B** — BP control
  - **C** — Cholesterol (lipid control)
