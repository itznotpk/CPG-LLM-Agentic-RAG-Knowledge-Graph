## Section 8: Complementary and Alternative Therapy in T2DM

### Summary of Updates

- There is insufficient evidence to recommend the use of supplements such as chromium, vitamin D, cinnamon or herbs/supplement.
- Alternative therapies may contain harmful ingredients, may be unsafe or may be improperly marketed as over-the-counter (OTC) products.
- A recent prospective RCT assessing vitamin D supplementation failed to slow progression of prediabetes to overt diabetes.

## 8.1 Complementary and Alternative Medicine (CAM)

- Complementary and alternative medicine (CAM) is defined as healthcare management outside of conventional medicine, with "complementary" meaning used together with, and "alternative" meaning used in place of conventional medicine.
- The 2020 American Diabetes Association guidelines state that there is **insufficient evidence** to recommend the daily use of supplements such as chromium, vitamin D, cinnamon or herbs/supplement (Level III).
- Many individuals with diabetes are hesitant to inform their healthcare providers of their complementary therapy use (Level III).
  - Unfortunately, alternative therapies may contain harmful ingredients, may be unsafe or may be improperly marketed as OTC products when they should be marketed as prescription products.
- Healthcare providers are **encouraged to enquire** about the use of CAM in people with diabetes (Level III).
- It is very important to **advise patients not to replace** conventional medical therapy for T2DM with an unproven alternative therapy.
  - Patients need to be cautioned on the potential side effects, drug interactions and lack of product standardisation.
  - In addition, the increased costs that patients may incur when they use ineffective therapies or delay treatment with proven therapeutic agents.
- Natural health products which are unlabelled should not be used to avoid intake of products that may be adulterated with pharmaceuticals or other contaminants (Level III).
- Medicines for T2DM and other health conditions may need to be adjusted if a person is taking an alternative treatment as drug-herb interactions may occur.

### Popular Supplements and Evidence of Benefits

Clear scientific evidence supporting the benefit from all herbal, vitamin and other supplementations for treatment and prevention of T2DM remain scarce.

- **Olive oil:** When taken as supplementation or part of a Mediterranean diet, has demonstrated favourable effects in reducing the risk of development of T2DM in prediabetes and improvement in glycaemic control in T2DM by 0.27% (Level I).
- **Vitamin D:** Although earlier observational studies have shown an association between low level of vitamin D and increased risk for T2DM, however, a recent RCT study on vitamin D supplementation **failed** to delay progression to T2DM (Level I).
- **Vitamin E** (Level I), **cinnamon** (Level I), **curcumin** (Level I): These have **no significant benefit** on glycaemic control in patients with T2DM.
- **Acupuncture:** Acupuncture has not been shown to improve glycaemic control in patients with T2DM.

## 8.2 Reporting Adverse Events

- Healthcare professionals and consumers are encouraged to report any adverse events related to products intended to treat or cure diabetes to the **Malaysian Adverse Drug Reaction Advisory Committee (MADRAC)** at www.npra.gov.my/index.php/en/health-professionals/reporting-adr

## Recommendations: Complementary and Alternative Therapy

| Recommendation | Grade |
|---|---|
| Healthcare providers should enquire about the use of CAM therapies in patients with prediabetes and T2DM. | **Grade C** |
| Benefits from all herbal, vitamin and other supplementations for treatment and prevention of T2DM remain unclear. | **Grade C** |

## Section 9: Key Performance Indicators

### Summary of Updates

- This section has been revamped and focuses on key performance indicators for management of T2DM.
- Two measures specific for KPIs and 3 measures for baseline data collection have been introduced.
- The results will allow for future re-strategizing where discrepancies and imbalances exist. It will also allow for sharing of best practices contributing to achieving higher standards of care.

## 9.1 Guide to Key Performance Indicators (KPI)

- Over the years, the MOH has been monitoring processes and key indicators (clinical and biochemical) — particularly in the public sector.
- The priority should be on coordinated care to maximise use of limited resources.
- To analyse clinical outcomes that matter:
  - The data should be collected and expressed as "outcomes per population" by region (district, state, specified geographical region, national).
  - Results will allow for future re-strategizing where discrepancies and imbalances exist. This is important for sharing of best practices that contribute to achieving higher standards of care particularly in regions performing below average.
  - Interventions to promote patient centred multi-level care (between primary and secondary care; between different disciplines; between private and public).
- The data collected should include parameters that fulfil the following:
  - Key clinical outcomes
  - Easy to collect
  - Collected without the intervention of the direct stakeholders.

### Figure 9-1: Key Performance Indicators for Management of T2DM

**KPI 1: Percentage of T2DM Patients with HbA1c >8.5%**

Formula:
```
Percentage of T2DM patients        =   Number of T2DM patients with HbA1c >8.5%          × 100%
(at >12-month follow-up)                Total number of T2DM (at >12-month follow up)
with HbA1c >8.5%                       patients attending the facility
```

**Proposed Target: <20% for primary care & tertiary care**

Formula:
```
Percentage of patients screened     =   Number of patients screened for complications     × 100%
for complications                       Total number of T2DM patients attending the facility
```

**Proposed Target: 75% for primary care & 90% for tertiary care**

*\*The targets are proposed based on existing data taking into account the practicality of the recommendations and the reality of the current available resources and facilities.*

## 9.2 Baseline Data Collection

The following are for data collection to enable future planning of T2DM care:

### A. Number of New Cases of Patients with T2DM Needing Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT) per Year per 100,000 Total Population

Formula:
```
No. of new cases of T2DM       =   No. of new T2DM cases needing RRT*
needing RRT/year/100,000            Total population
total population
```

*\*Collected from public and private hospitals, and renal dialysis centres in that region for one year.*

---

### B. Number of Below Knee Amputations (BKA) in Patients with T2DM per Year per 100,000 Total Population

Formula:
```
No. of BKA in patients with     =   No. of BKA in patients with T2DM*
T2DM/year/100,000                   Total population
total population
```

*\*Collected from the operating theatre of all private and public hospitals in that region for one year.*

---

### C. Number of Severe Hypoglycaemic Admissions in Patients with T2DM per Year per 100,000 Total Estimated Population with T2DM

Formula:
```
No. of severe hypoglycaemic        =   No. of severe hypoglycaemic admissions in patients with T2DM*
admissions in patients with             Total estimated population with T2DM
T2DM/year/100,000 total population
```

*\*Collected from all private and public hospitals in that region for one year.*

### Evidence Summary for Common CAM Products

| Supplement | Evidence | Effect on T2DM |
|---|---|---|
| Olive oil | Level I | Modest improvement in glycaemic control (~0.27% HbA1c reduction) |
| Vitamin D | Level I | No benefit in preventing T2DM progression |
| Vitamin E | Level I | No significant benefit on glycaemic control |
| Cinnamon | Level I | No significant benefit on glycaemic control |
| Curcumin | Level I | No significant benefit on glycaemic control |
| Chromium | Insufficient | Not recommended |
| Herbs/supplements | Insufficient | Not recommended |
| Acupuncture | No evidence | Not shown to improve glycaemic control |

### Targets for Control in T2DM (Quick Reference)

| Parameters | Target Levels |
|---|---|
| **HbA1c** | <7.0% (most); ≤6.5% (selected) |
| **Fasting/pre-prandial glucose** | 4.4–7.0 mmol/L |
| **Post-prandial glucose** | 4.4–8.5 mmol/L |
| **LDL-C** | ≤2.6 mmol/L (≤1.4 if established CVD) |
| **BP** | 130–139/70–79 mmHg |
| **Exercise** | ≥150 minutes/week |
| **Body weight** | Up to 10% weight loss if overweight/obese |
