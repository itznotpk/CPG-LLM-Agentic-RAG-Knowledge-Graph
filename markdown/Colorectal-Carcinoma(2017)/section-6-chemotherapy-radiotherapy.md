## Overview

While surgery remains the primary treatment for CRC, chemotherapy and radiotherapy (RT) serve as neoadjuvant, adjuvant and palliative options. Treatment selection depends on tumour stage, location (colon vs rectal), and patient fitness.

---

## 6.1 Colon Carcinoma

### a. Stage I and II Colon Carcinoma

| Stage | Prognosis | Adjuvant Therapy | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Stage I** | Good with surgery alone | Not indicated | — |
| **Stage II (low risk)** | Relatively favourable | No improvement in OS (RRR=0.96, 95% CI 0.88–1.05) | Level I |
| **Stage II (high risk)** | Relapse rate approaching Stage III | May be considered; DFS improved (RRR=0.83, 95% CI 0.75–0.92) | Level I |

> **Clinical Note:** Most Stage II trials used 5-FU + folinic acid ± levamisole. The MOSAIC trial showed no statistically significant benefit for oxaliplatin addition in stage II, though an exploratory analysis showed a trend toward improved DFS in high-risk stage II with FOLFOX4.

#### High Risk Features for Stage II Colon Carcinoma

| Feature |
|:---|
| Obstruction |
| Perforation |
| T4 disease |
| Poorly differentiated tumour |
| Lymphovascular invasion |
| Inadequate lymph node sampling (<12) |

---

### b. Stage III Colon Carcinoma

| Regimen | Outcome | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **5-FU based chemotherapy** | Well-established improvement in OS | Level I |
| **5-FU/LV (weekly vs 4-weekly schedule)** | No difference in recurrence or survival; 4-weekly has higher toxicity (diarrhoea, stomatitis, neutropaenia; p<0.001) | Level I |
| **FOLFOX4 vs 5-FU/LV** | FOLFOX4 more effective: improved 6-year OS (p=0.023) and 5-year DFS (p=0.005); more grade 3 AE (neutropaenia, neuropathy) | Level I |
| **Capecitabine vs bolus 5-FU/LV** | Equivalent DFS; improved relapse-free survival (p=0.04); significantly fewer AE (p<0.001) | Level I |
| **Duration** | No difference in RFS/OS between 3–6 months vs 9–12 months; ≤6 months recommended | Level I |

### Recommendation 11

- Adjuvant chemotherapy **may be considered** for stage II colon carcinoma with high risk features. Patient should be carefully selected and counselled.
- **5-FU/LV with oxaliplatin** should be given in stage III colon carcinoma.

## 6.2 Rectal Carcinoma

### Treatment by Stage

| Stage | Indication | Treatment |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **T1-T2 N0 (low risk)** | — | Surgery alone (unless margin compromised) |
| **T3-T4 and/or N+** | Local recurrence rates up to 50% without adjuvant treatment | Neoadjuvant CCRT/RT → TME surgery |

---

### Short-Course Pre-operative RT

| Trial | Regimen | Local Recurrence | OS Benefit | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Dutch Colorectal Cancer Group** | 25 Gy / 5 fractions + TME vs TME alone | 5% vs 11% at 10 years (p<0.0001) | No difference in overall OS; Stage III with negative CRM: 50% vs 40% at 10 years (p=0.032) | Level I |
| **Swedish Rectal Cancer Trial** | 25 Gy / 5 fractions pre-op vs surgery alone | 11% vs 27% at 5 years | 58% vs 48% at 5 years | Level I |

---

### Pre-operative vs Post-operative CCRT (German RCT)

| Outcome | Pre-operative CCRT | Post-operative CCRT | p-value | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Local failure** | 6% | 13% | p=0.006 | Level I |
| **Sphincter preservation** | 39% | 19% | NS | Level I |
| **Grade 3–4 acute toxicity** | 27% | 40% | p=0.001 | Level I |
| **Long-term toxicity** | 14% | 24% | p=0.01 | Level I |
| **OS** | No significant difference | — | NS | Level I |

---

### Pre-operative CCRT vs RT Alone

| Outcome | CCRT (45 Gy + 5-FU/LV) | RT Alone | p-value | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Pathological complete response** | 11.4% | 3.6% | p<0.05 | Level I |
| **5-year local recurrence** | 8.1% | 16.5% | p<0.05 | Level I |
| **Grade 3–4 acute toxicity** | 14.6% | 2.7% | p<0.05 | Level I |
| **Sphincter preservation** | No difference | — | NS | Level I |
| **5-year OS** | No difference | — | NS | Level I |

> **Clinical Note:** Neoadjuvant CCRT has become the preferred option for T3-T4/N+ rectal carcinoma. Multidisciplinary approach and CRM assessment prior to deciding therapy is important.

#### Adjuvant Chemotherapy in Rectal Cancer

- Role **not well established** — two large RCTs showed no improvement in DFS or OS after pre-operative CCRT/RT and TME

#### High Risk Features for Stage II Rectal Carcinoma

| Feature |
|:---|
| Obstruction |
| Perforation |
| T4 disease |
| Positive CRM |
| Poorly differentiated tumour |
| Lymphovascular invasion |
| Inadequate lymph node sampling (<12) |
| Incomplete mesorectum |

#### Post-operative CCRT Indications (Appendix 7)

| Category | Indications |
|:---|:---|
| **Sufficient and necessary** | CRM ≤1 mm, pT4b, pN2 extracapsular spread close to MRF, extranodal deposits (N1c), pN2 with poor mesorectal quality/defects |
| **Sufficient** | pN2 low tumours <4 cm from anal verge (risk of LPLN involvement), extensive extramural vascular/perineural invasion close to MRF |
| **Borderline sufficient** | pN2 low tumours <4 cm from anal verge, CRM 1–2 mm, circumferential obstructing tumours |
| **Insufficient/unnecessary** | pT1/pT2, pT3, CRM >2 mm, pT4a above peritoneal reflection, pN1, good quality intact mesorectum |

> **Abbreviations:** CRM=circumferential resection margin; LPLN=lateral pelvic lymph node; MRF=mesorectal fascia

### Recommendation 12

- Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy should be offered to **T3-T4 or node positive** rectal carcinoma.

## 6.3 Metastatic or Locally Advanced CRC

### Overview

Stage IV CRC accounts for a third of total CRC cases in Malaysia. Management centres on palliation, symptom control, and prolonging PFS/OS. Treatment decisions require a **multidisciplinary team approach**.

### Palliative Chemotherapy Efficacy

| Regimen | Outcome vs Control | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Palliative chemotherapy (any)** | Prolonged TTP at 12 months (RR=0.86, 95% CI 0.77–0.96); improved OS at 18 months (RR=0.88, 95% CI 0.82–0.96) | Level I |
| **5-FU/LV (2-weekly bolus+infusion)** | Longer PFS (p=0.001) and lower grade 3–4 toxicity (p=0.0004) vs 5-day bolus; no OS difference | Level I |
| **FOLFIRI** | Response rate 49% (p=0.001); +3.3 months OS vs 5-FU/LV alone (p=0.031) | Level I |
| **FOLFOX4** | Response rate 50.7% (p=0.0001); no survival benefit vs 5-FU/LV | Level I |
| **FOLFOX6 vs FOLFIRI** | No difference in PFS or OS; different toxicity profiles | Level I |
| **Capecitabine vs IV 5-FU/LV** | Superior response rate, equivalent TTP/OS, improved safety profile | Level I |
| **Peri-operative FOLFOX4** (resectable liver mets) | Improved PFS (HR=0.73, 95% CI 0.55–0.97) | Level I |

#### Toxicity Comparison

| Toxicity | More Frequent With |
|:---|:---|
| **Grade 3 sensory neurotoxicity, neutropaenia, thrombocytopaenia** | FOLFOX6 |
| **Grade 3–4 febrile neutropaenia, nausea, vomiting, mucositis, fatigue** | FOLFIRI |
| **Serious adverse events** | FOLFIRI (14% vs 5%, p=0.03) |

---

### a. Targeted Therapy

| Agent | Regimen | Outcome | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Bevacizumab + FOLFIRI** | vs FOLFIRI alone | Median PFS: 10.6 vs 6.2 months; OS: 20.3 vs 15.6 months | Level I |
| **Bevacizumab + oxaliplatin-based** | vs chemo alone | Modest PFS improvement (9.4 vs 8 months, p=0.0023); no OS difference | Level I |
| **Cetuximab + FOLFIRI** (wild-type KRAS) | vs FOLFIRI alone | PFS: 9.9 vs 8.7 months; no OS difference | Level I |

> **Clinical Note:** Patients with **wild-type KRAS** are likely to respond to anti-EGFR therapy (cetuximab). KRAS testing should guide treatment selection.

> **Cost-effectiveness:** Bevacizumab (1st-line) and cetuximab (2nd-line) not cost-effective per HTA 2007 and US economic evaluation.

---

### b. Palliative Pelvic Radiotherapy

| Symptom | Response Rate | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Overall symptom response** | 75% pooled rate (1,084 cases) | Level I |
| **Pain** | 78% | Level I |
| **Bleeding and discharge** | 81% | Level I |
| **Mass effect** | 71% | Level I |
| **Other pelvic symptoms** | 72% | Level I |

### Recommendation 13

- Palliative chemotherapy may be considered in metastatic CRC.
- **Combination chemotherapy** is the preferred regime.
- Oral chemotherapy (capecitabine) may be considered as an alternative.

## Chemotherapy Drugs and Side Effects (Appendix 6)

| Drug | Key Side Effects | Important Precautions |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Capecitabine** | Hand-foot syndrome, nausea, diarrhoea | Use cautiously in heart disease; dose reduction in moderate renal dysfunction |
| **5-FU (Fluorouracil)** | Stomatitis, diarrhoea, myelosuppression | Use cautiously in heart disease; prophylactic anti-emetics and corticosteroids |
| **Irinotecan** | Early-onset diarrhoea, cholinergic symptoms | Prophylactic atropine for cholinergic symptoms; anti-emetics for moderate emetic risk |
| **Oxaliplatin** | Peripheral neuropathy, allergic reactions | Reduce dose or discontinue for neuropathy; monitor for rash, urticaria, bronchospasm, hypotension |
| **Leucovorin (LV)** | Generally well tolerated | Used as modulator for 5-FU |

> **Hand-foot syndrome:** Palms and soles become dry, red, numb, tingling, with/without swelling, blistering, moist desquamation or pain.
