## 4.1 Diagnostic Investigations

### a. Colonoscopy

Complete endoscopic colonoscopy is required in all suspected CRC cases to detect synchronous lesions and obtain biopsy specimens.

### b. Barium Enema (BE)

- Established radiological method for symptoms suggestive of CRC, but utilisation is declining
- Virtual colonoscopy (VC) has higher sensitivity: detection rate of CRC/large polyps significantly higher with VC vs BE (RR=1.31, 95% CI 1.01–1.68)

### c. CT Colonography (CTC) / Virtual Colonoscopy
- An evidence showed that CTC or VC is effective and safe in identifying
carcinoma and polyps >10 mm when compared with CC with high
sensitivity and specificity. **[2, level II]**

| Parameter | Detail | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **CTC vs CC detection** | CRC/large polyp detection rate 11% for both | Level I |
| **Additional investigation** | CTC required additional colonic investigation vs colonoscopy (RR=3.65, 95% CI 2.87–4.65) | Level I |
| **Missed CRC** | CTC missed 1 of 29 CRC; colonoscopy missed 0 of 55 | Level I |

**Advantages of CTC:**
- Evaluates whole colon (especially with stenosing lesions)
- Can detect, stage CRC and identify incidental pathology
- Radiation ~20% lower than double-contrast BE
- No sedation required

> **Clinical Note:** CTC is an alternative modality for symptomatic patients at high risk. Radiation exposure from CTC is approximately 20% lower than the
typical dose for double-contrast BE. It is a more sensitive test than BE and can be the preferred radiological test.

### d. Carcinoembryonic Antigen (CEA)

- **Definition:** A glycoprotein present in normal mucosal cells.
- **Clinical Significance:** Elevated amounts are associated with adenocarcinoma (especially CRC), establishing its role as a tumour marker.

> **Important:** A normal level of CEA does NOT indicate the absence of CRC.

## 4.2 Radiological Investigations for Staging

### a. Computed Tomography (CT)

| Parameter | Accuracy | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **T1-T2 staging** | 75% (poor agreement with histopathology; κ=0.208) | Level III |
| **T3+ staging** | 86% | Level III |
| **N staging** | Up to 80% | Level III |
| **Overall T- and N-staging (vs HPE)** | Sensitivity 70.2%, specificity 79.2% | Level III |

- Routinely used for primary staging and disease surveillance
- Identifies location, size, local extension, distant metastases, complications (perforation, obstruction, abscess)

> **Clinical Note:** CT is not the best modality for early CRC. Radiological staging must include **contrasted CT thorax**. Report must include TNM classification.

---

### b. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

| Application | Key Finding | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **CRM prediction** | Accuracy of 94% (95% CI 91–96) if tumour within 1 mm of mesorectal fascia | Level III |
| **Pre-operative staging** | Accurate assessment of cancer local spread; feasible, reproducible, reliable | Level III |
| **Mesorectal fascia** | Consistently shown on high-resolution MRI | Level III |

> **Clinical Note:** MRI is the **best modality** for assessing the relation of rectal carcinoma with the potential CRM and predicting whether surgical margins will be clear.

#### MRI for Colorectal Liver Metastasis

| Technique | AUC for Liver Metastasis Detection | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **DW-MRI + Gd-EOB-DTPA combined** | 0.96–0.97 | Level II-2 |
| **Gd-EOB-DTPA alone** | 0.86–0.89 | Level II-2 |
| **DW-MRI alone** | 0.92–0.93 | Level II-2 |

> **Clinical Note:** Combined reading of DW-MRI with hepatocyte-specific contrast (Gd-EOB-DTPA) in hepatocellular phase is highly suggested for detecting liver metastasis.

---

### c. Endorectal Ultrasound

| Parameter | Endorectal US | MRI | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **T-staging sensitivity** | 0.88 | 0.89 | Level I |
| **T-staging specificity** | 0.79 | 0.76 | Level I |
| **N-staging sensitivity** | 0.63 | 0.76 | Level I |
| **N-staging specificity** | 0.80 | 0.77 | Level I |
| **Early rectal carcinoma** | Pooled sensitivity 0.97, specificity 0.96 | — | Level II-2 |

**Limitations:**
- Limited to non-stenosing tumours
- Accuracy significantly reduced after neoadjuvant chemo/RT
- MRI of pelvis remains preferred choice for rectal carcinoma staging

---

### d. FDG PET-CT

| Application | Advantage over CT Alone | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **T4 disease** | More accurate | Level II-2 |
| **Distant metastases** | More accurate | Level II-2 |
| **Recurrence** | More accurate | Level II-2 |
| **Elevated CEA with equivocal CT** | Useful adjunct | Level III |

> **Clinical Note:**
> - Contrast-enhanced FDG PET-CT is preferred as it will provide more detailed information as compared with uncontrasted FDG PET-CT study.
> - FDG PET-CT has a role in the evaluation of recurrent CRC with elevated CEA and often with equivocal/negative CT.
> - In utilising an imaging modality with ionising radiation, the indication must be justified in which benefits from the diagnostic information obtained outweigh the risks associated with radiation.
> - Basic radiation safety principle "As Low as Reasonably Achievable" to minimise radiation doses and employ "reasonable methods" to patients must be adhered to at all times. This helps to prevent unnecessary exposure and overexposure.

### Recommendation 6

- CT scan should be used for staging and surveillance of CRC.
- MRI is the modality of choice for diagnosing and staging rectal carcinoma.

## 4.3 Histopathological Examination

HPE of the resected colorectal specimen is essential for patient's management, including the estimation of post-operative outcome and the rationale for adjuvant therapy.

### a. Important Pathological Parameters

**[Level III]** Royal College of Pathologists Minimum Pathology Dataset identifies significant prognostic factors for 5-year survival in surgically resected CRC as follows:
- local invasion
- total number of lymph nodes retrieved
- nodal stage (number of lymph nodes involved by cancer)
- extramural vascular invasion
- peritoneal involvement
- tumour perforation
- distance of invasion beyond the muscularis propria
- CRM involvement and distance to this margin (for rectal carcinoma)

**Additional Clinical & Histological Prognosticators:**
- **WHO Classification:** **[Level III]** The World Health Organisation (WHO) Classification of CRC has been widely used because of its consistency and uniformity in pathologic reporting of colorectal histologic tumour type (refer to **Appendix 4**).
- **Tumour Subtype:** **[Level III]** Most CRC are adenocarcinoma. Some other histologic subtypes such as signet-ring and mucin-producing carcinoma have significantly poor prognostic features.
- **Lymph Node Yield:** **[Level III]** Patients with >12 nodes retrieved have significantly higher survival rate compared with those lower nodal yield (53.0% vs 45.4%; p<0.01).
- **MMR Deficiency / Lynch Syndrome:** **[Level III]** Evaluation for Lynch syndrome may include tumour screening for mismatch repair (MMR) deficiency. MMR-deficient (or microsatellite instability-high) carcinoma frequently demonstrates mucinous differentiation or medullary features in the form of a solid architecture with prominent tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes. MMR status can be readily evaluated by immunohistochemistry (IHC). However, given the resource implications of implementing this, it is not considered a core data item for reporting CRC resection specimen.
- **Tumour Differentiation:** **[Level III]** Tumour differentiation is important for prognosis. The prognostic validity of grade is highly significant, showing a better survival for the well-differentiated carcinomas compared with poorly differentiated type.
- **Perineural Invasion:** **[Level I]** In a systematic review, perineural invasion remained an independent prognostic factor for five-year disease-free survival (DFS), five-year overall survival (OS) and five-year cancer-specific survival.

**Prognostic Factors for Local Recurrence in Rectal Carcinoma:**
- **[Level III]** location below the peritoneal reflection and completeness of the plane of mesorectal excision
- **[Level III]** histologic grade of regression after pre-operative therapy
- **[Level III]** CRM
- **[Level III]** distal resection margin (DRM) (Hazard ratio (HR) for 5-year local recurrence in DRM ≤10 mm=2.33, 95% CI 1.28 to 4.25)

---

> **Clinical Note:** **[Level III]** Histopathological reports of CRC should include core histological data, which are:
> 
> **i. Macroscopic core items**
> - Nature of specimen and type of operation
> - Site of tumour
> - Maximum tumour diameter
> - Distance to nearer longitudinal resection margin
> - Relation of tumour to the peritoneal reflection (rectal tumours only)
> - Grade of plane(s) of surgical excision (total mesorectal excision (TME) for anterior resection and abdominoperineal resection (APR) specimens)
> 
> **ii. Microscopic core items**
> - Histological tumour type
> - Histological differentiation
> - Maximum extent of local invasion (pT stage) and maximum distance of extramural spread
> - Grade of tumour regression following pre-operative (neoadjuvant) therapy
> - Resection margins (longitudinal and circumferential margins)
> - Lymph nodes status (number present, number involved, highest lymph node status)
> - Venous invasion
> - Perineural invasion

---

### TNM Staging

- **Prognostic Significance:** **[Level III]** Clinical pathological stage is the most significant independent prognostic factor in CRC, classified by the Astler and Coller system (with stage D proposed by Turnbull) or pTNM.
- **International Standard:** CRC staging according to TNM is widely used internationally (refer to **Appendix 4**).
- **Edition Discrepancies:** **[Level III]** Major changes between the 5th and 7th editions concerning lymph node definitions destabilise historical staging data. Therefore, the **5th edition criteria** are retained for reporting.
- **Local Practice:** In the local setting, the pathology report include **both the 5th and 7th editions** of TNM staging, as well as Dukes' staging.

---

### b. Reporting

Complete and accurate histopathology reports are fundamental in providing quality cancer care. **[Level I]** A randomised controlled trial (RCT) showed that the use of pre-defined forms led to a 24.5% (95% CI 11.0 to 38.0) increase in complete reporting of a minimum required data for patient management.

**[Level III]** National electronic template reporting improves the inclusion of important key parameters for CRC resection specimen compared with reporting by checklists, locally developed electronic templates or free text (p<0.05).

In view of the importance of high quality reporting of CRC resection specimen, Pathology Service of MoH has developed a standardised histopathology reporting proforma for this purpose (refer to **Appendix 5**).

### Recommendation 7

- In CRC, reporting of resection specimens should contain core macroscopic and microscopic histological items.
- Standardised histopathology reporting proforma incorporating TNM staging should be used.
- Minimum of **12 lymph nodes** should be aimed for proper HPE.

---

### Levels of Evidence

* **Level I** = Evidence from at least one properly randomised controlled trial
* **Level II-2** = Evidence obtained from well-designed cohort or case-control analytic studies, preferably from more than one centre or group
* **Level III** = Opinions of respected authorities based on clinical experience; descriptive studies and case reports; or reports of expert committees

## Reference: Appendix 4 (WHO Classification and TNM Staging)
- **WHO Classification (2010):** Most CRCs are Adenocarcinoma (usual-type). Subtypes like Mucinous (>50% component) and Signet-ring cell carcinoma portend a poor prognosis.
- **TNM Primary Tumour (pT):** pT1 (submucosa), pT2 (muscularis propria), pT3 (beyond muscularis propria), pT4a (visceral peritoneum), pT4b (adjacent organs).
- **TNM Regional Nodes (pN):** pN0 (0 nodes), pN1 (1-3 nodes), pN2 (4-7+ nodes). Minimum requirement is 12 nodes evaluated.
- **TNM Distant Metastasis (pM):** pM1a (single site), pM1b (multiple sites/peritoneal).
- **Dukes' Staging:** A (limited to wall, nodes negative), B (beyond muscularis propria, nodes negative), C1/C2 (nodes positive, depending on highest node), D (distant metastasis).

## Reference: Appendix 5 (Histopathology Reporting Proforma)
- **1. Gross Description:** Specimen type, tumour site, maximum diameter, distance to margins, relation to peritoneal reflection, and grade of TME/APR plane.
- **2. Microscopic Assessment:** Tumour type, differentiation, pT stage, extramural spread distance, neoadjuvant regression grade.
- **Margin & Node Assessment:** Clear/involved measurements for proximal, distal, and circumferential margins (≤1 mm = involved). Explicit counting of total vs involved nodes.
- **Complete Resection Status:** R0 (complete, >1 mm margins), R1 (microscopic involvement), R2 (macroscopic involvement).
