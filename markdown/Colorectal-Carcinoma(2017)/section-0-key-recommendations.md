## Overview

The following recommendations were highlighted by the CPG Development Group as the key clinical recommendations that should be prioritised for implementation in the management of Colorectal Carcinoma (CRC).

---

## Screening & Surveillance

| Recommendation | Details |
|:---|:---|
| **Average risk screening** | Offer CRC screening at age 50–75 years |
| **Preferred method** | Immunochemical faecal occult blood test (iFOBT) |
| **If iFOBT positive** | Early colonoscopy is necessary |
| **If iFOBT negative** | Yearly test should be performed |
| **Positive family history** | Asymptomatic individuals should be screened; colonoscopy per risk category |
| **Hereditary syndromes** | Refer to clinical genetics service for genetic risk assessment |

---

## Diagnostic & Staging

| Recommendation | Details |
|:---|:---|
| **CT scan** | Should be used for staging and surveillance of CRC |
| **MRI** | Modality of choice for diagnosing and staging rectal carcinoma |
| **Histopathology** | Standardised reporting proforma incorporating TNM staging should be used |
| **Lymph nodes** | Minimum of 12 lymph nodes should be aimed for proper HPE |

---

## Surgical Management

| Recommendation | Details |
|:---|:---|
| **Pre-operative** | Antibiotic prophylaxis + VTE prophylaxis required |
| **Bowel preparation** | Should be performed in rectal surgery; may be performed in colon surgery |
| **Surgical exploration** | Thorough exploration at time of resection |
| **Low rectal surgery** | Should be performed by credentialed surgeons |
| **TME** | Total mesorectal excision for middle and low rectal carcinoma |
| **APR** | If required, should be performed as cylindrical APR |
| **Metastatic CRC** | Individualised treatment guided by multidisciplinary approach |

---

## Chemotherapy & Radiotherapy

| Recommendation | Details |
|:---|:---|
| **Stage II colon (high risk)** | Adjuvant chemotherapy may be considered; careful selection and counselling |
| **Stage III colon** | 5-FU/LV with oxaliplatin should be given |
| **Rectal (T3-T4/N+)** | Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy should be offered |
| **Metastatic CRC** | Palliative chemotherapy may be considered; combination regime preferred |
| **Oral chemotherapy** | May be considered as alternative (capecitabine) |

---

## 2. TREATMENT ALGORITHMS

### ALGORITHM A: SCREENING FOR COLORECTAL CARCINOMA

#### Step 1: Initial Assessment
**ASSESS PATIENT FOR SYMPTOMS OF CRC\***

↓

**PRESENCE OF SYMPTOM(S)**
* **YES:** → **REFER ALGORITHM B**
* **NO:** → **STRATIFY RISK FOR FAMILY HISTORY**

---

#### Step 2: Risk Stratification
**STRATIFY RISK FOR FAMILY HISTORY**

Categorize the patient into one of the following groups:

**Pathway A: CATEGORY 1 - AVERAGE RISK\*\***
* Perform **IMMUNOFAECAL OCCULT BLOOD TESTING (iFOBT)**
    * **If POSITIVE:** → **REFER FOR COLONOSCOPY**
    * **If NEGATIVE:** → **REPEAT iFOBT YEARLY**

**Pathway B: CATEGORY 2 - MODERATE RISK\*\***
* → **REFER FOR COLONOSCOPY**

**Pathway C: CATEGORY 3 - HIGH RISK\*\***
* → **REFER FOR COLONOSCOPY**

### Algorithm B: Primary Care Referral for Symptoms of Colorectal Carcinoma

#### Step 1: Initial Presentation
**Presence of any of the following signs or symptoms:**
* per rectal bleeding
* mucoid stool
* loss of weight or appetite
* abdominal discomfort
* altered bowel habits
* perianal symptoms
* tenesmus
* constipation
* anaemia
* palpable abdominal mass
* palpable anorectal mass

↓

#### Step 2: Clinical Assessment

**Focused history**
* Age and sex
* Rectal bleeding (colour)
* Altered bowel habit (alternating constipation and diarrhoea)
* Perianal symptoms (lump, pruritus, pain, discharge)
* Symptoms of anaemia (look for causes)
* Personal history of colorectal polyps or inflammatory bowel disease, or family history of CRC

**Focused physical examination and tests**
* Weight
* Look for signs of anaemia
* Abdominal examination
* Digital rectal examination and proctoscopy
* Full blood count

↓

#### Step 3: Referral Pathways

**Pathway A: Urgent**
**Criteria:**
Unexplained rectal bleeding with ≥1 of the following:
* fresh blood
* blood mixed with stool
* with altered bowel habits
* with significant weight loss
**AND/OR**
Unexplained iron deficiency anaemia
**AND/OR**
Palpable abdominal or rectal mass
* **Action:** → **URGENT REFERRAL FOR COLONOSCOPY WITHIN TWO WEEKS**

**Pathway B: Non-Urgent**
**Criteria:**
All other unexplained signs and symptoms that do not meet criteria for urgent referral
* **Initial Action:** → Treat signs or symptoms accordingly
* **Follow-up:** If signs and symptoms not resolved in 4-6 weeks → **REFER FOR ELECTIVE COLONOSCOPY**


### ALGORITHM C: TREATMENT FOR COLON CARCINOMA

#### Step 1: Initial Assessment
**Determine disease stage**

↓

#### Step 2: Treatment Pathways based on Staging

**Pathway 1: T1-T2 N0 M0**
* **Primary Treatment:** Surgery
* **Follow-up:** → **SURVEILLANCE**

**Pathway 2: T3-T4 N0 M0**
* **Primary Treatment:** Surgery
* **Secondary Assessment:** Check for High risk features*
    * **If No:** → **SURVEILLANCE**
    * **If Yes:** → Adjuvant chemotherapy → **SURVEILLANCE**

**Pathway 3: T1-T4 N1-N2 M0**
* **Primary Treatment:** Surgery
* **Secondary Treatment:** Adjuvant chemotherapy
* **Follow-up:** → **SURVEILLANCE**

**Pathway 4: T1-T4 Any N M1**
* **Treatment Options Include:**
    * Curative or palliative surgery
    * Palliative chemotherapy
    * Best supportive care

---

#### 📝 Clinical Notes
> **\*High risk features for stage II colon carcinoma are presence of any of the following:**
> * obstruction
> * perforation
> * T4 disease
> * poorly differentiated tumour
> * lymphovascular invasion
> * perineural invasion
> * inadequate lymph node sampling (<12)

### ALGORITHM D: TREATMENT FOR RECTAL CARCINOMA

#### Step 1: Initial Assessment
**Determine disease stage**

↓

#### Step 2: Treatment Pathways based on Staging

**Pathway 1: T1-T2 N0 M0**
* **Primary Treatment:** Surgery
* **Follow-up:** → **SURVEILLANCE**

**Pathway 2: T3-T4 N0 M0 OR T1-T4 N1-N2 M0**
*(Note: There are two options for this pathway)*
* **Option A:** Surgery → Adjuvant CCRT → **SURVEILLANCE**
* **Option B (Preferred option):** Neoadjuvant CCRT → Surgery → **SURVEILLANCE**

**Pathway 3: T1-T4 Any N M1**
* **Treatment Options Include:**
  * Surgery
  * Palliative radiotherapy
  * Palliative chemotherapy
  * Best supportive care

---

#### 📝 Clinical Notes & Abbreviations

> **\*High risk features for stage II rectal carcinoma are presence of any of the following:**
> * obstruction
> * perforation
> * T4 disease
> * positive CRM
> * poorly differentiated tumour
> * lymphovascular invasion
> * perineural invasion
> * inadequate lymph node sampling (<12)
> * incomplete mesorectum
