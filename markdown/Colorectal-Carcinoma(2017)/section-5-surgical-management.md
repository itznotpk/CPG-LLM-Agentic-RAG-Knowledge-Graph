## Overview

The mainstay of treatment for CRC is surgical resection, which offers the best curative outcome. Chemotherapy and RT are used to downstage, as adjuvant therapy and for palliative purposes. The treatments for colon and rectal carcinoma are outlined in **Algorithm C** and **Algorithm D**.

Surgery involves en-bloc removal of the cancer with clear margins and its associated regional lymphatic drainage. The aim is to achieve **complete resection (R0)**.

---

## 5.1 Pre-Operative Preparation

### a. Antibiotic Prophylaxis

| Parameter | Detail | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Wound infection reduction** | 66% reduction (RR=0.34, 95% CI 0.28–0.41) | Level I |
| **Best coverage** | Combined aerobic + anaerobic antibiotics | Level I |
| **Duration** | No difference in outcomes with varying duration | Level I |

---

### b. Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) Prophylaxis

| Intervention | Outcome | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Heparin** | Significantly prevented DVT/PE (OR=0.32, 95% CI 0.02–0.53) | Level I |
| **UFH vs LMWH** | No difference (OR=1.01, 95% CI 0.67–1.52) | Level I |
| **Combined modalities** (IPC + anticoagulants) | Reduced VTE by 61–84% vs single method | Level I |

---

### c. Bowel Preparation

**[Level I]** A Cochrane systematic review of 15 trials showed no significant difference between mechanical bowel preparation and no bowel preparation in terms of:
- anastomotic leakage
- peritonitis
- mortality
- wound infection
- re-operation

**[Level I]** In another sub-analysis on rectal surgery, there was no difference between mechanical bowel preparation and rectal enema for the above outcomes.

> **Clinical Note:** Even though there was no difference in post-operative complications between mechanical bowel preparation and no bowel preparation, consensus among RC and DG agreed that bowel preparation prior to rectal carcinoma surgery resulted in lesser morbidity.

### Recommendation 8

- Patients undergoing CRC surgery should have **antibiotic prophylaxis** and **VTE prophylaxis**.
- Mechanical bowel preparation: **should be** performed in rectal surgery; **may be** performed in colon surgery.

## 5.2 Techniques in Colorectal Surgery

### a. Colon Carcinoma

| Principle | Detail | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Surgical exploration** | Thorough intra-operative assessment to exclude synchronous lesion, extent of disease, distant metastases | — |
| **Complete mesocolic excision** | Reduced local recurrence (3.6% vs 6.5%), improved 5-year survival (89.1% vs 82.1%) | Level II-2 |
| **Lymph node harvest** | Minimum 12 nodes to assign N0 stage | Level I |
| **Synchronous colon carcinoma** | Two separate resections or subtotal colectomy | Level I |

> **Clinical Note:** Resection margins of CRC specimens must be tagged for orientation.

---

### b. Rectal Carcinoma

| Principle | Detail | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Credentialed surgeons** | Improved survival, fewer complications, more restorative procedures, fewer permanent ostomies | Level I |
| **Upper rectum** | Mesorectal excision extending 5 cm below distal edge of cancer | Level I |
| **Middle and lower rectum** | Total mesorectal excision (TME) required | Level I |
| **CRM** | Positive CRM = independent predictor of local recurrence and decreased survival | Level I |
| **Distal mural margin** | 2 cm adequate with TME; 1 cm acceptable at/below mesorectal margin | Level I |
| **Defunctioning stoma** | Decreased clinical anastomotic leak and re-operation rates (p<0.001); loop ileostomy preferred | Level I |
| **Rectal washout** | No strong evidence supporting it for preventing local recurrence; relatively risk-free | Level II-1 |
| **Cylindrical APR** (low rectal) | Reduced CRM involvement (p<0.013) and intra-operative perforations (p<0.026) vs traditional APR | Level III |

**Surgical Options for Rectal Carcinoma:**
- TEO (transanal endoscopic operation)
- TEMS (transanal endoscopic microsurgery)
- TAMIS (transanal minimally invasive surgery)
- Pelvic exenteration (for locally advanced with adjacent organ involvement)

---

### c. Laparoscopic Surgery

| Outcome | Laparoscopic vs Open | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Operative mortality** | No significant difference | Level I |
| **Recurrence at primary site** | No significant difference | Level I |
| **Blood loss** | Lesser in laparoscopic | Level I |
| **Post-operative hospital stay** | Shorter in laparoscopic | Level I |
| **Post-operative ileus** | Lower in laparoscopic | Level I |
| **Wound infections** | Lower in laparoscopic | Level I |

> **Clinical Note:** Same surgical principles apply in both open and laparoscopic resection.

### Recommendation 9

- A thorough surgical exploration should be performed at the time of resection.
- Low rectal surgery should be performed by **credentialed surgeons**.
- **TME** should be performed for middle and low rectal carcinoma.
- If APR is required, it should be performed as **cylindrical APR**.

## 5.3 Surgical Treatment of Metastatic CRC

### a. Liver Metastases

**Treatment options:** Hepatectomy, systemic chemotherapy, hepatic arterial infusion therapy, thermal coagulation therapy.

#### Criteria for Liver Resection

| Criterion | Detail | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Patient fitness** | Overall health, organ/liver function, concomitant disease assessed | Level III |
| **Primary cancer** | Controlled or can be controlled | Level III |
| **Extrahepatic metastases** | None, or can be controlled | Level III |
| **Resectability** | Margin-negative (R0) resection ≥1 mm achievable | Level III |
| **Liver remnant** | Preserve ≥2 contiguous segments; adequate vascular inflow/outflow/biliary drainage | Level III |
| **Future liver remnant volume** | ≥25% (normal liver); ≥35–40% (pre-treated/damaged liver) | Level III |

#### Other Modalities

| Modality | Role | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **RFA** | Role in unresectable metastases ± surgical resection. Insufficient evidence as standalone curative treatment | Level I |
| **SIRT** | Limited evidence for improvement in response rates and OS. Strict patient selection required | Level I |

---

### b. Pulmonary Metastases

| Parameter | Detail | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **5-year survival** | 41–56% in selected patients | Level III |
| **Mortality** | Low rate with curative resection | Level III |

#### Conditions for Curative Pulmonary Resection

- Technically resectable pulmonary metastases
- Patient fit for surgery
- Primary cancer controlled
- No extrathoracic metastases (except resectable hepatic lesions)
- Adequate remaining lung function

---

### c. Hepatopulmonary Metastases

- Offer curative resection if: physiologically capable + primary cancer controlled + no extra-hepatic/extra-pulmonary disease
- **Median survival:** 54.2 months; **5-year survival:** 43%

### Recommendation 10

- Treatment for metastatic CRC should be individualised and guided by a **multidisciplinary approach**.

---

## 5.4 Cancer-Related Emergencies

### a. Bleeding

| Principle | Detail | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Localised colon carcinoma** | Follow same resection principles as elective surgery | Level I |
| **Acute massive bleeding** | Selective embolisation → surgical resection is definitive | Level I |
| **Unlocalisable bleeding** | Consider subtotal colectomy if colonic cancer suspected | Level I |

---

### b. Perforation

| Scenario | Management | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Perforation proximal to obstructing cancer** | Resect cancer + perforated segment | Level I |
| **Perforation at cancer site (contained)** | En-bloc resection incorporating adjacent structures | Level I |
| **Primary anastomosis** | Consider if minimal contamination, healthy tissue, clinical stability | Level I |

> **Clinical Note:** Prognosis of colon perforation due to carcinoma is worse than from other causes due to association with advanced cancer or sepsis.

---

### c. Obstruction

| Option | Indication | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Resection ± anastomosis** | e.g. right hemicolectomy, Hartmann's procedure | Level I |
| **Resection of distended bowel** | e.g. subtotal/total colectomy | Level I |
| **Temporary relief** | Stoma or stenting | Level I |
| **Hartmann's procedure** | No survival benefit over segmental resection + primary anastomosis; consider in high surgical risk | Level III |
| **Colonic stenting** | If unfit for immediate resection or advanced disease; no advantage over emergency surgery in Cochrane review | Level I |

> **Clinical Note:** Prognosis in obstructing cancers may be worse due to more advanced disease, but this does not preclude curative resection.

---

### Levels of Evidence

* **Level I** = Evidence from at least one properly randomised controlled trial
* **Level II-1** = Evidence obtained from well-designed controlled trials without randomisation
* **Level II-2** = Evidence obtained from well-designed cohort or case-control analytic studies, preferably from more than one centre or group
* **Level III** = Opinions of respected authorities based on clinical experience; descriptive studies and case reports; or reports of expert committees

### Abbreviations
* **APR** = Abdominoperineal resection
* **CCRT** = Concurrent chemoradiotherapy
* **CI** = Confidence interval
* **CRC** = Colorectal carcinoma
* **CRM** = Circumferential resection margins
* **FOLFOX** = 5-FU/LV + oxaliplatin
* **MRI** = Magnetic resonance imaging
* **OR** = Odds ratio
* **OS** = Overall survival
* **RFA** = Radiofrequency ablation
* **RR** = Relative risk
* **RT** = Radiotherapy
* **SIRT** = Selective internal radiation therapy
* **TME** = Total mesorectal excision
* **TNM** = Tumour-Node-Metastasis
* **VTE** = Venous thromboembolism

## Reference: Algorithm C (Treatment for Colon Carcinoma)
- **Early Stage (T1-T2 N0 M0):** Surgery → Surveillance.
- **Intermediate Stage (T3-T4 N0 M0):** Surgery → Check for **High Risk Features***.
    - If Yes: Adjuvant Chemotherapy → Surveillance.
    - If No: Surveillance.
- **Node Positive (T1-T4 N1-N2 M0):** Surgery → Adjuvant Chemotherapy → Surveillance.
- **Metastatic (Any T, Any N, M1):** Curative or Palliative Surgery, Palliative Chemotherapy, or Best Supportive Care.

> **\*High risk features (Stage II):** Obstruction, perforation, T4 disease, poorly differentiated tumour, lymphovascular/perineural invasion, <12 nodes sampled.

## Reference: Algorithm D (Treatment for Rectal Carcinoma)
- **Early Stage (T1-T2 N0 M0):** Surgery → Surveillance.
- **Intermediate/Node Positive (T3-T4 N0 OR T1-T4 N1-N2 M0):**
    - **Option A:** Surgery → Adjuvant CCRT.
    - **Option B (Preferred):** Neoadjuvant CCRT → Surgery → Surveillance.
- **Metastatic (Any T, Any N, M1):** Surgery, Palliative RT, Palliative Chemotherapy, or Best Supportive Care.

> **\*High risk features (Stage II):** Same as colon + positive CRM, incomplete mesorectum.
