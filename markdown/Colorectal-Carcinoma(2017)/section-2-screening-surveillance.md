## Overview

Colorectal carcinoma (CRC) arises predominantly from adenomatous polyps progressing through the adenoma-carcinoma sequence. This slow transition provides a window for screening and early detection. Multiple screening modalities are available, each with distinct sensitivity, specificity and clinical utility.

---

## 2.1 Screening in Average Risk Population

Average risk population is defined as those with no known risk factors for CRC. Most major CRC guidelines recommend screening from **age 50 years**.

### Screening Intervals by Method (Table 1)

| Methodology | Minimum Interval |
|:---|:---|
| **Faecal occult blood test** | Yearly |
| **Stool DNA test** | 3-yearly |
| **CT colonography** | 5-yearly |
| **Colonoscopy** | 10-yearly |

> **Clinical Note:** The US Preventive Task Force recommends screening from age 50 to 75 years. For ages 76–85, screening should be individualised based on overall health and prior screening history.

---

## 2.2 Screening Modalities

### a. Faecal Tests

Faecal test is a non-invasive tool for screening CRC in general population. It can detect presence of blood, proteins e.g. enzyme M2-PK and DNA.

**[9, level III]**Faecal occult blood test (FOBT) has qualitative and quantitative testing methods. In a meta-analysis of fair to high quality evidence, the pooled sensitivity to detect CRC was 74% (95% CI 62 to 83) for quantitative test methods and 79% (95% CI 61 to 90) for qualitative test methods.

| Test | Sensitivity | Specificity | CRC Mortality Reduction | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **iFOBT (immunochemical)** | 67% (95% CI 0.61–0.73) | 85% (95% CI 0.83–0.87) | — | 10, Level I |
| **gFOBT (guaiac-based)** | 54% (95% CI 0.48–0.60) | 80% (95% CI 0.78–0.82) | — | 10, Level I |
| **FOBT (General)** | — | — | 16% (RR=0.84, 95% CI 0.78–0.90) | 11, Level I |
| **Faecal M2-PK** | 79% (95% CI 73–83) | 80% (95% CI 73–86) | — | 12, Level I |
| **Quantitative faecal DNA** | 92% (95% CI 84–97) | — | — | 9, Level III |

> **Clinical Note:** **[13, level II-2]**In a health technology assessment (HTA) report by MaHTAS, screening programme using iFOBT can be effective for prevention of advanced CRC (risk of developing advanced CRC was reduced by 28-46%) and reduced mortality by 23-60%. Regular iFOBT can detect pre-cancerous lesions and CRC in early stages and thus reduce mortality from CRC.

> **Note:** These two faecal tests (Faecal M2-PK and quantitative faecal DNA) for CRC screening are, however, not widely used locally in screening for general population due to high cost incurred.

---

### b. Sigmoidoscopy

| Parameter | Value | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **CRC incidence reduction** | 18–32% | Level I |
| **CRC mortality reduction** | 26–38% | Level I |
| **Bowel perforation risk** | Low incidence | Level I |

- Less rigorous bowel preparation required; can be clinic-based without sedation
- Small polyps can be biopsied; larger lesions (>1 cm) excised during subsequent colonoscopy

---

### c. Colonoscopy

| Parameter | Value | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **CRC incidence reduction** | 56% | Level II-2 |
| **CRC death reduction** | 68% | Level II-2 |
| **CRC risk reduction (up to 10 years)** | 91% | Level II-2 |
| **GI bleeding rate** | 0.29–1.59 per 1,000 colonoscopies | Level II-2 |
| **Perforation rate** | 0.19–0.89 per 1,000 colonoscopies | Level II-2 |

- Ability to visualise colonic mucosa directly, perform biopsy and excise polyps
- Can detect proximal lesions missed by sigmoidoscopy

> **Clinical Note:** A good quality colonoscopy should be practised to ensure effective and safe screening.

---

### d. Colon Capsule Endoscopy (CCE)

| Parameter | Value | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Sensitivity (polyps any size)** | 71% (95% CI 66–76) | Level II-2 |
| **Specificity (polyps any size)** | 75% (95% CI 66–83) | Level II-2 |
| **Side effects** | 4.1% (mild-moderate: nausea, abdominal pain) | Level II-2 |

- Less invasive but does **not** allow biopsy or polyp removal

---

### e. CT Colonography (CTC) / Virtual Colonoscopy

| Polyp Size | Sensitivity | Specificity | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **≥6 mm** | 82.9% (95% CI 74–89) | 91.4% (95% CI 84–96) | Level II-2 |
| **≥10 mm** | 87.9% (95% CI 82–92) | 97.6% (95% CI 95–99) | Level II-2 |

- Requires bowel preparation similar to colonoscopy; no sedation needed
- **Drawbacks:** Radiation exposure, need for subsequent colonoscopy for excision, flat adenomas more likely missed

---

### f. Carcinoembryonic Antigen (CEA)

> **Important:** No recommendation by USPSTF, NICE or SIGN for CEA as a CRC screening tool. CEA should **not** be relied on for screening purposes. Its role is confined to monitoring and follow-up.

---

### Recommendation 1

- Screening of CRC should be offered at age 50 years, continuing until age 75 years for average risk population.
- iFOBT is the preferred screening method for average risk population.
- If iFOBT positive → early colonoscopy is necessary.
- If iFOBT negative → yearly test should be performed.

---

## 2.3 Screening/Surveillance in Moderate and High Risk Groups

### 2.3.1 Family History

Family history is a well-established risk factor for CRC, affected by first-, second- and third-degree relatives.

#### a. First-Degree Relatives (FDRs) (Table 2)

| No. of Affected FDRs | FRR (95% CI) |
|:---|:---|
| 0 | 0.89 (0.87–0.91) |
| 1 | 1.91 (1.82–2.00) |
| ≥1 | 2.05 (1.96–2.14) |
| 2 | 3.01 (2.66–3.38) |
| 3 | 4.43 (3.24–5.90) |
| 4 | 7.74 (3.71–14.24) |
| 5 | 19.86 (7.26–43.24) |

- Asymptomatic patients with ≥1 FDR of CRC have greater risk of severely dysplastic lesions (OR=2.9, 95% CI 1.0–7.8)
- RR for ≥1 affected FDRs: **1.64** (1 relative) and **2.83** (≥2 relatives)
- Risk is specific to **colon carcinoma** (RR=1.99) but not significant for rectal carcinoma

#### Age at Diagnosis and Risk

| Age at Diagnosis | FRR (95% CI) | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **<45 years** | 4.63 (1.43–15.0) | Level II-2 |
| **<50 years** | 3.31 (2.79–3.89) | Level III |

---

#### b. Combined FDR + SDR + TDR Risk (Table 3)

| FDRs | SDRs | TDRs | FRR (95% CI) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.83 (0.81–0.86) |
| 0 | 1 | 2 | 1.33 (1.13–1.55) |
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 1.76 (1.63–1.89) |
| 1 | 1 | ≥3 | 3.28 (2.44–4.31) |
| 1 | ≥3 | ≥3 | 5.20 (2.24–10.24) |

---

#### Risk Categories for Family History (Table 4)

| Category | Description | Screening Recommendation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Category 1 (Average risk)** | No family history, age >50 | iFOBT per Algorithm A. Stop at 75. |
| **Category 2 (Moderate risk)** | ≥1 FDR; or 1 FDR + >1 SDR; or >3 relatives (≥1 FDR) | FDR diagnosed **<60 years:** colonoscopy at age 40 or 10 years before youngest affected relative, repeat 3–5 yearly. FDR diagnosed **≥60 years:** colonoscopy at age 40, repeat 10-yearly. Stop at 75. |
| **Category 3 (High risk)** | Family history of CRC <50; FAP; Lynch (HNPCC); Peutz-Jeghers; Juvenile polyposis; MAP | CRC <50 years: colonoscopy at 40 or 10 years before youngest affected. If normal, repeat 3–5 yearly. Hereditary syndromes: per Table 5. |

### Recommendation 2

- Asymptomatic individuals with positive family history should be screened for CRC.
- Colonoscopy should be performed according to risk category (refer to Table 4).

---

### 2.3.2 Post-Adenomatous Polypectomy

#### Risk Factors for Advanced Adenoma Recurrence

| Risk Factor | Risk Estimate | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Number of adenomas (1–2 vs 3)** | Pooled RR=2.52 (95% CI 1.07–5.97) | Level I |
| **Size (<1 cm vs ≥1 cm)** | Pooled RR=1.39 (95% CI 0.86–2.26) | Level I |
| **Villous histology** | Pooled RR=1.26 (95% CI 0.95–1.66) | Level I |
| **High-grade dysplasia** | Pooled RR=1.84 (95% CI 1.06–3.19) | Level I |
| **Proximal location** | Significantly 2x higher risk | Level II-2 |
| **Male with large adenomas** | RR=1.81 (95% CI 1.42–2.31) | Level II-2 |
| **Parental history of CRC** | RR=2.32 (95% CI 1.77–3.04) | Level II-2 |

#### Surveillance Intervals

| Risk Level | Surveillance Colonoscopy Interval |
|:---|:---|
| **Low risk** | Every 10 years after initial clearing |
| **High risk (advanced adenoma)** | Every 3 years after initial clearing |

> **Note:** Risk of recurrent advanced adenoma during surveillance is 2–3x higher among those with advanced adenoma vs low risk group.

### Recommendation 3

- Surveillance colonoscopy should be offered after removal of adenomatous polyps: every **10 years** for low risk and every **3 years** for high risk patients.

### 2.3.3 Hereditary Colorectal Cancer Syndromes (Table 5)

| Syndrome | Lifetime CRC Risk | Initial Screening Age | Modality | Surveillance Interval |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **FAP** | **[Level III]** 2.4x increase per 10 years | **[Level III]** Gene carriers: 10–14 years; **[Level III]** Attenuated FAP: 18–20 years | **[Level III]** Sigmoidoscopy initially → colonoscopy once adenoma detected. **[Level III]** Attenuated FAP: colonoscopy | **[Level III]** 1–2 yearly until surgery. **[Level III]** FDR without APC mutation: 2-yearly until 40, then 3–5 yearly, may stop at 50 |
| **Lynch Syndrome (HNPCC)** | **[Level III]** 78% cumulative lifetime risk | **[Level II-2/III]** 20–25 years or 5 years before earliest family diagnosis | **[Level II-2/III]** Colonoscopy | **[Level III]** 1–2 yearly |
| **Peutz-Jeghers** | **[Level III]** 39% cumulative risk | **[Level III]** 8 years | **[Level III]** Colonoscopy | **[Level III]** If polyps found: every 3 years. If no polyps: repeat at 18, then every 3 years |
| **Juvenile Polyposis** | **[Level III]** 38% cumulative risk | **[Level III]** 15 years (earlier if symptoms, especially rectal bleeding) | **[Level III]** Colonoscopy | **[Level III]** 2-yearly |
| **MAP (MUTYH-Associated)** | **[Level II-2]** 63% cumulative at age 60 | **[Level II-2]** 18–20 years | **[Level II-2]** Colonoscopy | **[Level II-2]** 1–2 yearly |

### Recommendation 4

- All hereditary CRC syndromes should be referred to and managed by colorectal surgeons.
- Surveillance should be based on the specific risk condition (refer to Table 5 and IBD section below).

### 2.3.4 Inflammatory Bowel Diseases (IBD)

| Condition | CRC Risk | Surveillance | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Ulcerative Colitis (UC)** | 2% at 10 years, 8% at 20 years, 18% at 30 years | Annual colonoscopy 7–8 years after symptom onset | Risk: Level II-2<br>Surv: Level III / II-2 |
| **Colonic Crohn's Disease** | Similar risk to UC | Same surveillance programme as UC | Level II-2 |

### 2.3.5 Other Risk Factors

| Risk Factor | Association with CRC | Evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Smoking (>25 years)** | 16% greater risk; RR=1.23 (95% CI 1.07–1.41) | Level II-2 |
| **Diabetes mellitus** | RR=1.27 (95% CI 1.21–1.34) | Level II-2 |
| **Obesity (high BMI)** | Significant positive association in males | Level II-2 |
| **Alcohol (>30 g/day)** | RR=1.23 (95% CI 1.07–1.12) | Level II-2 |
| **Red meat (100 g/day increase)** | OR=1.17 (95% CI 1.05–1.31) — Caucasian populations | Level II-2 |
| **Processed meat (25 g/day increase)** | OR=1.49 (95% CI 1.22–1.81) — Caucasian populations | Level II-2 |

> **Clinical Note:** SIGN recommends keeping red meat consumption <500 g (18 oz) per week and avoiding processed meat. Japanese studies showed no association, so further evidence is needed for generalised advice. It is advisable to avoid processed meat and minimise red meat
consumption as it may be a risk factor for CRC based on current but
limited evidence.

## 2.4 Genetic Counselling and Testing

### CRC Aetiology Breakdown

| Type | Proportion |
|:---|:---|
| **Sporadic CRC** | 65–75% |
| **Familial CRC** | 25–30% |
| **Hereditary syndromes** | 5–8% |

> **Clinical Note:** **[Level III]** Hereditary syndromes carry 70–90% lifetime CRC risk, while familial CRC has 2–3x increased risk vs general population.

### Identification of Lynch Syndrome

| Strategy | Description |
|:---|:---|
| **Amsterdam Criteria** | **[Level I]** Clinical criteria to identify MMR gene mutations |
| **Bethesda Guidelines** | **[Level I]** Identifies microsatellite instability in tumours |
| **Prediction models** | **[Level III]** MMRPredict, MMRPro, PREMM1,2,6 — superior performance over clinical guidelines; not yet used locally |

> **Limitation:** **[Level I]** Both Amsterdam Criteria and Bethesda Guidelines have limitations in sensitivity and specificity.

> **Clinical Note:** **[Level I]** Targeting genetic services for patients with a strong family history of cancer is cost-effective. Predictive or pre-symptomatic testing and identification of cancer-predisposing mutations in carriers have shown that surveillance, prophylactic and chemoprevention techniques extend survival and are cost-effective.

### Referral Criteria for Genetic Services

Refer patients with any of the following to clinical genetics service:

- Personal history of CRC before age 50
- Personal history of CRC and endometrial/ovarian cancer at any age
- Personal history of CRC and stomach, small bowel, biliary or urinary tract cancer
- Personal history of CRC + ≥2 FDRs with colorectal/endometrial/ovarian cancer
- Family history of known inherited syndromes (Lynch, FAP, familial diffuse gastric cancer)
- ≥10 adenomatous polyps
- Multiple primary colon cancers at any age
- Cumulative >5 proximal serrated polyps (≥2 of which >10 mm)
- Cumulative >20 serrated polyps
- ≥2 juvenile or Peutz-Jeghers polyps

### Recommendation 5

- All individuals with family history suggestive of a hereditary CRC syndrome should be referred to a clinical genetics service for genetic risk assessment, where accessible.

### Levels of Evidence

* **Level I** = Evidence from at least one properly randomised controlled trial
* **Level II-2** = Evidence obtained from well-designed cohort or case-control analytic studies, preferably from more than one centre or group
* **Level III** = Opinions of respected authorities based on clinical experience; descriptive studies and case reports; or reports of expert committees
