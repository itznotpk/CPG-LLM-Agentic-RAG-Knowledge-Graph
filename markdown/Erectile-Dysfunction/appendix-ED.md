## Key Recommendations
The following recommendations are highlighted by the CPG DG as the key recommendations that answer the main questions addressed in the CPG and should be prioritised for implementation.

### Diagnosis And Assessment
* A comprehensive medical, psychosocial and sexual history should be taken in every patient presenting with erectile dysfunction (ED).
* A validated questionnaire related to ED should be used to assess all sexual function domains (e.g. International Index of Erectile Function).
* A focused physical examination in the initial assessment of men with ED should be done to identify underlying medical conditions and co-morbid genital disorders that may be associated with ED.
* Routine laboratory tests should be performed to identify modifiable risk factors of ED.
* Patients with ED should have cardiac risk assessment and vice versa.

### Treatment
* All patients with erectile dysfunction (ED) should be advised on lifestyle and risk factor modifications.
* Phosphodiesterase-5-inhibitor should be offered to all patients with ED unless contraindicated.
* Mechanical devices (e.g. using vacuum erection device or shockwave therapy) may be offered in ED. Low-intensity extracorporeal shockwave therapy should be performed by urologists for mild to moderate ED.
* Penile prothesis may be offered to patients with ED who have failed other interventions.
* An integrated and collaborative approach with psychological interventions should be considered in the treatment of ED.

## Appendix 3: Relevant History Taking In Patients With Symptoms Of ED
| History Components | Questions |
|--------------------|-----------|
| **History of presenting complaint** | Patients with suspected ED will primarily complain of difficulties initiating or sustaining an erection. Further details surrounding these issues should be explored:<br>• Onset of sexual dysfunction (i.e. acute, gradual)<br>• Duration of sexual dysfunction<br>• Lack of libido<br>• Rigidity of erection<br>• Duration of sexual stimulation<br>• Difficulties with ejaculation/orgasm<br>• Absence of morning erection |
| **Past medical/ surgical history** | Previous sexual dysfunction, CVD, metabolic syndrome (i.e. hypertension, DM, obesity and dyslipidaemia) and pelvic surgery |
| **Medication history** | Antihypertensives, antidepressants, antipsychotics, anticonvulsants, nitrates and PDE5i |
| **Psychiatric history** | Current or previous psychological problems (e.g. depression, anxiety), stress, coping abilities, cognitive factors and previous trauma |
| **Social history** | Smoking, alcohol consumption, illicit drug use, diet, exercise, cultural and religious aspects |
| **Sexual history** | Current sexual partner(s), relationship status, partner's perception to ED, gender dysphoria or sexual orientation, sexual exposure and experience (e.g. masturbation, pornography consumption), plan for children |

## Appendix 5: Erection Hardness Score (EHS)
| Score | Erection Hardness Level |
|---|---|
| **0** | Penis does not enlarge. |
| **1** | Penis is larger but not hard. |
| **2** | Penis is hard but not hard enough for penetration. |
| **3** | Penis is hard enough for penetration but not completely hard. |
| **4** | Penis is completely hard and fully rigid. |

**Assessment Question:** *"How would you rate the hardness of your erection?"*

Visual Analogues (Clinical Comparison Guide)
To help patients and clinicians grade erection hardness intuitively, the following food analogues are used as a visual scale:
*   **Score 1 (Tofu):** The penis is larger but not hard (similar to the soft, compressible texture of a block of tofu).
*   **Score 2 (Peeled Banana):** The penis is hard but not hard enough for penetration (similar to a soft, peeled banana that bends easily).
*   **Score 3 (Unpeeled Banana):** The penis is hard enough for penetration but not completely hard (similar to an unpeeled banana which has structural firmness but retains slight yielding/flexibility).
*   **Score 4 (Cucumber):** The penis is completely hard and fully rigid (similar to a firm, fully rigid cucumber).

## Appendix 7: Application Of Vacuum Erectile Device (VED)
### Step-by-Step Clinical Instruction Guide
To assist patients and healthcare providers in the safe and effective use of the Vacuum Erectile Device (VED), the following three-step illustrated procedure is described in detail:

#### Step 1: Equipment Components and Assembly
*   **Visual Representation:** An illustration displays the complete VED system, which consists of three main components: a clear acrylic cylinder (vacuum chamber), a pump mechanism, and elastic constriction rings (tension bands).
*   **Description & Mechanics:**
    *   The equipment consists of a vacuum chamber, a vacuum pump, and a constricting ring or band.
    *   The pump can be operated manually (as a hand-squeeze pump connected by flexible tubing), but is usually battery-operated for easier, one-handed use.
    *   An elastic tension band is selected and prepared to constrict the base of the penis, which maintains the erection after it is achieved.

#### Step 2: Preparation and Vacuum Application
*   **Visual Representation:** An illustration shows a patient standing, holding the assembled vacuum chamber over the penis, pressing the open end firmly against the pubic area while operating the pump to create negative pressure.
*   **Description & Procedure:**
    *   Before the device is used, the elastic constricting band is lubricated with a water-soluble jelly and placed around the outer rim of the vacuum chamber near its open end.
    *   A generous amount of water-soluble lubricant/jelly is next applied directly to the base of the penis and surrounding pubic area to assure a completely airtight seal when the chamber is pressed against the body.
    *   The penis is carefully placed inside the vacuum chamber, and the pump is operated to create a vacuum. The negative pressure draws blood into the corpora cavernosa. The vacuum is applied for 3 to 6 minutes, or until the penis becomes fully rigid.

#### Step 3: Band Placement and Chamber Removal
*   **Visual Representation:** An illustration shows a close-up of the patient's hands sliding the elastic constricting band from the base of the cylinder onto the root of the now-erect penis, followed by releasing the vacuum pressure.
*   **Description & Safety Guidelines:**
    *   Once full rigidity is achieved, the elastic constricting band is slid off the end of the vacuum chamber directly onto the base/root of the penis to trap the blood and maintain the erection.
    *   The vacuum release valve on the pump is then pressed to release the pressure, and the chamber is safely removed from the penis. Sexual intercourse can be performed immediately after.
    *   **Safety Warning:** The constricting band must not be left on the penis for longer than 30 minutes to prevent tissue ischemia and permanent injury.

## Appendix 8: Low-Intensity Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy
### Anatomical and Treatment Overview
*   **Visual Representation:** Illustrations show the anatomy of the penis including the glans penis, corpus cavernosum, corpus spongiosum, bulbospongiosus muscle, and ischiocavernosus muscle. The highlighted treatment area targets the crura and penile shaft.
*   **How it works?**
    *   While the patient is lying down, ultrasound gel is applied to the applicator head and/or the treatment area.
    *   The applicator head is applied to areas of the penile shaft and crus.
    *   The shockwave technology skilfully creates waves that treat a broad area from the superficial surface of the penile shaft to the deeper erectile tissues of the crura.
    *   It is currently recommended that this treatment to be conducted once or twice a week, for at least 6 sessions.
    *   The waves stimulate tissue and do not cause any pain or external scarring. Patients can resume daily regular activity after the procedure. Side effects (if any) are usually mild and temporary.
    *   Most patients report progress after 3 - 4 sessions.

## Appendix 9: Examples Of Penile Prosthesis
### Prosthetic Device Types
The following are examples of penile prosthetic devices used for surgical intervention in ED:
*   **Three-piece inflatable penile prosthesis:** A system comprising two inflatable cylinders, a pump, and a separate fluid reservoir.
*   **Two-piece inflatable device:** A system comprising two inflatable cylinders with a combined pump and reservoir unit.
*   **Malleable prosthesis:** A system of bendable, semi-rigid rods without inflatable components.
