> **Context:** Key Recommendations + reference appendices for the 2024 Malaysian Management of ED CPG. Includes the diagnosis/treatment algorithm, cardiovascular-disease classification (Princeton consensus), history-taking template, IIEF-5 and EHS questionnaires, PDE5i dosing/contraindications, and procedural references (VED, low-intensity shockwave, penile prosthesis). The Key Recommendations are a consolidated digest — full clinical rationale is in Sections 2–8.

## Key Recommendations
The following recommendations are highlighted by the CPG DG as the key recommendations that answer the main questions addressed in the CPG and should be prioritised for implementation.

Diagnosis And Assessment:
* A comprehensive medical, psychosocial and sexual history should be taken in every patient presenting with erectile dysfunction (ED).
* A validated questionnaire related to ED should be used to assess all sexual function domains (e.g. International Index of Erectile Function).
* A focused physical examination in the initial assessment of men with ED should be done to identify underlying medical conditions and co-morbid genital disorders that may be associated with ED.
* Routine laboratory tests should be performed to identify modifiable risk factors of ED.
* Patients with ED should have cardiac risk assessment and vice versa.

Treatment:
* All patients with erectile dysfunction (ED) should be advised on lifestyle and risk factor modifications.
* Phosphodiesterase-5-inhibitor should be offered to all patients with ED unless contraindicated.
* Mechanical devices (e.g. using vacuum erection device or shockwave therapy) may be offered in ED. Low-intensity extracorporeal shockwave therapy should be performed by urologists for mild to moderate ED.
* Penile prothesis may be offered to patients with ED who have failed other interventions.
* An integrated and collaborative approach with psychological interventions should be considered in the treatment of ED.

## Algorithm 1: Diagnosis And Treatment Of ED
Overview: This algorithm outlines the complete clinical decision pathway for diagnosing and treating Erectile Dysfunction (ED), from initial patient presentation through to specific treatment selection. The pathway branches based on the type of ED identified (organic or psychogenic) and, for organic ED, further branches based on severity as determined by the IIEF-5 score.

**Clinical Definitions And Scoring Reference**
IIEF-5 (International Index of Erectile Function, 5-item version) is the validated scoring tool used to assess erectile function and classify severity. The total score is the sum of the ordinal responses to all 5 questions (range: 5 to 25).
| IIEF-5 Score | Clinical Classification |
|---|---|
| 22 to 25 | Normal (no erectile dysfunction) |
| 17 to 21 | Mild erectile dysfunction |
| 12 to 16 | Mild-to-moderate erectile dysfunction |
| 8 to 11 | Moderate erectile dysfunction |
| 5 to 7 | Severe erectile dysfunction |
Co-morbidities should always be considered when interpreting the IIEF-5 score and selecting a treatment pathway.

Step 1: Entry Point — Patient Presents With Symptoms Of ED
The algorithm begins when a patient presents with symptoms of erectile dysfunction. The clinician proceeds immediately to a comprehensive structured assessment before any diagnosis or treatment is made.

Step 2: Comprehensive Initial Assessment
All patients presenting with symptoms of ED must undergo the following assessment as a combined package:
- A comprehensive history including medical history (e.g. cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, neurological conditions), psychological history (e.g. depression, anxiety, relationship difficulties), and sexual history. Partner involvement in history-taking is preferred.
- Administration of the IIEF-5 questionnaire to quantify severity and guide treatment decisions.
- A focused physical examination to identify underlying medical conditions and co-morbid genital disorders.
- Laboratory investigations, ordered at clinical discretion (indicated by ± in source algorithm).
- Imaging studies, ordered at clinical discretion (indicated by ± in source algorithm).

The outcome of this assessment leads to a formal diagnosis of ED.

Step 3: Diagnosis Of ED And Classification By Type
Following assessment, the clinician classifies the patient's ED into one of the following types. This classification determines which treatment pathway is followed.

Organic ED: ED with a physical or medical cause, including vascular (e.g. arterial insufficiency, veno-occlusive disease), hormonal (e.g. hypogonadism), or neurogenic (e.g. spinal cord injury, post-prostatectomy) aetiology. Patients with organic ED proceed to severity assessment (Step 4).

Psychogenic ED: ED arising primarily from psychological causes including performance anxiety, depression, relationship conflict, or other psychosocial stressors. Patients with psychogenic ED proceed directly to the Psychogenic ED Treatment Pathway (Step 5C).

Mixed ED: Some patients present with a combination of organic and psychogenic factors (mixed ED). For these patients, the clinician should categorise the severity of mixed ED according to IIEF-5 and manage the case following either the organic or psychogenic pathway depending on which aetiology predominates.

Step 4: Severity Assessment For Organic ED — Using IIEF-5
For patients diagnosed with Organic ED, the IIEF-5 score is used to stratify severity into two groups, each leading to a distinct treatment pathway.

If the IIEF-5 score is between 8 and 21 (Mild or Moderate ED), the patient follows Pathway A.
If the IIEF-5 score is between 5 and 7 (Severe ED), the patient follows Pathway B.

Step 5: Treatment Pathways
Pathway A — Mild Or Moderate Organic ED (IIEF-5 Score 8 to 21)
Patients with mild or moderate organic ED are managed initially by the primary team with the following interventions:
- Lifestyle modification (e.g. weight loss, smoking cessation, exercise, glycaemic and blood pressure control).
- Pharmacotherapy, specifically phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (PDE5i), offered concurrently with lifestyle modifications (indicated by ± in source algorithm, meaning it is offered but not unconditionally mandatory at this stage).

If there is no improvement following the above initial management, the patient is referred to a urologist for consideration of mechanical devices (e.g. vacuum erection device, low-intensity extracorporeal shockwave therapy) or surgical intervention (e.g. penile prosthesis implantation). This referral is a definitive next step, not optional, once initial management has failed.

Pathway B — Severe Organic ED (IIEF-5 Score 5 to 7)
Patients with severe organic ED are referred directly to a urologist. The urologist manages the patient with the following combined interventions:
- Lifestyle modification.
- Pharmacotherapy (PDE5i), unless contraindicated.
- Mechanical devices (e.g. vacuum erection device or shockwave therapy), offered as an adjunct or alternative to pharmacotherapy.
- Surgical intervention (e.g. penile prosthesis), considered when other modalities are insufficient or inappropriate.

Pathway C — Psychogenic ED
Patients with psychogenic ED are managed with:
- Lifestyle modification.
- Pharmacotherapy (PDE5i), offered at clinical discretion (indicated by ± in source algorithm).
- Referral to mental health professionals for formal psychological intervention (e.g. cognitive-behavioural sex therapy, couples therapy). This referral is a core component of management and is not optional in psychogenic ED.

Patients with psychogenic ED who do not improve may also be considered for referral to a urologist for mechanical devices or surgical intervention, following the same pathway as those with organic ED who fail initial management. This is indicated by a dashed arrow in the source algorithm, meaning it is a secondary consideration rather than a primary step.

Summary Of Relationships — Algorithm 1
- Symptom presentation leads to comprehensive assessment.
- Assessment leads to diagnosis and type classification (organic, psychogenic, or mixed).
- Organic ED type leads to IIEF-5 severity classification.
- Mild/moderate severity (score 8 to 21) leads to lifestyle modification and pharmacotherapy first, then urologist referral if no improvement.
- Severe severity (score 5 to 7) leads directly to urologist referral with combined treatment.
- Psychogenic ED leads to lifestyle modification, pharmacotherapy at discretion, and mandatory referral to mental health professionals.
- Mixed ED follows either organic or psychogenic pathway based on predominant aetiology, using IIEF-5 to stratify severity.

## Algorithm 2: Classification For ED Patients With Cardiovascular Disease
Overview: This algorithm classifies patients with confirmed ED and co-existing cardiovascular disease (CVD) into cardiac risk categories, determines the safety of initiating ED treatment (specifically PDE5i), and resolves the presence of nitrate therapy as a critical contraindication decision point. The algorithm is based on the 2nd and 3rd Princeton Consensus on Sexual Dysfunction and Cardiac Risk.

If the patient has vasculogenic ED, an ASCVD (Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease) risk score assessment should be performed before entering the main algorithm.

Step 1: Entry Point — Patients With Confirmed ED And Cardiovascular Disease
The algorithm applies to patients who have already received a confirmed diagnosis of ED and who are known to have cardiovascular disease or are at cardiovascular risk. The purpose of this algorithm is not to diagnose ED, but to determine the safety of treating it.

Step 2: Assessment Of Exercise Ability
The first clinical action is to assess the patient's exercise ability. Exercise ability is used to guide the physician in estimating the cardiovascular risk associated with sexual activity, and this assessment must be established before the initiation of any ED treatment.

Exercise ability is benchmarked against the metabolic equivalent of sexual activity, which is equivalent to:
- Walking 1.6 kilometres (1 mile) on a flat surface in 20 minutes, OR
- Briskly climbing two flights of stairs in 10 seconds.

The outcome of the exercise ability assessment, combined with the patient's cardiac history, informs the cardiac risk stratification in the next step.

Step 3: Cardiac Risk Stratification According To Princeton Consensus
Using the patient's cardiovascular history and exercise ability, the clinician stratifies the patient into one of three cardiac risk categories according to the 2nd and 3rd Princeton Consensus. Refer to Table 1 (Princeton Consensus Risk Classification Table) for the specific criteria defining each risk tier.

The three risk categories are: Low Risk, Intermediate Risk, and High Risk. Each category leads to a distinct management pathway.

Step 4: Risk-Based Management Pathways

Low Risk Pathway:
Patients classified as Low Risk by the Princeton Consensus stratification may be offered elective (i.e. optional) additional risk assessment at the physician's discretion. This is not a mandatory step.
Low-risk patients are managed for their ED by the primary care team. They proceed directly to the Nitrate Assessment (Step 5) to determine which ED treatment is safe to prescribe.

Intermediate Risk Pathway:
Patients classified as Intermediate Risk must undergo a formal stress test before any ED treatment is initiated.
The stress test used is the Bruce treadmill protocol. Sexual activity is considered equivalent to 4 minutes of this protocol. A Pass result is defined as completion of the test without development of symptoms, arrhythmias, or a fall in systolic blood pressure.
If the stress test result is a Pass, the patient is reclassified as Low Risk. They are then managed by the primary care team and proceed to the Nitrate Assessment (Step 5).
If the stress test result is a Fail, the patient is reclassified as High Risk. They are then referred for further cardiac assessment and ED treatment is deferred until the cardiac condition is stabilised.

High Risk Pathway:
Patients classified as High Risk — either directly from the Princeton Consensus stratification or following a failed stress test — are referred for further cardiac assessment. ED treatment is deferred and must not be initiated until the underlying cardiac condition has been stabilised and the patient has been re-evaluated.

Step 5: Nitrate And Riociguat Assessment — Determining ED Treatment Selection
This step applies only to patients who have been cleared for ED treatment (i.e. Low Risk patients, or Intermediate Risk patients who passed the stress test). The critical question at this stage is whether the patient is currently prescribed nitrates or riociguat, as these agents are absolutely contraindicated with PDE5i due to the risk of severe hypotension.

Decision Branch A — Patient Is Not Prescribed Nitrates Or Riociguat
If the patient is NOT currently prescribed nitrates or riociguat, PDE5i (phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors) therapy is the recommended first-line ED treatment. The clinician selects the appropriate PDE5i agent and dose based on the patient's profile (refer to the pharmacological treatment table in Appendix 6B).

Decision Branch B — Patient Is Currently Prescribed Nitrates Or Riociguat
If the patient IS currently prescribed nitrates or riociguat, a second-level clinical decision is required: Is the nitrate (or riociguat) therapy essential for the patient's ongoing cardiovascular management?

If the nitrate is essential and cannot be safely discontinued, the patient must receive Non-PDE5i treatment for their ED. PDE5i agents are absolutely contraindicated in this scenario.

If the nitrate is not essential and can be safely discontinued, the clinician should consider stopping the nitrate therapy, and then — after an appropriate washout period — initiate PDE5i therapy for ED.

Summary Of Relationships — Algorithm 2
- Confirmed ED with CVD leads to exercise ability assessment, which informs cardiac risk stratification.
- Vasculogenic ED additionally requires ASCVD risk score assessment before risk stratification.
- Princeton Consensus stratification classifies the patient as Low Risk, Intermediate Risk, or High Risk.
- Low Risk leads directly to nitrate assessment and ED treatment selection by the primary team.
- Intermediate Risk leads to a mandatory stress test. A passing result reclassifies the patient as Low Risk (proceed to treatment). A failing result reclassifies the patient as High Risk (defer treatment).
- High Risk leads to further cardiac assessment. ED treatment is deferred until cardiac condition is stabilised.
- For patients cleared for treatment, if NOT on nitrates/riociguat, the treatment is PDE5i.
- For patients cleared for treatment, if ON nitrates/riociguat, the clinician assesses if nitrate is essential. If essential, Non-PDE5i treatment is used. If not essential, nitrate cessation followed by PDE5i is considered.

## Appendix 5: Erection Hardness Score (EHS)
| Score | Erection Hardness Level |
|---|---|
| 0 | Penis does not enlarge. |
| 1 | Penis is larger but not hard. |
| 2 | Penis is hard but not hard enough for penetration. |
| 3 | Penis is hard enough for penetration but not completely hard. |
| 4 | Penis is completely hard and fully rigid. |

Visual Analogues (Clinical Comparison Guide)
To help patients and clinicians grade erection hardness intuitively, the following food analogues are used as a visual scale:
*   Score 1 (Tofu): The penis is larger but not hard (similar to the soft, compressible texture of a block of tofu).
*   Score 2 (Peeled Banana): The penis is hard but not hard enough for penetration (similar to a soft, peeled banana that bends easily).
*   Score 3 (Unpeeled Banana): The penis is hard enough for penetration but not completely hard (similar to an unpeeled banana which has structural firmness but retains slight yielding/flexibility).
*   Score 4 (Cucumber): The penis is completely hard and fully rigid (similar to a firm, fully rigid cucumber).

## Appendix 7: Application Of Vacuum Erectile Device (VED)
Step-by-Step Clinical Instruction Guide
To assist patients and healthcare providers in the safe and effective use of the Vacuum Erectile Device (VED), the following three-step illustrated procedure is described in detail:

Step 1: Equipment Components and Assembly
*   Visual Representation: An illustration displays the complete VED system, which consists of three main components: a clear acrylic cylinder (vacuum chamber), a pump mechanism, and elastic constriction rings (tension bands).
*   Description & Mechanics:
    *   The equipment consists of a vacuum chamber, a vacuum pump, and a constricting ring or band.
    *   The pump can be operated manually (as a hand-squeeze pump connected by flexible tubing), but is usually battery-operated for easier, one-handed use.
    *   An elastic tension band is selected and prepared to constrict the base of the penis, which maintains the erection after it is achieved.

Step 2: Preparation and Vacuum Application
*   Visual Representation: An illustration shows a patient standing, holding the assembled vacuum chamber over the penis, pressing the open end firmly against the pubic area while operating the pump to create negative pressure.
*   Description & Procedure:
    *   Before the device is used, the elastic constricting band is lubricated with a water-soluble jelly and placed around the outer rim of the vacuum chamber near its open end.
    *   A generous amount of water-soluble lubricant/jelly is next applied directly to the base of the penis and surrounding pubic area to assure a completely airtight seal when the chamber is pressed against the body.
